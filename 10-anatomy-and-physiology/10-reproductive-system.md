# The Reproductive System

## Why it matters

The **reproductive system is the only system dispensable for the individual yet indispensable for the species** — gonads and ducts can be removed without threatening life, but no other failure removes a lineage. Every structure therefore exists to **produce, deliver or support gametes**, and nothing else.

| Property | Systems 01–09 | Reproductive system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential to the individual** | Yes — failure kills | No — failure causes **infertility** only |
| **Control motif** | Negative feedback throughout | Negative feedback **with rare positive steps** (LH surge, milk let-down) |
| **Switched on** | At birth | **At puberty**, then cyclically |
| **Shared plumbing** | — | Male urethra common to urinary and reproductive tracts ([08 — Urinary system](08-urinary-system.md)) |

Control is the **hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis** of [04 — The endocrine system](04-endocrine-system.md), and this is its clearest worked example because here a feedback loop **switches sign mid-cycle**. Loops are read with the format of [03 — Homeostasis: the full control loop](../09-human-biology/03-homeostasis-control-loop.md). Gametogenesis, fertilisation and development belong to section 11 — [01 — Spermatogenesis and oogenesis](../11-reproduction-and-development/01-spermatogenesis-and-oogenesis.md), [02 — Fertilisation and the zygote](../11-reproduction-and-development/02-fertilization-and-the-zygote.md) — so they are linked, never repeated. The chapter runs **structure → function → mechanism → regulation → homeostasis**.

## Male reproductive tract: structure → function

### Testes: two organs in one capsule

A dense **tunica albuginea** divides each testis into lobules holding coiled **seminiferous tubules** (~250 m per testis). Two separate organs share the capsule:

| Compartment | Cells | Signal | Product | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Inside the tubule** | Sertoli + germ cells | **FSH** → Sertoli | Spermatozoa, **inhibin B** | **Spermatogenesis** ([11 — Spermatogenesis and oogenesis](../11-reproduction-and-development/01-spermatogenesis-and-oogenesis.md)) |
| **Interstitium** | **Leydig cells** | **LH** | **Testosterone** (cholesterol-derived, [02 — Lipids](../01-biochemistry/02-lipids.md)) | Virilisation, libido, support of meiosis |

Leydig cells sit *outside* the tubules, so testosterone diffuses inward and intratesticular levels run **~50–100× serum** — which is why exogenous testosterone, raising serum level and switching the axis off, starves the tubule. Sertoli tight junctions form the **blood–testis barrier**, hiding the novel antigens meiotic cells display after crossing over ([12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md)) from lymphocytes.

### The scrotum: cooling as anatomy doing physiology

Spermatogenesis **fails above ~35 °C**, so the testes are held **2–3 °C below core** by an externalised, actively regulated pouch.

```
COLD → DARTOS and CREMASTIC reflexes CONTRACT → skin wrinkles,
       surface area ↓, testes drawn toward the warm body
WARM → both RELAX → thin, hairless, fat-free skin radiates heat;
       testes hang free
PAMPINIFORM PLEXUS: testicular vein wraps the testicular artery
       → COUNTERCURRENT EXCHANGE → arterial inflow at ~33–34 °C
       → SPERMATOGENESIS RUNS AT 32–34 °C
```

An **undescended testis** sits at 37 °C: meiosis fails and germ cells die by apoptosis. A **varicocele** — dilation of the pampiniform plexus, usually left, where the testicular vein joins the renal vein at a right angle — impairs heat dissipation and adds oxidative stress. Both are sought in infertility workups.

### Epididymis and vas deferens: maturation, storage, transport

| Structure | Structure → function |
| --- | --- |
| **Epididymis** — single ~6 m tube, head/body/tail | Sperm arrive **non-motile and infertile**; over **~12–14 days** the epithelium reabsorbs fluid, membranes remodel and sperm **acquire progressive motility**; the **tail stores** them until ejaculation |
| **Vas deferens** — thick circular and longitudinal muscle, **sympathetic hypogastric nerve** | **Rapid peristaltic transport** from tail to ampulla at emission; passes through the inguinal canal; the testicular artery is untouched, so occluding the duct blocks **transport only** |
| **Ejaculatory duct** | Where gland secretion meets sperm, entering the prostatic urethra |

### Accessory glands

They contribute **~95–98% of semen volume**; sperm are a sliver of the ejaculate.

| Gland | % of semen | Secretion | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Seminal vesicles** | **~60–70%** | **Fructose**, sorbitol, **prostaglandins**, semenogelin (clotting protein); **alkaline pH 7.2–7.8** | Fructose = sperm fuel; prostaglandins stimulate female-tract motility; alkaline fluid **buffers vaginal acidity**; clotting retains semen at the cervix |
| **Prostate** | ~25–30% | **PSA** (serine protease), fibrinolysin, citrate, zinc; alkaline | **Liquefaction** — PSA digests semenogelin minutes later, freeing sperm |
| **Bulbourethral (Cowper's)** | <1% | Clear alkaline mucus | **Pre-ejaculate**: lubrication and **neutralisation of acidic urine left in the urethra** |

The sequence is deliberate: **coagulate immediately, liquefy in 10–20 minutes**.

### Penis: three cylinders, two jobs

| Structure | Arrangement | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Corpora cavernosa** (paired) | Dorsal, wrapped in inelastic **tunica albuginea** | Fill and expand, compressing contained veins → **rigidity** |
| **Corpus spongiosum** (single) | Ventral, **surrounds the penile urethra**, ends as the glans | Stays softer, so the **urethra remains patent for ejaculation** |
| **Helicine arteries / venules** | Coiled arteries; venules pierce the tunica | Wide inflow when relaxed; **veno-occlusion** when expanded |

### Semen: composition and function

| Component | Typical value | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volume, pH | **1.5–5 mL**; **7.2–8.0** | Vehicle; alkalinity neutralises vaginal pH 3.8–4.5 |
| Spermatozoa | **~200–300 million**; ≥15 million/mL | Deliver one haploid nucleus |
| Fructose | ~2–5 g/L (seminal vesicles) | Sperm glycolysis |
| Prostaglandins | High | Stimulate female-tract peristalsis and sperm transport |
| PSA, fibrinolysin | Active after clotting | **Liquefaction** |
| Seminalplasmin, spermine | Antimicrobial | Early defence of the tract |

## Male mechanism: erection, ejaculation, libido

### Erection: parasympathetic → nitric oxide → cGMP

Erection is **active smooth-muscle relaxation** — the cleanest second-messenger chain in the body, and the target of **PDE5 inhibitors**.

```
SEXUAL STIMULUS → parasympathetic PELVIC SPLANCHNIC fibres (S2–S4)
        ↓ NITRIC OXIDE from nerve endings (nNOS) and endothelium (eNOS)
NO → SOLUBLE GUANYLATE CYCLASE → GTP ──► cGMP
        ↓ PKG: Ca²⁺ sequestered, myosin light chain dephosphorylated
HELICINE ARTERIES DILATE; SINUSOIDS RELAX AND FILL
        ↓ expanding sinusoids COMPRESS VENULES AGAINST TUNICA ALBUGINEA
VENOUS OUTFLOW OBSTRUCTED → ENGORGEMENT → ERECTION
        ↓
PDE5 hydrolyses cGMP → 5'-GMP → detumescence
PDE5 INHIBITOR (sildenafil, tadalafil) blocks this → cGMP persists
```

Sympathetic **noradrenaline at α₁ receptors contracts** the muscle, which is why anxiety detumesces an erection. Because the chain starts with **NO release**, a PDE5 inhibitor amplifies an existing signal — stimulation is still required.

### Ejaculation: two reflexes, two different nerves

| Phase | Innervation | Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Emission** | **Sympathetic** T11–L2 (hypogastric) | Vas, ampulla, vesicles and prostate contract; **internal sphincter closes** so semen cannot enter the bladder |
| **Expulsion** | **Somatic** pudendal (S2–S4) | **Bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus** contract rhythmically, external sphincter relaxes → semen expelled in pulses |

Erection (parasympathetic) and ejaculation (sympathetic + somatic) are mutually antagonistic ([03 — Nervous system](03-nervous-system.md)); failure of the internal sphincter gives **retrograde ejaculation**.

### Libido, testosterone and feedback

**Testosterone** drives libido centrally and maintains **nitric oxide synthase expression** in penile tissue, so chronic hypogonadism causes erectile difficulty. It is also converted to **dihydrotestosterone** by 5α-reductase (prostate, skin) and to **oestradiol** by aromatase (brain, bone).

```
HYPOTHALAMUS ──pulsatile GnRH──► PITUITARY ──► LH + FSH
                                      │            │
                                 LEYDIG CELLS  SERTOLI CELLS
                                      │            │
                                TESTOSTERONE   INHIBIN B
   testosterone ⊣ GnRH and LH (long loop); inhibin B ⊣ FSH ALONE,
   so sperm output is tuned independently of virilisation
```

**Exogenous testosterone suppresses GnRH, LH and FSH**, collapsing intratesticular testosterone → azoospermia while the man stays virilised.

## Female reproductive tract: structure → function

### Ovaries: follicle as factory and gland

The cortex holds **follicles at every stage**; the ovary does **oogenesis** ([11 — Spermatogenesis and oogenesis](../11-reproduction-and-development/01-spermatogenesis-and-oogenesis.md)) and **steroidogenesis** at once — but needs two cell types and two gonadotrophins:

```
LH ──► THECA CELLS → cholesterol → ANDROGENS ──► diffuse to granulosa
FSH ──► GRANULOSA CELLS → AROMATASE → androgens ──► OESTRADIOL
   (theca cannot aromatise, granulosa cannot make androgen from
    cholesterol: the two-cell, two-gonadotrophin system)
```

| Follicular structure | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Growing follicles | Oocyte enlarges, granulosa proliferate, theca form; FSH recruits a **cohort** each cycle |
| **Dominant follicle** | Most FSH receptors, highest aromatase → oestrogen rises; the rest undergo **atresia** |
| **Corpus luteum** | Post-ovulatory gland: **progesterone + oestradiol** under LH |
| **Corpus albicans** | Scar of an unrescued luteum — secretion stops |

### Fallopian tubes: capture, transport, meeting place

| Region | Structure → function |
| --- | --- |
| **Infundibulum + fimbriae** | Ciliated fingers sweep the ovarian surface and **draw the ovulated oocyte in**; oestrogen raises ciliary beat at ovulation |
| **Ampulla** | Widest, thinnest — usual **site of fertilisation** (~70%) |
| **Isthmus** | Thick, narrow — **sperm reservoir** and controlled gate |
| **Wall** | Smooth muscle **peristalsis** plus ciliated secretory epithelium move the ovum ~3 days toward the uterus |

Fimbrial damage or tubal blockage explains tubal infertility and **ampullary ectopic pregnancy**.

### Uterus: three layers, three jobs

An organ of all four tissue types ([01 — Four tissue types](../09-human-biology/01-four-tissue-types.md)).

| Layer | Tissue | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Perimetrium** | Mesothelium | Smooth outer covering |
| **Myometrium** | **Smooth muscle**, ~80% of wall | Expels menstrual fluid; at term **gap junctions (connexin-43) and oxytocin receptors surge**, turning independent cells into a coordinated pump |
| **Endometrium** | Glands, stroma, vessels | Implantation bed: **stratum basalis** (regenerates, never shed) and **stratum functionalis** (built and shed monthly) |

The functionalis is fed by coiled **spiral arteries**, whose vulnerability to spasm explains menstruation. Implantation occurs there **~6–7 days after fertilisation**.

### Cervix: a hormonally gated valve

| Hormonal state | Mucus | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High oestrogen** (periovulatory) | Thin, watery, alkaline, stretchable (*spinnbarkeit*) | **Permissive** — sperm penetrate; abnormal sperm filtered out |
| **Progesterone** (luteal, pregnancy) | Thick, scant, cellular | **Impermeable** — forms the **mucus plug**, blocking sperm and pathogens |
| **Labour** | Collagen remodelling, prostaglandins | Softens and dilates |

A valve open one day a month, shut for 40 weeks, then open for hours cannot be muscular — it is a **secretion-switched mucus gate**.

### Vagina: a chemically defended passage

Stratified squamous epithelium with rugae, serving as copulatory organ, sperm reservoir and birth canal; defence is chemical:

```
OESTROGEN → vaginal epithelium loads with GLYCOGEN
        ↓
LACTOBACILLUS ferment glycogen → LACTIC ACID → pH 3.8–4.5
   (see [07 — Beneficial microorganisms](../07-microbiology/07-beneficial-microorganisms.md))
   inhibits Candida, Trichomonas, Neisseria
        ↓
ALKALINE SEMEN buffers locally for hours → sperm survive, then
acidity returns; lose the lactobacilli and pH rises
```

### Mammary glands: modified sweat glands

**15–20 lobes** of **alveoli** (the secretory units) drain into **ducts** and **lactiferous ducts**; **myoepithelial cells** ring each alveolus. Fibroadipose stroma gives shape — **breast size is fat, not gland**. At puberty **oestrogen drives duct growth and fat deposition**, progesterone adds lobulo-alveolar tissue, and pregnancy completes development ready for prolactin.

## Regulation: the HPG axis and the GnRH clock

Two features of the controller govern everything below, both from [04 — The endocrine system](04-endocrine-system.md): **pulsatility encodes the message** (continuous GnRH down-regulates gonadotrophs — the basis of drug therapy), and **feedback is normally negative with two famous positive exceptions**.

| GnRH pulse pattern | Favouring | Context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Faster** | **LH** | Late follicular phase, mid-cycle surge |
| **Slower** | **FSH** | Early follicular and luteal phases — progesterone slows pulses |
| **Continuous** | **Neither** — receptor down-regulation | GnRH-agonist therapy |

| Feedback | Signal | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Long-loop negative** | Testosterone, oestradiol, progesterone | ⊣ GnRH, ⊣ LH/FSH |
| **Selective negative** | **Inhibin B** (A luteally) | ⊣ **FSH alone** |
| **Positive (rare)** | **Sustained high oestradiol** | ↑ GnRH + sensitised pituitary → **LH surge** |
| **Positive (rare)** | **Suckling, cervical stretch** | ↑ **oxytocin** → ejection → more stimulus |

## The ovarian cycle: the centrepiece

A ~28-day cycle (range 21–35) runs the ovarian and uterine cycles together; days are counted from day 1 of menstruation.

### Phase 1 — early follicular phase: recruitment under negative feedback

```
DAY 1 — progesterone and oestradiol have fallen (luteolysis)
        ↓ negative feedback LIFTED from hypothalamus and pituitary
GnRH PULSES RESUME AT FULL DRIVE → FSH RISES
        ↓
FSH RECRUITS ~6–12 ANTRAL FOLLICLES → they grow
        ↓ modest oestrogen → NEGATIVE FEEDBACK (axis restrained)
FSH drifts down as follicles consume it
```

### Phase 2 — dominance and the feedback switch

```
ONE FOLLICLE HAS MORE FSH RECEPTORS AND MORE AROMATASE
   → keeps growing while FSH falls; secretes INHIBIN B ⊣ FSH
THE REST LOSE THE RACE → ATRESIA  ("one follicle per cycle" safeguard)
        ↓
DOMINANT FOLLICLE → SUSTAINED HIGH OESTRADIOL
   (>~200 pg/mL for ~50 continuous hours)
        ↓
*** FEEDBACK SIGN FLIPS: NEGATIVE → POSITIVE ON THE PITUITARY ***
   hypothalamus speeds GnRH pulses; pituitary is sensitised
        ↓
ONE PEAK IN AN OTHERWISE NEGATIVE SYSTEM PROVOKES ITS OWN TRIGGER
```

### Phase 3 — the LH surge and ovulation (~day 14)

```
POSITIVE FEEDBACK → LH SURGE (smaller FSH surge with it)
        ↓ peak LH; ~34–36 h later
   • primary oocyte COMPLETES MEIOSIS I (secondary oocyte + polar body)
   • prostaglandins and proteolytic enzymes weaken the follicular wall
        ↓
OVULATION — secondary oocyte released with its cumulus
        ↓ fimbriae sweep it into the AMPULLA
```

**Ovulation is triggered by LH**, and LH rises because **sustained high oestrogen switched feedback sign** — the most examinable mechanism here.

### Phase 4 — the luteal phase (days 15–28): a temporary gland

```
RUPTURED FOLLICLE → granulosa + theca LUTEINISED UNDER LH → CORPUS LUTEUM
   (lifespan designed for ~10–14 days)
        ↓
PROGESTERONE (dominant) + oestradiol + inhibin A
        ↓ NEGATIVE FEEDBACK → FSH and LH FALL
   slower GnRH pulses; no new follicle recruited; no second ovulation
        ↓
progesterone transforms the endometrium and raises basal body
temperature ~0.3–0.5 °C (used for fertility charting)
```

### Two endings: menstruation, or pregnancy recognition

**Ending A — no implantation:**

```
NO hCG → corpus luteum has a FIXED LIFESPAN → LUTEOLYSIS (corpus albicans)
        ↓ PROGESTERONE AND OESTRADIOL FALL SHARPLY
feedback lifted → FSH RISES (next cohort); on the endometrium
   spiral arteries SPASM → ISCHAEMIC NECROSIS OF THE STRATUM
   FUNCTIONALIS → MENSTRUATION (days 1–5); basalis survives
```

**Ending B — implantation:**

```
IMPLANTING BLASTOCYST → syncytiotrophoblast secretes hCG
   (β subunit is its own; α subunit is SHARED with LH)
        ↓ hCG BINDS THE LH RECEPTOR ON THE CORPUS LUTEUM
CORPUS LUTEUM RESCUED → progesterone maintained → NO MENSTRUATION
        ↓ "corpus luteum of pregnancy" runs to ~weeks 8–10, then the
   placenta takes over steroidogenesis entirely
   ([05 — Placenta and foetal exchange](../11-reproduction-and-development/05-placenta-and-maternal-fetal-exchange.md))
```

hCG is what pregnancy tests detect — and what decides between the endings.

## The uterine cycle matched to hormones

| Phase (of 28 days) | Hormonal state | Endometrium | Spiral arteries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Menstrual (1–5)** | Progesterone **and** oestrogen **fall** after luteolysis | Prostaglandins → myometrial cramps and **ischaemic shedding** of the functionalis; **basalis retained** | **Spasm → ischaemia → rupture** — shedding is ischaemic, not mechanical |
| **Proliferative (6–14)** | **Oestrogen** from the dominant follicle | Mitosis rebuilds glands, stroma, epithelium and vessels from the basalis | Grow with the lining |
| **Secretory (15–28)** | **Progesterone** (+ oestrogen) from the corpus luteum | Glands coiled and **glycogen-rich**, stroma oedematous, pinopodes → **receptive window days 20–24** | Coiled and dilated; if no implantation, these are the vessels that spasm |

Menstruation is caused by **hormone withdrawal**, not by a hormone rising: FSH rises *because* the steroids fell, starting the next cycle rather than ending the last.

## Homeostasis across the lifespan

### Puberty: switching on the controller, not the gonads

The gonads are competent from foetal life; what is switched on is the **GnRH pulse generator**, gated by **kisspeptin (KNDy) neurons**.

```
PERMISSIVE SIGNALS — adipose LEPTIN (a lipokine, [02 — Lipids](../01-biochemistry/02-lipids.md)),
   nutrition, sleep, adrenal androgens
        ↓ KISSPEPTIN NEURONS BEGIN PULSING GnRH → LH and FSH rise
   TESTIS: testosterone → virilisation, spermatogenesis begins
   OVARY:  follicle recruitment → oestrogen → growth spurt
        ↓
OESTROGEN (both sexes) drives the GROWTH SPURT, then FUSES THE
EPIPHYSEAL PLATES ([01 — Skeletal system](01-skeletal-system.md))
   → early puberty is tall then short, late puberty the reverse
```

Girls: **thelarche → growth spurt → pubarche → menarche**, about 2 years after thelarche, with the first cycles often **anovulatory**. One hormone pair localises delay: **low LH/FSH with low steroids = hypogonadotrophic** (hypothalamic–pituitary: low weight, Kallmann's); **high LH/FSH with low steroids = hypergonadotrophic** (gonad failure: Turner 45,X, Klinefelter 47,XXY).

### Lactation: two hormones, two different jobs

```
SUPPLY — PROLACTIN (anterior pituitary)
SUCKLING → nipple afferents → hypothalamus → DOPAMINE FALLS
   → release from TONIC INHIBITION → prolactin → ALVEOLI MAKE MILK
   → prolactin also ⊣ GnRH → LACTATIONAL AMENORRHŒA
     (contraceptive, but fertility can return before the first period)

LET-DOWN — OXYTOCIN (posterior pituitary, made in hypothalamus)
SUCKLING / sight of infant → oxytocin → MYOEPITHELIAL CELLS CONTRACT
   → milk ejected → suckling intensifies → MORE oxytocin
   ★ POSITIVE FEEDBACK, self-limiting: an empty breast ends the stimulus
```

**Involution** after weaning: without prolactin stimulation the alveolar epithelium undergoes **apoptosis**, fat returns, only scarred ducts remain. A **prolactinoma** causes amenorrhoea and infertility because dopamine is prolactin's brake ([04 — The endocrine system](04-endocrine-system.md)).

### Menopause: the lesion is the ovary, the signal is lost feedback

Menopause (median ~51) is the endpoint of a **pool fixed before birth** — unlike sperm there is no stem-cell reserve.

```
FOLLICLE POOL DEPLETED → fewer follicles recruited each cycle
        ↓ INHIBIN B AND OESTRADIOL FALL
NEGATIVE FEEDBACK LIFTED → FSH RISES MARKEDLY
   (earliest sign, often >25 IU/L on day 3); LH rises modestly
        ↓
ANOVULATION — luteal phase shortens first (premenopause), cycles
become irregular, then absent
        ↓
SYMPTOMS FOLLOW OESTROGEN DEFICIENCY, NOT HIGH FSH:
   vasomotor flushes, vaginal atrophy, accelerated bone loss
   ([01 — Skeletal system](01-skeletal-system.md)),
   adverse lipid change ([05 — Cardiovascular system](05-cardiovascular-system.md))
```

**High gonadotrophins with low end hormone localises failure to the gonad** — FSH marks the missing feedback; it does not cause the flushes.

## Contraception: blocking one step in the pathway

Each method cuts one link; the events cut are in [02 — Fertilisation and the zygote](../11-reproduction-and-development/02-fertilization-and-the-zygote.md).

```
SPERM DEPOSITED → cervix → uterus → tube  ┐
                                           ├─► MEET → FERTILISATION
OOVUM ovulated → fimbriae → tube          ┘        │
                                          zygote → IMPLANTATION (~day 6–7)
```

| Method | Mechanism (where the chain is cut) | Prevents | Typical 12-month failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Combined hormonal** (pill, patch, ring) | Exogenous oestrogen + progestogen → **suppress GnRH pulsatility → FSH/LH fall** → no dominant follicle, **no LH surge**; mucus thickened | **Ovulation** | ~7% |
| **Progestogen-only pill** (desogestrel) | Central **suppression of the LH surge**, hostile mucus, unfavourable endometrium | Ovulation in most cycles | ~7% |
| **Depot progestogen** (DMPA) | Sustained progestogen → anovulation, mucus, endometrial atrophy | Ovulation | ~4%/year |
| **Levonorgestrel IUS** | Local progestogen → **mucus plug**, endometrial atrophy, some anovulation | Fertilisation, implantation | <1% |
| **Copper IUD** | **Copper ions spermotoxic** (enzyme and membrane damage) plus sterile endometrial inflammation | **Sperm and oocyte before fertilisation** | <1% |
| **Male/female condom** | Physical **barrier** in the tract | Sperm reaching the cervix — and, uniquely, **STI transmission** | ~13% (male), ~24% (female) |
| **Diaphragm + spermicide** | Barrier plus **nonoxynol-9**, a sperm-membrane detergent | Fertilisation | ~17% |
| **Vasectomy** | **Occludes the vas deferens** — transport only; Leydig cells, testosterone, libido and erection untouched | Sperm reaching the ejaculate | <1% |
| **Tubal ligation** | Occludes the fallopian tube | Oocyte–sperm encounter, zygote transport | ~0.5% |
| **Emergency (levonorgestrel, ulipristal, copper IUD)** | **Delay or block ovulation** if taken before the LH surge — all **upstream of fertilisation** | Fertilisation | Falls steeply with time |
| **Abstinence / fertility awareness** | No sperm in the fertile window (sperm survive ~5 days + ovulation day) | Sperm–oocyte encounter | ~12–24% |

**Typical versus perfect use** explains the numbers: typical figures count real couples over 12 months, so methods needing **action at every act** (pill, condom, awareness) show a large perfect→typical gap, while **fit-and-forget devices** (IUD, implant, sterilisation) sit near perfect use. Central methods fail on a missed dose; local methods fail only when the barrier fails.

## Medical relevance

**Infertility workup follows the pathway backwards.** After 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse (6 months if the partner is ≥35): **semen analysis ×2** (volume, concentration, ≥40% motility — [11 — Spermatogenesis and oogenesis](../11-reproduction-and-development/01-spermatogenesis-and-oogenesis.md)), **ovulation tracking** (basal body temperature, urinary LH kits, day-21 progesterone proving recent ovulation), **tubal patency** (hysterosalpingography) and uterine cavity assessment. About 40% of couples have a female factor, 40% a male factor, 20% combined or unexplained — so both partners are tested in parallel, and because a spermatogenic cycle takes ~64–74 days, febrile illness or drugs are sought **three months back**.

**Polycystic ovary syndrome is anovulation, not a meiotic defect.**

```
INSULIN RESISTANCE → HYPERINSULINAEMIA
   → drives theca androgen synthesis; suppresses hepatic SHBG
     → FREE androgens rise further
FAST GnRH PULSES → LH:FSH ratio ↑ → theca driven, granulosa under-supported
        ↓ many antral follicles START, NONE reaches dominance
no sustained oestrogen rise → NO POSITIVE-FEEDBACK SWITCH → NO LH SURGE
        ↓
CHRONIC ANOVULATION: oligomenorrhoea, infertility, hirsutism, acne,
   and UNOPPOSED OESTROGEN → endometrial hyperplasia risk
```

Rotterdam criteria (oligo/anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic morphology, other causes excluded) make it a **diagnosis of exclusion**. Weight loss restores pulsatility; **letrozole** is first-line for ovulation induction; metformin targets the insulin arm. The machinery is intact — **the failure is the signal**.

**Endometriosis is the menstrual cycle in the wrong place.** Endometrial glands and stroma outside the uterus (ovaries — endometriomas, pouch of Douglas, uterosacral ligaments) **respond to the same steroids**, so they bleed cyclically → peritoneal blood provokes inflammation, adhesions and scarring → **dysmenorrhoea, dyspareunia, cyclical pain and infertility** through distorted tubal anatomy. Retrograde menstruation is near-universal (Sampson), so disease in only some women implicates immune clearance and genetics. Treatment removes the cycle: **progestogens, combined contraception or continuous GnRH agonism**, or surgery.

**Sexually transmitted infections turn a duct system into a one-way climb.** Gonorrhoea (*Neisseria gonorrhoeae*) and chlamydia (*Chlamydia trachomatis*, an obligate intracellular bacterium) infect the cervix first — often silently — then ascend; attachment, invasion and immune evasion are in [06 — Host–pathogen interaction](../07-microbiology/06-host-pathogen-interaction.md):

```
CERVICITIS (often asymptomatic) → ENDOMETRITIS → SALPINGITIS
   = PELVIC INFLAMMATORY DISEASE → tubal scarring, fimbrial damage
        ↓
TUBAL FACTOR INFERTILITY + ECTOPIC PREGNANCY
```

Chlamydia is screened for *because* it is silent; untreated gonorrhoea can ascend within days. **The condom is the only method blocking both routes** — a barrier for sperm and for organism-bearing secretions. Existing defences matter too: cervical mucus, the acidic vagina with its lactobacilli ([07 — Beneficial microorganisms](../07-microbiology/07-beneficial-microorganisms.md)) and antimicrobial seminal fluid must all be defeated first.

**Cervical cancer is viral oncogenesis with a screening window.** High-risk **HPV 16 and 18** infect basal cervical epithelium through micro-abrasions; persistent infection overexpresses **E6 (degrades p53)** and **E7 (inactivates Rb)**, removing both major cell-cycle brakes — the viral strategy is in [03 — Viruses](../07-microbiology/03-viruses.md). CIN 1 → 3 → invasive carcinoma takes years, which is why **HPV testing plus cytology (Pap smear)** can intercept it and **vaccination before sexual debut** prevents it; clearance is cell-mediated, so immunosuppression and smoking raise risk.

**Hormonal contraception side effects follow from hepatic steroid action.** Oral oestrogen passes through the liver and **raises clotting factors II, VII, IX and X while lowering antithrombin and thrombomodulin** — a prothrombotic shift raising **venous thromboembolism** risk ~3–4×, highest in the first three months and compounded by smoking, obesity, age >35 and migraine with aura ([05 — Cardiovascular system](05-cardiovascular-system.md)). Those patients receive **progestogen-only or non-hormonal** methods. Nausea, breast tenderness and headache are oestrogen-dose dependent; blood pressure rises via angiotensinogen.

**Erectile dysfunction drugs are the NO–cGMP chain applied.** **PDE5 inhibitors** do not release nitric oxide — they block breakdown of cGMP that NO already made, so an intact parasympathetic signal and **sexual stimulation are required**. The dangerous interaction is with **nitrates**: glyceryl trinitrate feeds the *same* guanylate cyclase pathway, so together they drive cGMP too far → profound, occasionally fatal, **hypotension**. α-blockers add hypotension; headache and flushing are off-target PDE inhibition (PDE1, PDE6); the same pathway treats **pulmonary arterial hypertension** ([06 — Respiratory system](06-respiratory-system.md)).

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "The reproductive system is essential like the others" | It is **essential to the species, not the individual** — removal threatens only fertility, never life. |
| "Sperm are made in the interstitium" | Tubules make sperm (**FSH → Sertoli**); interstitial **Leydig cells make testosterone (LH)**. Two compartments, two hormones, two feedback signals. |
| "Sperm are motile when produced" | They leave the testis **non-motile and infertile**; **motility is acquired in the epididymis**, fertilising capacity only after capacitation in the female tract. |
| "Semen is mostly sperm" | Sperm are **<1–5%**; **~60–70% is seminal-vesicle fluid** (fructose, prostaglandins, alkaline), ~25–30% prostatic (PSA). |
| "A vasectomy lowers testosterone" | It occludes the **vas deferens only** — transport, not endocrine. Leydig cells, testosterone, libido and erection are unchanged. |
| "Erection is simply more blood in" | Inflow must be matched by **veno-occlusion** of venules against the tunica albuginea; arterial disease or a venous leak each defeats it. |
| "Ovulation is triggered by FSH" | FSH recruits the follicle; **ovulation is triggered by the LH surge**, which sustained high oestradiol provokes by switching negative to **positive** feedback. |
| "Oestrogen is the only ovarian hormone" | The **corpus luteum makes progesterone** (dominant days 15–28) and theca cells make androgens; the three act at different times on different targets. |
| "Menstruation is caused by rising FSH" | It is caused by **progesterone and oestrogen withdrawal** → spiral-artery spasm → ischaemic shedding; FSH rises *because* steroids fell. |
| "Prolactin and oxytocin both make milk" | **Prolactin = synthesis** (released from dopamine's tonic inhibition); **oxytocin = ejection** by myoepithelial contraction — a positive-feedback reflex. |
| "High FSH causes menopausal symptoms" | FSH **rises because oestrogen fell** — loss of negative feedback; symptoms are **oestrogen-deficiency effects**, FSH only localises the lesion. |
| "All contraception works the same way" | Each cuts a different link: hormonal = **no LH surge**, copper = sperm/oocyte toxicity, barrier = no sperm in the tract, sterilisation = transport, emergency = ovulation delayed. |

## Key facts

- **Species, not individual**: structures produce, deliver or support gametes; control is the HPG axis, uniquely including **positive-feedback steps** (LH surge, milk let-down).
- **Testis = two organs**: tubules (FSH → Sertoli → spermatogenesis + inhibin B) and interstitium (LH → Leydig → testosterone at ~50–100× serum level).
- **Scrotum** holds the testes **2–3 °C below core** via dartos/cremaster reflexes and **countercurrent cooling in the pampiniform plexus**; heat damages meiosis (cryptorchidism, varicocele, fever effects lag ~3 months).
- **Epididymis** = maturation (motility acquired) and storage; **vas deferens** = sympathetic rapid transport; **glands** = seminal vesicles ~60–70% (fructose, prostaglandins, alkaline), prostate (PSA), bulbourethral (neutralise urethral acid).
- **Erection**: parasympathetic → **NO → guanylate cyclase → cGMP → relaxation → sinusoidal filling with veno-occlusion**; **PDE5 inhibitors block cGMP breakdown**; α₁ sympathetic tone causes detumescence.
- **Emission is sympathetic** (T11–L2, internal sphincter closes); **expulsion is somatic** (pudendal nerve, bulbospongiosus).
- **Female tract**: fimbriae capture the oocyte, **ampulla is the usual fertilisation site**; uterus = perimetrium / myometrium / endometrium, with the functionalis shed by **spiral-artery spasm**; cervix mucus is **oestrogen-thin, progesterone-thick**; vagina held at **pH 3.8–4.5 by lactobacillus fermentation of glycogen**.
- **Ovarian cycle**: FSH recruits a cohort → one dominant follicle → **sustained oestradiol flips negative to positive feedback → LH surge → ovulation (~day 14)** → **corpus luteum** progesterone → FSH/LH suppressed → without hCG, luteolysis → **menstruation**.
- **Pregnancy recognition**: embryonic **hCG mimics LH (shared α subunit)** and rescues the corpus luteum; the placenta takes over steroidogenesis by ~weeks 8–10.
- **Uterine cycle**: menstrual = hormone fall → ischaemic shedding; proliferative = **oestrogen rebuild**; secretory = **progesterone, glycogen-rich glands**, receptive days 20–24.
- **Puberty is switching on GnRH pulsatility** (kisspeptin gate, permitted by leptin), not the gonads; **oestrogen drives then terminates** growth by fusing the epiphyses.
- **Lactation**: prolactin = synthesis under dopamine inhibition; oxytocin = ejection by **positive feedback**; suckling ⊣ GnRH → lactational amenorrhoea; weaning → **involution**.
- **Menopause**: follicle depletion → **oestradiol and inhibin fall → loss of negative feedback → FSH rises markedly**; symptoms follow oestrogen deficiency, not FSH.

## Practice questions

**1. The testes are held 2–3 °C below core temperature by all of the following EXCEPT**

A. Countercurrent heat exchange in the pampiniform plexus
B. Contraction of the dartos and cremasteric muscles in the cold
C. Thin, hairless scrotal skin with little subcutaneous fat
D. Increased metabolic heat production by Leydig cells

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Cooling depends on vasomotor reflexes, an externalised thin-walled pouch and venous countercurrent exchange. Leydig cells generate heat rather than dissipating it, so steroidogenesis works against thermoregulation.

---

**2. Spermatozoa first acquire progressive motility during transit through the**

A. Seminiferous tubules
B. Rete testis
C. Epididymis
D. Vas deferens

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Sperm leaving the testis are immotile; over ~12–14 days of epididymal transit membrane remodelling produces progressive motility, and the tail stores them. Fertilising capacity comes only after capacitation in the female tract.

---

**3. The fraction making up about 70% of semen volume is contributed by the**

A. Prostate
B. Seminal vesicles
C. Bulbourethral glands
D. Seminiferous tubules

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Seminal-vesicle fluid is the largest fraction (~60–70%) and supplies fructose, prostaglandins and alkaline buffering. The prostate contributes ~25–30% with PSA; the bulbourethral glands under 1%.

---

**4. Parasympathetic nerves produce an erection by releasing nitric oxide, which**

A. Activates adenylyl cyclase to raise cAMP
B. Opens voltage-gated calcium channels in smooth muscle
C. Stimulates soluble guanylate cyclase to make cGMP, relaxing smooth muscle
D. Blocks phosphodiesterase type 5 in endothelium

**Answer: C**

Explanation: NO activates soluble guanylate cyclase, GTP becomes cGMP, calcium falls and the sinusoids relax and fill. PDE5 inhibitors prevent cGMP breakdown, amplifying this chain rather than starting it.

---

**5. During ejaculation the internal urethral sphincter contracts under sympathetic control in order to**

A. Allow retrograde flow into the bladder
B. Close the bladder neck so semen is expelled forward
C. Engorge the corpus spongiosum
D. Initiate the erection reflex from S2–S4

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Emission is sympathetic (T11–L2) and simultaneous bladder-neck closure prevents retrograde ejaculation; failure of that closure causes it. Erection is parasympathetic, expulsion somatic.

---

**6. Mid-cycle, oestrogen switches from suppressing to stimulating gonadotrophin release because**

A. The corpus luteum begins secreting LH
B. FSH receptors are down-regulated by inhibin
C. Sustained high oestradiol for ~50 hours switches the pituitary to positive feedback
D. Progesterone rises and sensitises the hypothalamus

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Modest oestradiol from the growing follicle inhibits the axis, but the dominant follicle's sustained high output flips the sign, speeding GnRH pulses and sensitising the pituitary to give the LH surge. Progesterone dominates only after ovulation.

---

**7. In the secretory phase the endometrium becomes glycogen-rich and receptive under the influence of**

A. Falling progesterone causing spiral-artery spasm
B. Rising oestrogen from the dominant follicle
C. Progesterone from the corpus luteum
D. hCG from an implanting blastocyst

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The corpus luteum's progesterone converts the proliferative lining into coiled glycogen-rich glands with a receptive window at days 20–24. A describes menstruation, B the proliferative phase, D only applies if implantation occurs.

---

**8. A man two years after vasectomy has normal libido, erections and testosterone because the procedure**

A. Removes the Leydig cells from the interstitium
B. Blocks sperm transport while leaving arterial supply and the HPG axis intact
C. Lowers testosterone by preventing absorption from the epididymis
D. Suppresses GnRH through inhibin feedback

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Vasectomy occludes the vas deferens only; the testicular artery, Leydig cells and LH/FSH drive are untouched, so androgen physiology is normal. Only sperm are absent from an otherwise normal-volume ejaculate.

---

**9. After weaning the breast undergoes involution because of**

A. Loss of prolactin stimulation causing alveolar apoptosis
B. Sudden withdrawal of oxytocin causing duct rupture
C. Rising FSH acting on mammary receptors
D. Dopamine directly attacking the alveoli

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Milk synthesis requires continuous prolactin signalling, so removing suckling removes prolactin and the glandular epithelium undergoes programmed cell death with fat replacement. Oxytocin mediates ejection, not tissue maintenance.

---

**10. A 51-year-old has hot flushes and a day-3 FSH of 60 IU/L. The mechanism linking these is**

A. High FSH directly damaging the thermoregulatory centre
B. Primary ovarian failure removing negative feedback so FSH rises, while symptoms follow oestrogen deficiency
C. Excess GnRH pulsatility caused by ovarian androgens
D. Anterior pituitary failure to clear progesterone

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Follicle depletion means low oestradiol and inhibin, so feedback is lifted and FSH rises markedly — the marker of ovarian failure, not its cause. Flushes, vaginal atrophy and bone loss are consequences of oestrogen deficiency itself.
