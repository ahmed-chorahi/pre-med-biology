# The Digestive System

## Why it matters

Food arrives as macromolecules — starch, protein, triacylglycerol — too large, and in fat's case too insoluble, to cross a membrane. The body therefore does its chemistry **outside cells**: it secretes water, ions, and enzymes into a tube, hydrolyses food there, then absorbs the products across a single epithelium using the carriers, pumps, and osmosis of [03 — Active transport](../03-cellular-processes/03-active-transport.md) and [02 — Passive transport](../03-cellular-processes/02-passive-transport.md).

Two facts organise the chapter. The **alimentary canal is one tube, open at both ends** — mouth to anus, about seven metres — whose lumen is technically outside the body. And the tube is fed by a **chemical plant** (salivary glands, liver, pancreas) that never touches food but pours reagents into it.

```
FOOD   ── hydrolysis outside cells (enzymes + water) ──▶  MONOMERS
            mouth: salivary amylase · stomach: pepsin
            small intestine: pancreatic + brush-border enzymes
        ── absorption across one epithelium ──▶
BLOOD → portal vein → LIVER (first pass)   ·   LYMPH (fat, via lacteals)
        ──▶  BODY CELLS: oxidation or storage
```

The logic: **break food into the monomers the body already knows how to use, then cross one barrier.** The section rule runs throughout — **structure → function → mechanism → regulation → homeostasis** — ending with the loops that keep a wall intact while it secretes acid strong enough to dissolve metal.

## The alimentary canal as an organ

[02 — Organs and organ systems](../09-human-biology/02-organs-and-organ-systems.md) defines an organ as tissues combined for a common function. The gut wall is the clearest case in the body: **the same four-layer recipe is repeated from oesophagus to rectum**, only the proportions change.

### The four layers

| Layer | Composition | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mucosa** | Epithelium + lamina propria (CT, vessels, nerves, lymphoid tissue) + muscularis mucosae | **Secretion and absorption**; the only layer touching food; folds into villi |
| **Submucosa** | Dense irregular CT | Support; carries vessels and the **submucosal nerve plexus** |
| **Muscularis externa** | **Inner circular** + **outer longitudinal** smooth muscle, myenteric plexus between | **Motility** — circular narrows and mixes, longitudinal shortens and propels |
| **Serosa / adventitia** | Mesothelium (abdomen) or fibrous CT | Outer coat; serosa lets loops slide without friction |

Two orthogonal muscle sheets around a tube generate every movement the gut needs, and the **nerve plexuses sit in the wall itself** — a segment cut free of its extrinsic nerves still peristalses. The mix is the four tissue types of [01 — Four tissue types](../09-human-biology/01-four-tissue-types.md): epithelial lining, connective support, smooth-muscle movement, neural control.

### Regional modifications

| Region | Epithelium | Specialisation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oesophagus** | **Stratified squamous, non-keratinised** | Abrasion resistance — **no villi, no keratin, no absorption** |
| **Stomach** | Simple columnar | Deep **gastric pits and glands**; mucus surface, acid-secreting cells beneath |
| **Small intestine** | Simple columnar + goblet cells | Circular folds → **villi** → **microvilli** — the absorptive machine |
| **Large intestine** | Simple columnar, goblet-rich | No villi; thick mucus for lubrication |

Three generalisations: **villi occur only in the small intestine**; stratified squamous means abrasion-resistance, not skin (the oesophagus is never keratinised); goblet cells increase toward the colon because the problem downstream is lubrication, not defence. Salivary glands, liver, and pancreas are **accessory** organs — food never passes through them, they only supply reagents through ducts.

## The mouth

### Mechanical breakdown

Chewing (temporalis, masseter, pterygoids, with the tongue positioning the bolus) cuts food and mixes it with saliva. Its purpose is **surface area**: an intact almond and a mouthful of crumbs expose the same mass to very different amounts of enzyme.

### Chemical breakdown

Saliva (0.5–1.5 L/day, pH 6.3–7.4, buffered by HCO₃⁻) contains:

| Component | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Salivary amylase (ptyalin)** | Cleaves internal α-1,4 bonds of starch → **maltose, maltotriose, α-limit dextrins**; cannot cut α-1,6 branches or cellulose |
| **Lingual lipase** | Acid-stable — keeps working **in the stomach** |
| **Lysozyme, IgA, lactoferrin** | Oral antimicrobial defence |
| **Mucins, HCO₃⁻** | Lubrication, bolus formation, buffering |

```
STARCH ── salivary amylase ──▶ maltose + maltotriose + limit dextrins
   ── inactivated by gastric acid ──▶ pancreatic amylase resumes in the duodenum
```

Salivary amylase is **secreted ready-made, not as a zymogen**: it must work in an open, non-sterile cavity where an inactive precursor could never be reliably switched on.

### Cephalic phase: taste, chewing, vagus

Sight, smell, taste, and even the thought of food fire the **cephalic phase** through the **vagus nerve** ([03 — Nervous system](03-nervous-system.md)):

```
taste / smell / chewing / anticipation
        ▼
brainstem ── vagal efferents ──▶ stomach: HCl, pepsinogen, gastrin, histamine
```

Acetylcholine acts directly on parietal and chief cells and gastrin-releasing peptide on G cells, so **acid is already pouring when the bolus lands**. Surgical **vagotomy** therefore reduced ulcer recurrence for decades before antibiotic treatment of *Helicobacter pylori* made it obsolete. Sympathetic activation does the opposite — stress shuts digestion down.

## The stomach

### Structure: pits and glands

A J-shaped reservoir with cardia, fundus, body, and antrum; its muscularis externa adds a **third oblique layer**, which is what allows churning. Mucosal folds (**rugae**) flatten on filling; **gastric pits** open into **gastric glands**.

| Cell | Secretion | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surface mucous cells** | Alkaline **mucus** + HCO₃⁻ | Barrier against acid |
| **Parietal (oxyntic) cells** | **HCl** and **intrinsic factor** | pH 1.5–3.5; binds vitamin B₁₂ |
| **Chief (zymogenic) cells** | **Pepsinogen** | Protein digestion once activated |
| **G cells** (antrum) | **Gastrin** | Stimulates acid, pepsin, mucosal growth |
| **ECL cells** | **Histamine** | Paracrine drive of parietal cells |
| **D cells** (antrum) | **Somatostatin** | The brake — switches acid off |

### Parietal cells: HCl and intrinsic factor

**Acid is made by primary active transport.** Parietal cells are packed with mitochondria and canalicular membranes studded with **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase**:

```
H₂O + CO₂ ── carbonic anhydrase ──▶ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
H⁺  ── apical H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (1 H⁺ out : 1 K⁺ in, ATP) ──▶ LUMEN  pH 1.5–3.5
Cl⁻ ── apical channels ──▶ LUMEN              HCO₃⁻ ──▶ blood ("alkaline tide")
```

The luminal proton concentration is roughly a million-fold above blood. The pump is **the target of proton-pump inhibitors** (omeprazole), which acylate it irreversibly until new protein is made; H₂-receptor antagonists block the histamine signal instead, less completely.

**Intrinsic factor (IF)** is made only here. It binds **vitamin B₁₂**, and the IF–B₁₂ complex is absorbed in the **ileum**. Without IF, B₁₂ cannot be absorbed however much is eaten: autoimmune loss of parietal cells gives **pernicious anaemia**, and B₁₂ deficiency impairs thymidylate synthesis, producing **megaloblastic (macrocytic) anaemia** — a blood disorder caused by a stomach defect, linked directly to red-cell production in [05 — Cardiovascular system](05-cardiovascular-system.md).

### Chief cells: pepsinogen to pepsin

Chief cells release **pepsinogen**, an inactive **zymogen** — the standard defence wherever a protease is made by living cells ([05 — Enzymes](../01-biochemistry/05-enzymes.md)):

```
pepsinogen ── HCl (pH < 5) ──▶ PEPSIN + peptide fragment
pepsinogen ── pepsin ──▶ more pepsin                    ← autocatalysis

PEPSIN: endopeptidase, acid optimum pH 1.5–2.0, denatured above pH 6
        ▶ large polypeptides — amino acids come later, in the duodenum
```

HCl does two jobs at once: it **denatures** protein, unfolding it so peptide bonds are exposed, and it **activates** the enzyme that cuts them. Neutralisation in the duodenum then denatures pepsin — a built-in switch-off.

### Why the stomach does not digest itself

```
1  MUCUS LAYER       mucins gel into a 200–600 µm blanket; traps H⁺, slows approach
2  BICARBONATE       epithelial HCO₃⁻ neutralises H⁺ INSIDE the gel:
                     luminal pH 2  ──▶  pH 6–7 at the cell surface
3  TIGHT JUNCTIONS   paracellular reflux blocked; mucus flows to the lumen;
                     prostaglandins E₂/I₂ sustain blood flow that clears stray H⁺
4  RAPID TURNOVER    stem cells replace the surface every 3–5 days — injury is
                     shed faster than it accumulates
   failure of any arm ──▶ erosion of epithelium ──▶ ULCER
   (NSAIDs block COX → less prostaglandin → defences 2 and 4 weaken)
```

### Gastrin, churning, chyme

**G cells** release **gastrin** into the blood when peptides, amino acids, distension, or vagal input stimulate them. Gastrin drives parietal cells directly, drives ECL histamine, and is **trophic** — it grows the acid-secreting mucosa. **D-cell somatostatin** shuts it off when antral pH falls below about 3: negative feedback defending against over-acidification.

Meanwhile the muscle produces **retropulsion** — the antrum contracts against a closed pylorus, forcing chyme backward to grind particles below 1–2 mm and mix them with gastric juice into **chyme**. The **pyloric sphincter** releases only small squirts, because the duodenum can neutralise only so much acid at a time: **emptying is matched to neutralising capacity**.

## The small intestine

About 90% of nutrient absorption and most water absorption occur here: **duodenum** (chemical processing), **jejunum** (bulk absorption), **ileum** (vitamin B₁₂ and bile-salt capture).

### Surface-area arithmetic

Transport rate rises with area and falls with distance ([02 — Passive transport](../03-cellular-processes/02-passive-transport.md)). A bare seven-metre tube of 2.5 cm radius offers only ~0.5 m², so the mucosa amplifies three times:

| Level | Structure | Factor | Cumulative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Circular folds (plicae circulares)** — spiral the chyme, slow the flow | ×3 | ~1.5 m² |
| 2 | **Villi** — 0.5–1.5 mm fingers, each with a capillary loop and a **lacteal** | ×10 | ~15 m² |
| 3 | **Microvilli** — the **brush border**, bearing the terminal enzymes | ×20 | **≈ 200 m², ~200× the tube** |

```
0.5 m² ── folds ×3 ──▶ 1.5 ── villi ×10 ──▶ 15 ── microvilli ×20 ──▶ ≈ 200 m²
```

Each villus is **one epithelium thick**, so diffusion distance to the capillary is ~40–50 µm: area up, distance down. The **brush-border enzymes** (maltase, sucrase, lactase, peptidases) sit in the membrane itself, so hydrolysis happens **at the point of absorption**, each monomer captured before it can diffuse away.

### Regional specialisation

| Region | Task | Distinctive features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Duodenum** (~25 cm) | Neutralise and process | Receives bile and pancreatic juice; Brunner's glands; I, S, K endocrine cells |
| **Jejunum** (~2.5 m) | **Bulk absorption** | Longest villi, thickest wall, most folds — area at its maximum |
| **Ileum** (~3.5 m) | Scavenge and recycle | Cubilin receptors for **IF–B₁₂**; **bile salt reabsorption**; Peyer's patches ([09 — Immune system](09-immune-system.md)) |

The **enterohepatic circulation** returns ~95% of secreted bile salts to the liver for reuse — a small pool recycled several times per meal. The order is chemical: neutralise first, absorb the bulk second, capture what is scarce last.

### Carbohydrate: digestion and absorption

| Step | Site | Machinery | Product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Luminal hydrolysis | Duodenum | **Pancreatic amylase** | Maltose, maltotriose, limit dextrins |
| Terminal hydrolysis | Brush border | **Maltase, sucrase, isomaltase, lactase** | **Glucose, galactose, fructose** |
| Apical entry | Enterocyte | **SGLT1** — Na⁺-linked symport (secondary active); fructose via **GLUT5** | Monomers inside |
| Basolateral exit | Enterocyte → blood | **GLUT2** — facilitated diffusion | Portal capillary |

```
lumen: Na⁺ HIGH (set up by the basolateral Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase)
SGLT1: 2 Na⁺ + glucose ──▶ enterocyte   (glucose dragged AGAINST its gradient)
GLUT2: glucose ──▶ capillary ──▶ PORTAL VEIN
```

This is **secondary active transport** exactly as in [03 — Active transport](../03-cellular-processes/03-active-transport.md): the transporter hydrolyses no ATP, it borrows the pump's Na⁺ gradient — block the pump and sugar absorption stops too. It also explains **oral rehydration therapy**: with SGLT1 intact, glucose drags Na⁺ and therefore water with it even during secretory diarrhoea.

### Protein: digestion and absorption

HCl and **pepsin** start it in the stomach; **trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase**, and **carboxypeptidase** cut it to peptides in the duodenum; **brush-border aminopeptidases and dipeptidases** finish it. Absorption uses **Na⁺-linked amino-acid symporters** plus **PepT1**, an H⁺-coupled symporter for **di- and tripeptides** hydrolysed *inside* the cell. Two points are routinely missed: most protein is absorbed as **peptides, not free amino acids**, and the process is active and **saturable** — a large protein load is absorbed more slowly than the same mass of sugar.

### Fat: micelles, re-esterification, lacteals

Fat has a physical problem (insoluble) as well as a chemical one, so three distinct stages are needed — **emulsification (physical), hydrolysis (enzymatic), uptake (transport)**:

```
1  EMULSIFICATION — PHYSICAL: BILE SALTS coat droplets, prevent re-fusion ──▶ area ↑
2  HYDROLYSIS — ENZYMATIC: lipase + COLIPASE ──▶ 2 fatty acids + 2-monoacylglycerol
                            phospholipase A₂, cholesterol esterase
3  MICELLE FERRY: bile salts + FA + monoglycerides + vitamins ──▶ MICELLE
                  ──▶ monomers DIFFUSE across the brush border into the enterocyte
4  RE-ESTERIFICATION (smooth ER): FA + 2-monoacylglycerol + cholesterol + apoB-48
                                  ──▶ CHYLOMICRON
5  EXPORT — LYMPH FIRST: chylomicron ──▶ LACTEAL ──▶ lymph ──▶ thoracic duct
                         ──▶ subclavian vein ──▶ blood ──▶ lipoprotein lipase
                         ──▶ remnants ──▶ LIVER
```

**Why lymph?** Chylomicrons (75–450 nm) are too large to hold comfortably in capillaries, and routing them through the **portal vein** would send dietary fat to the liver before muscle and adipose had taken their share — so lymph delivers fat **downstream of the liver on the first pass**. Only short- and medium-chain fatty acids, water-soluble and albumin-bound, enter portal blood directly: the exception that proves the rule. Bile salts are not consumed either — the ileum recovers ~95%, so a resected ileum causes **fat malabsorption and steatorrhoea**.

### Water and electrolytes

About **9 L/day** enters the gut (2 L swallowed, 7 L secreted); the small intestine absorbs ~8 L, the colon ~1 L, and <100 mL leaves as faeces. **Water is never absorbed actively** — it follows solute osmotically. **Na⁺** is the main driver, entering with glucose and amino acids (SGLT1), with amino acids alone, and through **ENaC** channels in the colon; Cl⁻ follows and water follows both. K⁺ is absorbed in the small intestine but **secreted by the colon** — which is why diarrhoea depletes potassium disproportionately.

## Accessory organs

### Pancreas: zymogens and a bicarbonate flood

About 98% of the pancreas is exocrine: **acinar cells** secrete enzymes as **zymogens**, **duct cells** secrete buffer.

| Zymogen | Activated by | Active enzyme | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Trypsinogen** | **Enterokinase** on the duodenal brush border | **Trypsin** | Activates all the others |
| Chymotrypsinogen | Trypsin | Chymotrypsin | Aromatic amino-acid bonds |
| Proelastase | Trypsin | Elastase | Elastin and small peptides |
| Procarboxypeptidase | Trypsin | Carboxypeptidase A/B | Terminal amino acids |
| Prophospholipase A₂ | Trypsin | Phospholipase A₂ | Phospholipids |

Pancreatic **amylase** digests starch and **lipase + colipase** digests fat; both stop below about pH 4.

```
ENTEROKINASE (duodenal brush border — the only place it exists)
      ▼
trypsinogen ──▶ TRYPSIN ──▶ chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin
                            proelastase       → elastase
                            procarboxypeptidase → carboxypeptidase
                            trypsinogen       → more trypsin   ← amplification
```

The design is **fail-safe by compartmentalisation**: proteases capable of digesting the pancreas are made inactive, and activation rights belong to an enzyme present only on the duodenal surface. Premature activation inside the gland is **acute pancreatitis** — autodigestion — and **pancreatic secretory trypsin inhibitor** neutralises small escapes.

Duct cells solve the other problem: chyme arriving at pH 1.5–3.5 would ulcerate the duodenum.

| Stimulus | Hormone | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acid chyme (pH < 4.5) | **Secretin** (S cells) | **HCO₃⁻-rich juice**, pH ~8 |
| Fat and protein | **CCK** (I cells) | Enzyme-rich juice |
| Vagal input | ACh | Volume and enzymes |

**The bicarbonate gates the enzymes**: below pH 4 amylase and lipase are inactivated, so neutralisation must win before catalysis can start.

### Liver: the chemical plant

Some 1.4 L/min of nutrient-laden blood arrives from the **hepatic portal vein**:

| Function | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| **Bile production** | Bile salts **emulsify fat** (physical, not enzymatic); phospholipid and cholesterol keep fat in solution; **bilirubin** from heme colours faeces |
| **Plasma proteins** | Albumin (colloid osmotic pressure) and clotting factors — hence liver failure bleeds |
| **Urea cycle** | Nitrogen from deamination of excess amino acids: toxic **NH₃ → urea** → kidney ([08 — Urinary system](08-urinary-system.md)) |
| **Glycogen storage** | Glycogenesis, glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis — buffering glucose between meals |
| **Detoxification** | Smooth-ER **cytochrome P450** oxidises drugs, alcohol, hormones; conjugation makes them water-soluble |
| **First-pass metabolism** | Portal blood passes the liver *before* the systemic circulation |
| **Defence** | Kupffer cells filter gut-derived bacteria and debris ([09 — Immune system](09-immune-system.md)) |

**The urea cycle answers a problem digestion itself creates**: deaminating excess amino acids yields **NH₃**, which is lipid-soluble and crosses the blood–brain barrier; wrapping two nitrogens into urea turns a neurotoxin into an excretable waste.

### Gallbladder: store, concentrate, contract

Bile is made continuously but needed only after meals, so the gallbladder stores 30–50 mL and **concentrates it 5–10-fold** by absorbing water. Regulation is hormonal:

```
fat / protein in the duodenum ──▶ I CELLS release CCK
   ├─▶ gallbladder CONTRACTS
   ├─▶ sphincter of Oddi RELAXES
   ├─▶ pancreatic acini release enzymes
   └─▶ vagal afferents → satiety
```

A stone blocking the cystic duct therefore causes pain **because the gallbladder contracts against a closed outlet** (biliary colic), and stasis is also how stones form: cholesterol precipitates once bile salts can no longer hold it in solution.

## The large intestine

Caecum (with appendix), colon, rectum, anal canal — about 1.5 m, with **taeniae coli** gathering the wall into **haustra** for slow **haustral churning**. It has **no villi and no brush-border enzymes**: there is no enzymatic digestion here by human enzymes. Its jobs:

1. **Absorb water, Na⁺, and Cl⁻** — about 1 L/day, reclaiming what secretion would otherwise cost.
2. **Secrete HCO₃⁻ and mucus** to lubricate and balance absorption.
3. **Store and compact faeces** — indigestible residue, bacteria, sloughed cells, mucus, bilirubin pigments.
4. **Ferment fibre** with the resident microbiota.
5. **Expel** — the **defecation reflex**: rectal distension → stretch receptors → parasympathetic pelvic output → colonic peristalsis, with voluntary relaxation of the external anal sphincter. Transit takes 12–48 hours because the goal is recovery, not speed.

### The microbiome

The colon holds ~10¹¹ bacteria per gram — the densest microbial community in the body ([07 — Beneficial microorganisms](../07-microbiology/07-beneficial-microorganisms.md)). The human genome encodes **no enzyme for the β-1,4 bonds** of cellulose and hemicellulose, so fibre reaching the colon is bacterial property:

| Contribution | Mechanism | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short-chain fatty acids** | Fermentation of fibre → **butyrate, propionate, acetate** | Butyrate is the **preferred fuel of colonocytes**; propionate reaches the liver; acetate enters peripheral metabolism |
| **Vitamin K, B vitamins** | Bacterial synthesis, absorbed in the colon | Important in newborns and after prolonged antibiotics |
| **Colonisation resistance** | Competition for nutrients and adhesion sites, bacteriocins, low pH | Antibiotic suppression opens niches → ***C. difficile*** expansion ([06 — Host–pathogen interaction](../07-microbiology/06-host-pathogen-interaction.md)) |
| **Gas** | CO₂, H₂, CH₄ | Normal flatus; excess causes bloating when unabsorbed substrate arrives |
| **Immune training** | Continuous antigenic stimulation | Development of gut-associated lymphoid tissue and IgA |

## Motility and its regulation

### The enteric nervous system: the "second brain"

The gut wall contains **~500 million neurons** — more than the spinal cord — in two plexuses:

| Plexus | Location | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Myenteric (Auerbach's)** | Between the two muscle layers | **Motility** — frequency and strength of contraction |
| **Submucosal (Meissner's)** | Submucosa | **Secretion and mucosal blood flow** |

Sensory → interneuron → motor arcs run entirely within the wall, so a **transplanted or denervated segment still peristalse**: extrinsic nerves *modulate* rather than generate activity. **Vagal (parasympathetic) input increases** motility and secretion; **sympathetic input inhibits** both and closes sphincters ([03 — Nervous system](03-nervous-system.md)) — the same antagonism seen elsewhere, and the reason stress stops digestion.

### Peristalsis and segmentation

**Peristalsis** obeys the **law of the intestine (Bayliss–Starling reflex)**:

```
DISTENSION by a bolus
   ├─▶ UPSTREAM (behind): ACh + substance P ──▶ circular muscle CONTRACTS
   └─▶ DOWNSTREAM (ahead): VIP + nitric oxide ──▶ muscle RELAXES
                    ▼
        pressure gradient pushes the bolus forward — then repeat
```

The reflex is intrinsic to the myenteric plexus and **direction-agnostic**: reverse the gradient and the reflex reverses, which is how vomiting recruits the same machinery retrograde.

**Segmentation** is the other mode — circular muscle contracts at alternating points with no net movement, **churning chyme against the mucosa** to maximise contact with enzymes and epithelium while minimising transit. Propulsion and mixing are alternating modes of one plexus: peristalsis and segmentation after a meal, the **migrating motor complex** housekeeping sweep between meals.

### Hormonal regulation

| Hormone | Source | Stimulus | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gastrin** | **G cells**, gastric antrum | Peptides, distension, vagal GRP; **inhibited by low antral pH** | ↑ HCl, ↑ pepsinogen, **trophic** growth of acid-secreting mucosa |
| **CCK** | **I cells**, duodenum | **Fat and protein** | Gallbladder contraction, sphincter relaxation, **pancreatic enzyme release**, satiety, slower gastric emptying |
| **Secretin** | **S cells**, duodenum | **Acid, pH < 4.5** | **HCO₃⁻-rich pancreatic juice**; inhibits gastrin and gastric acid |
| **GIP** | **K cells**, duodenum | Glucose and fat | **Incretin** — amplifies insulin after oral glucose; inhibits acid |

Two loops are worth naming. **Secretin** is negative feedback terminating on luminal pH: acid arrives → bicarbonate poured in → pH rises → secretion falls. **GIP's incretin effect** is why oral and intravenous glucose are not metabolically equivalent — and why drugs that copy that effect exist.

## Medical relevance

**Peptic ulcer disease and *Helicobacter pylori*.** Most duodenal and many gastric ulcers follow colonization of the gastric mucus by *H. pylori*, a spiral Gram-negative that survives pH 1 with **urease**:

```
HOST UREA ── bacterial UREASE ──▶ 2 NH₃ + CO₂
NH₃ + H⁺ ──▶ NH₄⁺  ──▶ LOCAL pH RISES around the bacterium
      ▼
survives in the mucus layer → adheres → inflammation (CagA, vacA) ──▶ ULCER
```

The strategy is **niche construction** — changing the chemistry of its own surroundings rather than tolerating the original one ([06 — Host–pathogen interaction](../07-microbiology/06-host-pathogen-interaction.md)). Treatment follows the mechanism: a **proton-pump inhibitor** raises luminal pH, weakening the shield and slowing replication, while **two antibiotics** (clarithromycin with amoxicillin or metronidazole) clear the organism — and **under-dosing selects resistance**, which is why the full course matters. NSAID ulcers are a separate mechanism: blocking cyclo-oxygenase removes the prostaglandin-driven mucus, bicarbonate, and mucosal blood flow of the chain above.

**Coeliac disease** is autoimmune: in HLA-DQ2/DQ8 individuals, gluten peptides drive T-cell inflammation and **villous atrophy**. The malabsorption is arithmetic — flattened villi mean lost brush border, so SGLT1, lactase, and peptidases fall with the surface area ([09 — Immune system](09-immune-system.md)). Secondary lactose intolerance follows, because lactase sits on the villus tip, the first cells destroyed.

**Lactose intolerance is a transport defect, not an infection:**

```
lactose unabsorbed (no brush-border lactase)
   ├─▶ osmotic water retention in the lumen ──▶ OSMOTIC DIARRHOEA
   └─▶ bacterial fermentation ──▶ SCFA + CO₂/H₂ ──▶ gas, bloating, cramps
```

**Primary (adult-type) hypolactasia** is the normal age-related decline in lactase; **secondary** deficiency follows villous damage (coeliac disease, gastroenteritis) and is reversible.

**Gallstones** are about 80% **cholesterol stones** — bile holding more cholesterol than bile salts and phospholipid can keep in solution (**supersaturation**), favoured by stasis and oestrogen. Cystic duct obstruction gives biliary colic, common bile duct obstruction gives **obstructive jaundice**, and ampullary impaction gives **gallstone pancreatitis** — blocked zymogen drainage, the mechanism above.

**Hepatitis** is hepatocyte inflammation from viral causes (**A, B, C, D, E** — [03 — Viruses](../07-microbiology/03-viruses.md)), alcohol, or toxins. **Paracetamol overdose** saturates the glucuronide and sulphate pathways, so CYP2E1 makes **NAPQI**, which binds hepatocyte proteins once glutathione is exhausted → centrilobular necrosis; **N-acetylcysteine** replenishes glutathione. Liver failure then shows the whole organ at once: coagulopathy (no clotting factors), jaundice (no bilirubin excretion), encephalopathy (no urea cycle → ammonia).

**Appendicitis** starts with **luminal obstruction** (faecolith or lymphoid hyperplasia) → trapped secretion → rising pressure → ischaemia → bacterial invasion → inflammation of the parietal peritoneum. The appendix has **somatic afferents**, unlike most of the gut, so its inflammation is felt as sharp, well-localised right iliac fossa pain; rupture spreads infection, hence time-critical appendicectomy.

**Inflammatory bowel disease** is chronic idiopathic inflammation in two forms: **Crohn's disease** — skip lesions, transmural, any region, granulomas, strictures and fistulae — versus **ulcerative colitis** — continuous, mucosal, confined to colon and rectum, crypt abscesses, bleeding. Both reflect failed immune regulation against commensal flora; continuity versus skip distribution on endoscopy is the examinable distinction.

**First-pass metabolism is why route changes dose.** An oral drug is absorbed into the **portal vein** and cleared by the liver before reaching the heart; the same drug intravenously bypasses it. Bioavailability therefore falls (morphine ~25%, nitroglycerin <1%, lidocaine so thoroughly destroyed orally that it is never given by mouth) — a defence against toxins and a liability for alcohol and paracetamol, which damage the cells doing the clearing.

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "The stomach digests itself" | It holds **pepsinogen, not pepsin**, and acid is confined by **mucus, epithelial bicarbonate, tight junctions, and 3–5-day turnover**. Ulcers follow *failure of these defences* (or NSAID loss of prostaglandin), not the mere presence of acid. |
| "Villi and microvilli are the same thing" | **Villi** are multicellular mucosal projections with a capillary loop and lacteal (×10 area); **microvilli** are single-cell membrane extensions carrying brush-border enzymes and transporters (×20). |
| "The oesophagus is keratinised and has villi" | It is **stratified squamous, non-keratinised**, built for abrasion. **Villi are exclusive to the small intestine.** |
| "Bile digests fat enzymatically" | Bile salts **emulsify** — a physical increase in surface area. Hydrolysis is done by **pancreatic lipase with colipase**. |
| "Fat is absorbed into blood capillaries" | Long-chain products become **chylomicrons in lacteals → lymph → thoracic duct**; only short- and medium-chain fatty acids enter the portal vein directly. |
| "The pancreas secretes active trypsin" | It secretes **trypsinogen**; activation requires **enterokinase on the duodenal brush border**. Premature activation inside the gland is **acute pancreatitis**. |
| "Glucose simply diffuses into gut cells" | Apical entry is **SGLT1, Na⁺-coupled secondary active transport**; **GLUT2** only exits basolaterally. Diffusion could never clear the lumen. |
| "Intrinsic factor comes from chief cells" | **Parietal cells** make intrinsic factor *and* HCl; **chief cells** make pepsinogen. No intrinsic factor → no B₁₂ → **megaloblastic anaemia**. |
| "Pepsin works at blood pH" | Pepsin has an **acid optimum near pH 1.5–2.0** and is denatured above pH 6 — switched off the moment chyme is neutralised. |
| "The large intestine digests fibre" | Humans have no enzyme for **β-1,4 glycosidic bonds**; **colonic bacteria** ferment fibre and the host absorbs the short-chain fatty acids. |
| "Lactose intolerance is an infection" | Unabsorbed lactose is **osmotically active** (water retained → diarrhoea) and is then fermented to gas and acid. No invasion occurs. |
| "Oral and intravenous doses are equivalent" | An oral drug enters the **portal vein** and is partly cleared before systemic circulation — **first-pass metabolism** lowers bioavailability. |

## Key facts

- Digestion is **hydrolysis outside cells** followed by **absorption across one epithelium** using section 03's transporters; the canal is one tube open at both ends, fed by accessory glands that never touch food.
- The gut wall repeats a **four-layer recipe** — mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa (inner circular / outer longitudinal), serosa — with the myenteric and submucosal plexuses inside it; the oesophagus is **stratified squamous, villus-free**.
- **Surface area: 0.5 m² → folds ×3 → villi ×10 → microvilli ×20 ≈ 200 m²** at a diffusion distance of ~40–50 µm — area up, distance down.
- **Mouth**: salivary **amylase → maltose**, **lingual lipase** continuing in the stomach; taste and chewing trigger the **cephalic phase** via the **vagus**.
- **Parietal cells**: **HCl** by **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase** (the PPI target) and **intrinsic factor** — deficiency gives **megaloblastic anaemia**. **Chief cells**: **pepsinogen → pepsin** at pH 1.5–2.0.
- The stomach survives itself via **mucus, bicarbonate, tight junctions, prostaglandins, and rapid turnover**; NSAIDs weaken those defences by blocking prostaglandin synthesis.
- **G cells → gastrin**, switched off by **D-cell somatostatin** when antral pH falls; retropulsion and the pylorus yield **chyme**, released in squirts matched to duodenal neutralising capacity.
- Carbohydrate absorption is **SGLT1 (Na⁺-linked) then GLUT2**, fructose via **GLUT5**; protein is absorbed mostly as **di/tripeptides via PepT1** plus Na⁺-linked amino acids — both routes active and saturable.
- Fat: **bile emulsification → lipase hydrolysis → micelle ferry → re-esterification → chylomicrons → lacteals → lymph → thoracic duct**; only short-chain fatty acids go straight to the portal vein.
- **Pancreas**: **enterokinase → trypsin → cascade** activating chymotrypsinogen, proelastase, and procarboxypeptidase; duct cells pour **HCO₃⁻** under **secretin**, which must neutralise acid before the enzymes work.
- **Liver**: bile (physical emulsification), plasma and clotting proteins, **urea cycle** (NH₃ → urea), glycogen storage, **P450** detoxification, **first-pass metabolism**; the **gallbladder** concentrates bile and contracts under **CCK**.
- The **large intestine** absorbs water and salt with **no human enzymatic digestion**; the **microbiome** supplies **short-chain fatty acids**, **vitamin K**, and **colonisation resistance**.
- **Peristalsis** obeys the **law of the intestine** (contraction behind, relaxation ahead); **segmentation** mixes without advancing; **gastrin, CCK, secretin, and GIP** couple secretion to food arrival.

## Practice questions

**1. A patient with a peptic ulcer is started on omeprazole. The drug works by**

A. Neutralising secreted HCl in the gastric lumen
B. Irreversibly inhibiting the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase on parietal cells
C. Blocking histamine receptors on ECL cells
D. Increasing somatostatin release from D cells

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Omeprazole acylates the luminal H⁺/K⁺-ATPase, the final common step of acid secretion, so output recovers only as new pump protein is made. H₂-receptor antagonists block histamine instead (C); antacids neutralise lumen acid without touching secretion (A).

**2. Pepsin is secreted as pepsinogen chiefly because**

A. The enzyme requires bile salts for activation
B. An inactive precursor prevents autodigestion of the chief cells producing it
C. Pepsinogen is more soluble in alkaline pancreatic juice
D. Pepsin cannot be synthesised on free ribosomes

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Zymogens are the standard solution wherever a protease is made by living cells — activated only after secretion into the compartment that needs it. Activation then occurs in the lumen by HCl and autocatalysis; solubility and ribosome type are irrelevant.

**3. Contraction behind a bolus and relaxation ahead of it is best identified as**

A. The migrating motor complex
B. The law of the intestine, mediated by the myenteric plexus
C. The CCK-mediated gallbladder reflex
D. The defecation reflex

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Luminal distension triggers an intrinsic reflex — ACh and substance P contract circular muscle upstream while VIP and nitric oxide relax it downstream. The migrating motor complex is the inter-digestive sweep; the defecation reflex is a spinal parasympathetic response to rectal stretch.

**4. Glucose absorption against its concentration gradient in the small intestine is driven by**

A. Direct hydrolysis of ATP by the glucose transporter
B. Facilitated diffusion through GLUT2 alone
C. The Na⁺ gradient maintained by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, with Na⁺ and glucose entering together via SGLT1
D. Endocytosis of glucose across the apical membrane

**Answer: C**

Explanation: SGLT1 couples sodium flowing down its gradient to glucose entry uphill — secondary active transport, with ATP spent earlier by the basolateral pump. GLUT2 is the basolateral exit route (B), and no transporter here hydrolyses ATP directly (A).

**5. Long-chain fatty acids absorbed in the jejunum first reach the systemic circulation via**

A. The hepatic portal vein, as free fatty acids
B. The pancreatic duct
C. The enterohepatic bile salt circulation
D. The thoracic duct, carried inside chylomicrons in lymph

**Answer: D**

Explanation: In the enterocyte they are re-esterified, packaged with apolipoprotein B-48 into chylomicrons, and exocytosed into the lacteal; lymph takes them to the thoracic duct and then the subclavian vein, so the liver sees only remnants. Only short- and medium-chain fatty acids take the portal route (A).

**6. A patient with autoimmune gastritis develops macrocytic anaemia. The missing link is**

A. Loss of pepsin causing protein malnutrition
B. Loss of parietal-cell intrinsic factor, so vitamin B₁₂ cannot be absorbed
C. Deficient salivary amylase reducing carbohydrate absorption
D. Failure of secretin to release pancreatic bicarbonate

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Antibodies against parietal cells destroy HCl and intrinsic factor; without it, B₁₂ is not absorbed in the ileum, thymidylate synthesis fails, and red-cell precursors fail to divide properly — megaloblastic anaemia. Acid loss alone does not cause anaemia, and neither amylase nor secretin handles B₁₂.

**7. The enzyme that initiates activation of pancreatic zymogens is**

A. Pepsin, acting in the stomach
B. Trypsin, secreted in active form by acinar cells
C. Enterokinase on the duodenal brush border
D. Bile salt-activated lipase

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Enterokinase exists only on the duodenal surface; it converts trypsinogen to trypsin, which then activates chymotrypsinogen, proelastase, and procarboxypeptidase in an amplifying cascade. The pancreas never secretes active trypsin (B) — premature activation is the mechanism of acute pancreatitis.

**8. The principal action of secretin released by S cells is to**

A. Contract the gallbladder
B. Stimulate pepsinogen release from chief cells
C. Increase gastric acid secretion
D. Stimulate duct cells to secrete HCO₃⁻-rich pancreatic juice

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Secretin is released when acid below pH 4.5 enters the duodenum, and its job is to make the pancreas pour bicarbonate so amylase and lipase can function — negative feedback terminating on luminal pH. Gallbladder contraction and enzyme-rich secretion are CCK's actions (A); secretin inhibits acid (C).

**9. Helicobacter pylori survives gastric acid primarily by producing**

A. A polysaccharide capsule that excludes H⁺
B. Urease, which converts urea to ammonia and raises the pH of its immediate surroundings
C. Bicarbonate that neutralises the entire stomach contents
D. Endospores that remain dormant at low pH

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Urease hydrolyses host urea to ammonia, which neutralises acid locally as it diffuses through mucus — constructing a survivable microenvironment rather than tolerating the original one. The organism has no such capsule (A), cannot buffer the lumen (C), and does not form spores (D).

**10. A patient develops bloating and osmotic diarrhoea two hours after drinking milk. The mechanism is**

A. Immune-mediated villous atrophy blocking all absorption
B. Bacterial invasion of the colonic mucosa
C. Absence of brush-border lactase, so unabsorbed lactose retains water and is fermented to gas and acid
D. Failure of bile secretion preventing fat digestion

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Without lactase, lactose stays in the lumen, holds water osmotically (diarrhoea), and is fermented to short-chain fatty acids and gas (bloating, cramps, delayed onset). Villous atrophy is coeliac disease (A), no invasion occurs (B), and the substrate is carbohydrate, not fat (D).
