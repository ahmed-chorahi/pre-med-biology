# Foundations and Biochemistry MCQs

50 questions covering [00 — Foundations](../00-foundations/INDEX.md) and [01 — Biochemistry](../01-biochemistry/INDEX.md). Read [the practice README](README.md) first for how to score these.

Answers and explanations follow each question. Stop at the horizontal rule before reading on.

## 1. Which statement best describes a living organism in terms of energy?

A. It acquires energy from its environment and uses it to maintain internal order
B. It does not require energy because it is self-sustaining
C. It converts matter into energy and back again repeatedly
D. It creates energy from nothing and releases it as heat

**Answer: A**

Explanation: A living thing is an open system. It takes in energy and matter, uses part of it to build and maintain internal order, and exports the rest as heat and waste. B violates the conservation of energy. C is false — metabolism requires continuous energy input. D describes nuclear processes, not biology.

## 2. A characteristic shared by all living organisms is

A. A cell wall containing cellulose
B. The ability to maintain a relatively stable internal environment
C. Sexual reproduction
D. Independent movement to obtain food

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Homeostasis is universal. Sexual reproduction is absent in sterile organisms and bacteria; a cellulose cell wall is a plant feature; independent movement excludes plants, fungi, and sessile organisms.

## 3. An evolutionary adaptation operates at which level?**

A. Within a single cell during mitosis
B. Within an organ during development
C. Within an individual organism during its lifetime
D. Within a population across generations

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Natural selection acts on individuals, but the response is a change in allele frequencies within a population across generations. An individual does not adapt genetically during its life — that is the Lamarckian error. The unit of evolution is the population.

## 4. Which correctly pairs a structure with its function?**

A. Nucleus — site of protein synthesis
B. Mitochondrion — cellular respiration
C. Golgi apparatus — DNA replication
D. Ribosome — ATP production

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Mitochondria carry out the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain. A is wrong because translation happens at ribosomes; the nucleus stores genetic material. C is wrong because the Golgi modifies and sorts proteins; replication occurs in the nucleus. D is inverted — ATP synthase in mitochondria produces ATP; replication occurs in the nucleus.

## 5. A property that emerges only when many cells are organised together, and not from any single cell, is called**

A. An emergent property
B. A theoretical property
C. A chemical property
D. A reductionist property

**Answer: A**

Explanation: An emergent property arises from interactions between components rather than from any component alone — coordinated pumping by the heart, for example. Reductionism is the opposite analytical approach, taking the whole apart. C and D are not the terms being described.

## 6. Which set contains only covalent bonds?**

A. The bond between two atoms in a water molecule, and the peptide bond between two amino acids
B. The hydrogen bonds holding DNA's two strands together, and the bonds within a phospholipid bilayer
C. The bond between sodium and chloride in table salt
D. The attraction between a proton and an acetate ion in solution

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Both are shared-electron bonds between nonmetals. C is ionic — electrons are transferred. B mixes non-covalent interactions with a covalent one; the hydrogen bonds between DNA strands and the interactions between lipids are non-covalent, while the bonds inside a phospholipid are covalent. D describes ion–dipole attraction in solution.

## 7. Water has a high specific heat. Which property of water accounts for this?**

A. The energy required to disrupt hydrogen bonding when water is heated
B. Its molecular bent shape
C. Its ability to dissolve ionic compounds
D. Its low molecular weight

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Much of the energy supplied to heat water goes into breaking hydrogen bonds rather than raising kinetic energy, so the temperature rises slowly. This is why large bodies of water change temperature slowly. C explains solvent power. B explains polarity, which is the underlying cause but not the specific mechanism for heat capacity.

## 8. Why is ice less dense than liquid water?**

A. Ice contains fewer oxygen atoms
B. Ice is colder, and cold substances are always denser
C. Hydrogen bonds force an open hexagonal lattice containing more empty space
D. Ice molecules are larger than liquid water molecules

**Answer: C**

Explanation: In ice each molecule forms four hydrogen bonds in a rigid open lattice, so the same mass occupies more volume. This is why ice floats and why lakes freeze from the surface down, insulating the water below. A, B, and D are not the mechanism.

## 9. Which statement about the hydrophobic effect is correct?**

A. Nonpolar molecules are repelled by hydrophobic covalent bonds
B. Water molecules repel nonpolar molecules
C. Nonpolar substances dissolve readily in water
D. Nonpolar molecules cannot form hydrogen bonds with water, so clustering them together minimises the disruption of surrounding water

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Water does not repel nonpolar molecules; it fails to interact favourably with them. Exposing nonpolar surfaces requires an ordered arrangement of water molecules, so clustering minimises the ordered region. This is why membranes self-assemble and proteins fold with hydrophobic side chains buried. A invents a bond type — there is no such thing as a hydrophobic covalent bond. C is the opposite of what happens.

## 10. A solution has a pH of 3. Compared with a solution at pH 5, its hydrogen ion concentration is**

A. Twice as high
B. One hundred times lower
C. Ten times higher
D. One hundred times higher

**Answer: D**

Explanation: The scale is logarithmic, so each unit is a tenfold change. Two units from pH 5 to pH 3 means $10^2 = 100$ times higher. This is why a one-unit difference is not a small difference.

## 11. At 25 °C, what is the relationship between $[\mathrm{H}^+]$ and $[\mathrm{OH}^-]$?**

A. Their product is $1.0 \times 10^{-7}$
B. Their sum is always 14
C. They are always equal
D. Their product is $1.0 \times 10^{-14}$

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Water's ionisation establishes a constant product, $[\mathrm{H}^+][\mathrm{OH}^-] = K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$. They are equal only in neutral water, at $10^{-7}$ each. So raising one necessarily lowers the other.

## 12. A buffer resists a change in pH because it contains**

A. A very large volume of water
B. Only hydrogen ions
C. A weak acid and its conjugate base in appreciable amounts
D. A strong acid and a strong base

**Answer: C**

Explanation: When acid is added the conjugate base consumes it; when base is added the weak acid consumes it. Either way the change is limited — but only until one component is depleted, after which the pH moves sharply. This is why a stressed buffer system eventually fails rather than protecting indefinitely.

## 13. Arterial blood pH falls from 7.40 to 7.32. What is the most direct consequence for oxygen delivery?**

A. Haemoglobin holds oxygen more tightly
B. Haemoglobin releases more oxygen to the tissues
C. Red blood cells become more rigid
D. Blood becomes more alkaline

**Answer: B**

Explanation: By the Bohr effect, a lower pH reduces haemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, increasing release at a given partial pressure. A is the effect of a *rise* in pH. C is not the mechanism here. D is false — a fall in pH is a move toward acidity.

## 14. What is the main functional difference between a prokaryotic and a eukaryotic cell?**

A. Eukaryotes compartmentalise functions inside membrane-bound organelles, including a membrane-bound nucleus
B. Prokaryotes lack DNA
C. Eukaryotes lack ribosomes
D. Prokaryotes have a nucleus; eukaryotes do not

**Answer: A**

Explanation: The membrane-bound nucleus and internal compartmentalisation are what define the eukaryotic plan. B is exactly reversed. C and D are false — both cell types contain DNA and ribosomes, and the difference in ribosome type is a consequence of the division, not an absence.

## 15. Bacterial cells generate ATP although they have no mitochondria. This occurs because**

A. Bacteria perform glycolysis only
B. Bacteria import ATP from their environment
C. The electron transport chain and ATP synthase are located in the bacterial plasma membrane
D. Bacterial ATP is produced in the nucleus

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Function is conserved even when location changes — bacteria need only a membrane capable of holding a proton gradient, and they have one. A is wrong: glycolysis yields far less ATP than respiration. B and D are false; bacteria have no nucleus and are net ATP producers.

## 16. Human mitochondrial ribosomes resemble bacterial ribosomes rather than human cytoplasmic ones. What does this most directly indicate?**

A. Mitochondria retain bacterial-type ribosomes, consistent with their origin as engulfed bacteria
B. Mitochondrial ribosomes do not function
C. Mitochondria synthesise all the cell's DNA
D. Human cells are partly prokaryotic

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Bacterial-type ribosomes, a double membrane, bacterial-type division, and their own DNA together support endosymbiotic origin. This is also why some antibiotics cause mitochondrial toxicity. D does not follow — a human remains a eukaryote whose organelles retain ancestral features.

## 17. Archaea differ from bacteria in that archaea**

A. Contain peptidoglycan in their cell walls
B. Have 80S ribosomes
C. Lack ribosomes entirely
D. Have ether-linked, branched membrane lipids that resist extreme conditions

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Ether linkages and branched isoprenoid tails make archaeal membranes resistant to hydrolysis, heat, pH, and salinity — which is why archaea occupy hot and highly saline environments. Their resistance to penicillin follows from having no peptidoglycan. B and C are false.

## 18. Why is the similarity between a bird's wing and a bat's wing not evidence of close evolutionary relationship?**

A. The two wings perform different functions
B. The wings are analogous structures that evolved independently, arising from different ancestral structures
C. Birds and bats are not closely related
D. Both wings contain bone

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Bat wings are skin stretched over elongated fingers; bird wings are modified forelimbs with feathers. Similar function from different origins is convergence, producing analogy. Shared function is not evidence of relatedness — that is the whole point of the homology distinction. C is a partial truth that does not establish homology, because the relevant structures differ.

## 19. A taxonomic group contains a common ancestor but excludes some of its descendants. It is**

A. Monophyletic by definition
B. A valid clade
C. Paraphyletic
D. Polyphyletic

**Answer: C**

Explanation: A valid clade must include an ancestor and *all* its descendants. Excluding some produces a paraphyletic group — a grouping created by human choice rather than a discovered evolutionary unit. Polyphyly is different again, grouping organisms from separate lineages without their most recent common ancestor.

## 20. Which of these is a nucleotide?**

A. A base plus a sugar, with no phosphate
B. A base alone
C. A base plus a pentose sugar, with a phosphate group
D. Two bases joined by hydrogen bonds

**Answer: C**

Explanation: A nucleotide is a base, a pentose sugar, and one or more phosphate groups. Base plus sugar without phosphate is a nucleoside. A base alone is a nucleobase. D is a base pair, not a nucleotide.

## 21. DNA differs from RNA in that DNA contains**

A. Uracil in place of cytosine
B. Ribose rather than deoxyribose
C. Thymine rather than uracil
D. A single strand rather than two

**Answer: C**

Explanation: DNA uses thymine, RNA uses uracil; the other three are reversed or wrong. DNA uses deoxyribose and is normally double-stranded. This substitution is not arbitrary — thymine is 5-methyluracil, so it can be distinguished from uracil, which arises by cytosine deamination.

## 22. What determines the direction in which a new DNA strand is synthesised?**

A. The concentration of dNTPs in the cell
B. The temperature of the reaction
C. The shape of the polymerase's active site
D. The availability of a free 3'-OH group

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Polymerisation joins a free 3'-OH to an incoming 5' phosphate, so a strand can only be extended at its 3' end. The chemistry fixes the direction, which is why the two strands of a replication fork are made in opposite physical directions. Concentration affects rate; the active site accommodates the chemistry rather than determining it.

## 23. Which contributes most to the overall stability of the DNA double helix?**

A. Hydrogen bonds between complementary bases
B. Covalent bonds within the sugar–phosphate backbone
C. Stacking of adjacent bases
D. Ionic bonds between phosphate groups

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Base stacking is the dominant stabilising factor, because packing hydrophobic base rings together reduces their exposure to water — the same effect that drives protein folding. Hydrogen bonds confer *specificity* and allow reversible separation, which is a different and equally important property. B holds each strand together, not the two strands together.

---

## 24. A protein's tertiary structure is disrupted by heating, but its peptide bonds remain intact. This is**

A. Denaturation
B. Misfolding
C. Hydrolysis
D. Proteolysis

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Denaturation destroys tertiary and secondary structure while leaving the primary structure intact, which is why it is usually reversible. Proteolysis and hydrolysis both break peptide bonds and are not reversible. Misfolding is a distinct process: the chain adopts an incorrect conformation despite a normal sequence.

---

## 25. A protein's amino acid sequence is normal but the folded protein is non-functional and aggregates. This is best described as**

A. Denaturation
B. Proteolysis
C. Hydrolysis
D. Misfolding

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Misfolding is a failure of folding, in which a chemically normal protein adopts an incorrect conformation — a distinct process from denaturation, and a mechanism behind several neurodegenerative conditions. Denaturation involves disruption of existing structure by a physical or chemical agent; proteolysis and hydrolysis involve breaking bonds.

## 26. What stabilises an α-helix?**

A. Disulfide bonds between cysteines
B. Hydrogen bonds between amino acid side chains
C. Ionic bonds between charged side chains
D. Hydrogen bonds between backbone C=O and N–H groups four residues apart

**Answer: D**

Explanation: α-helix backbone hydrogen bonds run from residue $i$ to residue $i+4$, giving about 3.6 residues per turn. B is wrong because secondary structure uses backbone groups, never side chains. A and C are tertiary structure interactions, contributing to overall fold rather than to helix formation.

## 27. Why are nonpolar amino acids usually found in the interior of a globular protein?**

A. Clustering them in the interior minimises the disruption of surrounding water
B. They form covalent bonds to the backbone
C. They carry charges that are neutralised inside the protein
D. They are heavier, so they sink

**Answer: A**

Explanation: This is the hydrophobic effect, the major driving force of folding. Nonpolar groups cannot hydrogen-bond with water, so exposing them requires an energetically unfavourable ordered arrangement of water. Mass, covalent bonding, and charge do not explain the distribution.

## 28. Haemoglobin's sigmoidal oxygen dissociation curve results from**

A. Cooperativity, where binding of one oxygen increases the affinity of the remaining subunits
B. Its binding carbon dioxide
C. Its containing four heme groups rather than one
D. Its being located inside red blood cells

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Positive cooperativity between the four subunits makes loading efficient at high oxygen partial pressure in the lungs and unloading efficient at the lower pressure in tissues. Myoglobin, which is a monomer and lacks cooperativity, has a hyperbolic curve. C describes capacity — more haem groups raises total capacity, not the shape of the relationship.

## 29. Fetal haemoglobin can obtain oxygen from maternal blood across the placenta because**

A. It has a higher oxygen affinity, owing to weaker binding of 2,3-BPG
B. It contains more heme groups
C. It carries more carbon dioxide
D. It is found only in the fetus

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Fetal haemoglobin binds 2,3-BPG less strongly, which shifts its dissociation curve to the left and raises its oxygen affinity, allowing it to take oxygen from maternal blood. This is a clinical consequence of the same allosteric regulation described in enzymes. B, C, and D are not the mechanism.

## 30. In sickle cell disease, the sequence of events from mutation to symptoms is**

A. Gene → tissue → gene → cell
B. Organ → tissue → cell → gene
C. Gene → amino acid → protein assembly → cell shape → vessel occlusion
D. Cell → protein → gene → tissue

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The causal chain runs from the molecular level upward: a base substitution changes an amino acid, which creates a hydrophobic patch on haemoglobin, which permits polymerisation, which distorts the red cell, which blocks capillaries. Each step is a different level of organisation, which is why a single-base change can produce a clinical disease.

## 31. Collagen provides tensile strength because**

A. Glycine's bulky side chain packs the chains closely
B. Its tightly packed chains are linked by many hydrogen bonds acting in parallel
C. It is rich in charged residues
D. It contains extensive disulfide cross-linking

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Collagen's chains pack tightly and are held by many hydrogen bonds running in parallel along the fibre axis, so individually weak bonds sum to high tensile strength along that axis. A is wrong because glycine has the *smallest* side chain of all, which is what allows the tight packing. C and D are not the basis of its properties.

## 32. What is the difference between a nucleoside and a nucleotide?**

A. A nucleoside contains only a base
B. There is no difference; the terms are interchangeable
C. A nucleoside is base plus sugar; a nucleotide adds phosphate
D. A nucleoside contains a phosphate group; a nucleotide does not

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Nucleoside = base + sugar. Nucleotide = base + sugar + phosphate. The distinction is functional: a nucleoside cannot be incorporated into a nucleic acid chain without phosphorylation.

## 33. Which statement correctly describes rRNA?**

A. It is an intermediate copy of a gene
B. It forms the structural and catalytic core of the ribosome and performs peptide bond formation
C. It is composed of protein only
D. It delivers the correct amino acid to the ribosome

**Answer: B**

Explanation: rRNA makes up the bulk of the ribosome and carries out the peptidyl transferase reaction — which means rRNA is a ribozyme, and this is key evidence that RNA preceded DNA in evolution. A describes mRNA, D describes tRNA, and C is wrong because ribosomes contain rRNA as well as protein.

## 34. An enzyme reduces the time required for a reaction to reach equilibrium. It does not**

A. Lower activation energy
B. Increase reaction rate below the activation barrier
C. Act as a catalyst
D. Change the free-energy change or equilibrium position of the reaction

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Catalysts lower activation energy and speed up both forward and reverse reactions equally, so the equilibrium position and ΔG are unchanged. An enzyme cannot drive an unfavourable reaction forward. This is why enzyme failure produces slow reactions and metabolic accumulation rather than a change in thermodynamics.

## 35. A competitive inhibitor and the substrate can occupy the same site. Which consequence follows?**

A. Vmax is unchanged and apparent Km increases
B. Both Vmax and Km increase
C. Vmax falls and Km is unchanged
D. Neither Vmax nor Km is affected

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Because inhibition can be overcome by raising substrate concentration, the enzyme's maximum capacity is intact, so Vmax is unchanged; but more substrate is needed to reach half that maximum, so apparent Km rises. This is the pattern for non-competitive inhibition to contrast against — there, Vmax falls and cannot be recovered.

---

## 36. A non-competitive inhibitor binds at an allosteric site. Why does increasing substrate concentration fail to overcome it?**

A. The inhibitor blocks substrate from entering the cell
B. The inhibitor occupies the active site
C. Substrate becomes denatured
D. The inhibitor has already distorted the active site's shape, and substrate cannot dislodge it by competing

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Because the inhibitor binds elsewhere and changes the conformation of the active site, more substrate does not help. This is why Vmax falls. The distinction from competitive inhibition is exactly this: competitive inhibition can be outcompeted because the inhibitor occupies the site substrate needs.

## 37. Why are pancreatic proteases secreted as inactive precursors?**

A. Because the pancreas cannot synthesise active enzymes
B. Because active proteases would digest the tissue that produces them
C. Because activation requires the removal of an amino acid at the N-terminus only
D. Because inactive proteins travel more easily in blood

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Zymogen activation is a safety mechanism. Proteases are made inactive and switched on only in the duodenum. Activation within the pancreas — the mechanism of acute pancreatitis — causes the organ to digest itself. C and D are incorrect.

## 38. A patient is deficient in a vitamin required to make NAD⁺. Which effect is most likely?**

A. Only enzymes using that one coenzyme are affected
B. Protein synthesis stops immediately
C. Multiple dehydrogenases across several pathways are impaired, because the coenzyme is shared
D. ATP synthesis in mitochondria is unaffected

**Answer: C**

Explanation: NAD⁺ is used by many dehydrogenases across carbohydrate, lipid, and amino acid metabolism, so its deficiency impairs many reactions at once — which is why a single vitamin deficiency produces multi-system failure. A is the opposite of the point. B is wrong because NAD⁺ is a coenzyme, not a substrate for translation. D is wrong because mitochondrial NADH oxidation feeds the electron transport chain directly.

## 39. Which enzyme inhibition is irreversible?**

A. Competitive inhibition by a molecule resembling the substrate
B. Acetylation of cyclooxygenase by aspirin
C. Allosteric inhibition by a regulatory molecule
D. Feedback inhibition by the end product of a pathway

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Aspirin transfers an acetyl group to a serine residue in cyclooxygenase, forming a stable covalent bond that cannot be hydrolysed — so the inhibition is effectively irreversible for the enzyme's lifetime, and new enzyme must be synthesised. Competitive, allosteric, and feedback inhibition are all reversible, and all are removed by increasing substrate or by displacing the inhibitor.

## 40. Glycogen is more highly branched than starch. What is the functional advantage?**

A. Greater solubility in water
B. The ability to bind more water molecules
C. Higher energy content per gram
D. More chain ends, so more glucose can be released simultaneously when needed

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Enzymes act at chain ends. Glycogen's many branches provide hundreds of ends, so many residues can be liberated at once — essential when blood glucose must be corrected within minutes. The residues are identical glucose either way, so energy per gram is unchanged, and branching has nothing to do with solubility or water binding.

## 41. Why is cellulose indigestible to humans?**

A. Humans lack β(1→4) glycosidase, the enzyme that cleaves its glycosidic bonds
B. It is made of a monosaccharide humans cannot make
C. It is insoluble and cannot reach digestive enzymes
D. It is broken down by stomach acid before reaching the intestine

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Cellulose's glucose units are joined by β(1→4) bonds; human enzymes cleave α bonds. Ruminants host cellulase-producing bacteria in the rumen. C is not the reason — insolubility slows digestion but does not prevent it, as starch is also largely insoluble. B and D are incorrect.

## 42. A disaccharide is formed from two monosaccharides by dehydration synthesis. What happens in the reverse reaction?**

A. A water molecule is consumed to break the glycosidic bond
B. Nothing; the reaction is not reversible
C. A phosphate group is required
D. A water molecule is released

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Hydrolysis consumes water — the OH from one monosaccharide and an H from the other — to split the bond. This is exactly what digestion does. It is the reverse of condensation, and the reason hydrolysis reactions require water as a reactant.

## 43. Which statement about saturated fatty acids is correct?**

A. They contain at least one carbon–carbon double bond
B. They contain no carbon–carbon double bonds, so their chains pack tightly
C. They have a kink at each carbon
D. They are water-soluble

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Saturated means every carbon carries the maximum possible number of hydrogens, which requires no C=C bonds. Tighter packing raises the melting point, which is why saturated fats are typically solid at room temperature. A, C, and D describe unsaturated fatty acids, or are simply wrong.

## 44. A cis double bond lowers a fatty acid's melting point because**

A. It introduces polar groups into the chain
B. It shortens the chain
C. The kink it introduces prevents neighbouring tails from packing closely, reducing dispersion interactions
D. It weakens the covalent bonds along the chain

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Dispersion interactions between well-packed tails are the main attractive force in a lipid layer, and a cis double bond imposes a permanent bend that prevents alignment. Fewer contacts means a lower melting point. Covalent bond strength is not meaningfully changed, no polar groups are added, and no carbons are removed.

## 45. Phospholipids form a bilayer because**

A. They form covalent bonds between adjacent molecules
B. Their polar heads repel water and their tails attract it
C. They are soluble in water
D. Their hydrophobic tails cluster away from water while their polar heads remain in contact with it

**Answer: D**

Explanation: This is the hydrophobic effect, and it produces a structure that is both stable and fluid. The bilayer is not covalently cross-linked, which is what allows lipids to move within it — necessary for membrane function.

## 46. Cholesterol's effect on membranes is best described as**

A. Increasing fluidity at all temperatures
B. Forming covalent bonds with phospholipids
C. Buffering fluidity, reducing it when it is too high and preventing rigidification when it is too low
D. Forming the bilayer's basic structure

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Cholesterol's rigid four-ring structure lacks the flexible tails of a fatty acid, so it inserts between them and restrains movement. It reduces excessive fluidity at high temperature and prevents tight packing at low temperature. Phospholipids, not cholesterol, form the basic bilayer structure.

## 47. Which explains why some antibiotics inhibit human mitochondrial protein synthesis?**

A. Antibiotics enter mitochondria more readily than the cytoplasm
B. Human ribosomes are 80S in the cytoplasm but mitochondria contain ribosomes of bacterial type
C. Human mitochondria use RNA instead of DNA
D. Mitochondria contain no ribosomes

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Mitochondria retain bacterial-type 70S ribosomes as a consequence of endosymbiotic origin, so agents that act on bacterial ribosomes can act on them too. This explains mitochondrial toxicity in high-dose or prolonged antibiotic use. A, C, and D are false.

## 48. Lipoproteins are named according to**

A. The number of triglycerides they carry
B. Their density, which reflects the protein-to-lipid ratio
C. Their diameter
D. The organ that produces them

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Density tracks composition: HDL is protein-rich and most dense, while LDL and chylomicrons are lipid-rich and less dense. This is why "low density" and "high density" correlate oppositely with cardiovascular risk. Organ of origin correlates with composition but is not the naming criterion.

## 49. A group of organisms has identical anatomy but lives in a completely different environment and is not closely related. This is best explained by**

A. Horizontal gene transfer
B. Convergent evolution producing similar structures under similar selective pressures
C. Divergent evolution from a recent common ancestor
D. Genetic drift

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Similar solutions to similar problems arising independently under similar selective pressures produce similar structures without close relatedness. That pattern is the evidence that similarity alone does not indicate ancestry. Divergent evolution produces difference from a common ancestor, which is the opposite pattern.

---

## 50. The most accurate general statement about an enzyme is that it**

A. Increases the equilibrium yield of a product
B. Lowers activation energy, accelerating a reaction without changing the equilibrium position
C. Makes an energetically unfavourable reaction proceed
D. Is consumed during the reaction and must be regenerated

**Answer: B**

Explanation: An enzyme accelerates a reaction by lowering the barrier; it speeds the forward and reverse reactions equally, so the equilibrium position is unchanged, and it is returned unchanged after each cycle. A and C are thermodynamically impossible, and D describes consumption, which is the opposite of catalysis.
