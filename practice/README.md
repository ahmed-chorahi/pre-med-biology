## How to use this

**Do not read the explanation until you have committed to an answer.** Reading ahead converts a test into a read, and the point of the exercise is to find out what you actually recall.

**Score honestly and by topic.** A total score is much less useful than knowing *which* questions you missed. Those are either a knowledge gap — go back and read that chapter — or a confusion, which is a different kind of problem. If a distractor looked plausible, you have a specific misconception, and it is worth finding.

**Distinguish three kinds of wrong answer.**

| Kind | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do not know the fact | Gap in the material | Read the chapter again |
| Knew it but chose the distractor | Misconception, not a gap | Read [Common confusions](common-confusions.md) for that topic |
| Understood the question but reasoned wrongly | Reasoning practice needed | Redo the question from scratch without looking |

The third is the one that separates exam readiness from content knowledge.

**Come back later.** Spaced repetition beats massed review. A bank you have completed once is worth revisiting after a week, then a month. Questions you missed the first time are worth revisiting more often than the rest.

**Do these without notes after you finish the matching section.** If you are looking things up, you are reading, not testing — and the retrieval effort is what produces the memory.

## What these questions test

The questions are not written to reward sentence memorization. They mostly ask for one of five things:

| Type | What it requires |
| --- | --- |
| **Recall** | Knowing a term, a structure, or a location |
| **Conceptual** | Understanding why something is the way it is |
| **Comparison** | Holding two things apart correctly |
| **Application** | Applying a mechanism to an unfamiliar situation |
| **Reasoning** | Working out a consequence from a principle |

Application and reasoning are where the marks are, and where most candidates lose them. The pattern in this bank is deliberate: each concept is tested once by recall and then again by application, so that recognising a fact and reasoning from it are assessed separately.

## A note on question style

Every question here should have exactly one correct answer. Where an option is *nearly* right, the explanation says why it is not — that is often the more useful part of the item. If you find a question that seems to have two defensible answers, that is worth reporting as a flaw in the question rather than as your own uncertainty.
