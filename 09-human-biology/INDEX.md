# 09 — Human Biology

**The four building materials of every organ, the organs themselves, and the control loops that hold them together.** This section is the hinge of the curriculum. It takes cell biology and cellular processes and asks what happens when similar cells band together to do a job — and it establishes the two ideas that every system chapter in section 10 depends on: that an organ is a recurring recipe of four tissues, and that every physiological variable is defended by a feedback loop running receptor → control centre → effector → response. Learn those two templates here and anatomy becomes mechanism rather than memorisation.

**Prerequisites:** [00 — Foundations](../00-foundations/), where [homeostasis](../00-foundations/09-homeostasis.md) is introduced as a concept and [biological organization](../00-foundations/02-biological-organization.md) sets the levels; [02 — Cell Biology](../02-cell-biology/), because a tissue is cells plus their junctions and matrix; and [03 — Cellular Processes](../03-cellular-processes/), where the transport and energy mechanisms that tissues use are already established.

**What this section gives you that an anatomy atlas does not:** why a squamous epithelium is one cell thick wherever exchange happens and stratified wherever friction happens; why blood, cartilage, and bone are one tissue type under three different matrix recipes; how to read any clinical statement as a failed feedback loop (diabetes is the glucose loop, hypertension is the pressure loop, fever is a set point that has been moved); and the component vocabulary — receptor, afferent, efferent, effector — that section 10 reuses in every system chapter.
