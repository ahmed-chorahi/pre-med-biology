# The Four Tissue Types

## Why it matters

Every organ you will ever be examined on is built from four materials: **epithelial tissue**, **connective tissue**, **muscle tissue**, and **nervous tissue**. A heart is cardiac muscle wrapped in epithelium, held together by connective tissue, wired by nervous tissue, and supplied by blood — itself connective tissue. Anatomy stops being memorisation once tissue logic is in place.

```
cover a surface, selective barrier, exchange  → EPITHELIUM
fill space, bear load, transport, scaffold    → CONNECTIVE TISSUE
shorten on command, generate force            → MUSCLE
sense, decide, signal at speed                → NERVOUS TISSUE

four tissues arranged differently → ORGAN → ORGAN SYSTEM → HOMEOSTASIS
```

Two ideas make the rest derivable. **Structure predicts function**: one cell thick because something must cross fast; fifty thick because something must survive being scraped. And the four differ in **cell versus extracellular material** — packed epithelial cells versus sparse connective cells in matrix they made themselves, which is why epithelium is fed from below and connective-tissue diseases are diseases of *matrix*.

Vocabulary for [02 — Organs and organ systems](02-organs-and-organ-systems.md), [03 — Homeostasis control loop](03-homeostasis-control-loop.md), [10 — Anatomy and Physiology](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/); organisation levels in [02 — Biological organization](../00-foundations/02-biological-organization.md), cells in [01 — Cell theory and cell types](../02-cell-biology/01-cell-theory-and-cell-types.md).

## Epithelial tissue

An **epithelium** is a continuous sheet of tightly packed cells covering a surface, lining a cavity, or forming a gland. Three properties follow: **polarised** (two different faces), **avascular** (no vessels inside), **highly mitotic** (replaced from below).

### Classification: layers × cell shape

Two questions classify every epithelium: **how many layers**, and **what shape are the cells touching the free surface**.

| Layers | Squamous (flat) | Cuboidal (cube) | Columnar (tall) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple** (one layer) | Shortest diffusion path: **alveoli**, capillaries, Bowman's capsule | Secretion/absorption round a lumen: **kidney tubules**, ducts | Absorption/secretion: **gut**, gall bladder |
| **Stratified** (many) | Sacrificial layers resist wear: **skin**, oesophagus, vagina | Rare — small ducts | Rare — male urethra |
| **Pseudostratified** (one layer, looks many) | — | — | **Trachea and airway**: ciliated, goblet cells |
| **Transitional** | **Bladder**: cells round up when full, flatten when empty | — | — |

**Pseudostratified** misleads: every cell sits on the basement membrane, but only some reach the lumen, so nuclei sit at different heights. The rule: **thin where substances must cross, thick where surfaces are rubbed, tall and busy where secretion or absorption occurs** — tall cells hold mitochondria and ER to **pump and secrete**; stratified cells are sacrificial.

### Polarity and apical specialisations

The **apical** face meets the lumen, the **basal** face meets the vascular connective tissue, the **lateral** faces touch neighbours. This **polarity** is maintained by tight junctions fencing apical transporters away from basal ones ([02 — Plasma membrane and nucleus](../02-cell-biology/02-plasma-membrane-and-nucleus.md)).

| Apical specialisation | Built from | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Microvilli** | **Actin** bundles cross-linked to the cortex | **Absorption**: ~20-fold surface area, folding only ([05 — Structure and movement](../02-cell-biology/05-structure-and-movement.md)) |
| **Stereocilia** | Very long microvilli | Absorption, mechanosensation — inner ear |
| **Cilia** | **9+2 axoneme**, **dynein** arms | **Move mucus or fluid** past a stationary cell — sliding becomes bending ([05 — Structure and movement](../02-cell-biology/05-structure-and-movement.md)) |
| **Keratin fill** | Intermediate filaments | Resist abrasion — cells flatten and die at the surface |

**Microvilli move nothing; cilia move material** — with goblet cells, tracheal cilia form the **mucociliary escalator**.

### The basement membrane, avascularity, and the mitotic engine

The **basement membrane** is secreted by the epithelium itself (**basal lamina**: type IV collagen, laminin) plus the connective tissue (**reticular lamina**). It anchors, filters, separates the two compartments — and is the boundary a cancer must breach to become invasive.

```
capillary in connective tissue BELOW
        ↓ diffusion (O2, glucose, amino acids) across the basement membrane
BASAL surface → cytoplasm → APICAL surface; wastes leave in reverse
        ↓ NO vessel ever enters the sheet → it must be THIN, fed from BELOW
surface cells shed → basal stem cells DIVIDE (high mitotic rate)
→ daughters pushed up, differentiate, then are lost at the surface
```

So epithelia must be **thin where exchange matters** (simple squamous alveoli), the **lamina propria** beneath must be **vascular** (cut the supply below and the epithelium above dies), and constant renewal is why epithelium heals by **regeneration** while muscle and nervous tissue do not — checkpoints for such division are covered in 03 — Cellular Processes.

### Cell junctions: how a sheet behaves as one barrier

A sheet is only a barrier if its cells are sealed together (inventory in [02 — Plasma membrane and nucleus](../02-cell-biology/02-plasma-membrane-and-nucleus.md), filaments in [05 — Structure and movement](../02-cell-biology/05-structure-and-movement.md)):

| Junction | Proteins | Cytoskeleton | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tight junction** | Claudins, occludin | Actin ring | **Seals** the space between cells; fences apical from basal proteins |
| **Adherens junction** | Cadherins | **Actin** belt | Ties the sheet together so it moves as one |
| **Desmosome** | Desmoglein, desmocollin | **Intermediate filaments** (keratin) | Spot welds resisting **shear** (failure: pemphigus) |
| **Hemidesmosome** | Integrins | **Intermediate filaments** | Anchors the cell to the **basement membrane**, not a neighbour |
| **Gap junction** | Connexins | — | Ion channels → shared electrical state (failure: arrhythmia) |

**Tight junctions seal, desmosomes resist shear, hemidesmosomes anchor to matrix, gap junctions couple.** Intermediate filaments are rope-like and motor-free, right for tension — why keratin mutations blister skin from trivial friction.

### Glandular epithelia: exocrine versus endocrine

A cluster of secretory epithelial cells is a **gland**; the two kinds differ only in plumbing:

| Feature | **Exocrine** | **Endocrine** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duct | **Yes** — secretion carried to a surface | **No** — ductless |
| Destination | Skin, gut, airway, vagina | **Blood capillaries** in the surrounding connective tissue |
| Product | Enzymes, mucus, sweat, sebum, milk — bulk, local | Hormones — tiny amounts, broadcast, acting only where receptors exist |

Exocrine secretion splits three ways by how much cell is sacrificed: **merocrine** (exocytosis, cell intact), **apocrine** (apical cytoplasm pinched off), **holocrine** (the whole cell ruptures — **sebaceous glands**, hence lipid-rich sebum). Feedback control is [03 — Homeostasis control loop](03-homeostasis-control-loop.md); the pancreas carries both gland types.

## Connective tissue

Connective tissue is the **odd one out**: elsewhere the cells *are* the tissue; here they are a minority and the material around them does the work — one class, many matrices: blood, bone, fat, cartilage, tendon.

### The design principle: cells are the minority, matrix does the job

**Connective tissue is defined by abundant extracellular matrix (ECM) that the resident cells secreted themselves**, and that material's composition sets the mechanical properties:

```
resident cell (fibroblast, osteoblast, chondrocyte, adipocyte …)
        ↓ synthesises protein + ground substance OUTWARD
EXTRACELLULAR MATRIX — fibres + ground substance
        ↓ composition sets mechanical properties
collagen in parallel → tendon (pull, ONE direction)
mineralised osteoid  → bone   (rigid, resists compression)
proteoglycan gel     → cartilage (water as shock absorber)
liquid plasma        → blood  (transports cells and solutes)
```

Since the **matrix rather than the cell** carries the function, connective-tissue diseases are usually matrix-protein defects — collagen in osteogenesis imperfecta, elastin in cutis laxa ([03 — Proteins](../01-biochemistry/03-proteins.md)).

### Fibres and ground substance

| Fibre | Molecule | Property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Collagen** | Type I (tendon, bone, skin), II (cartilage), III (reticular), IV (basement membrane) | Enormous **tensile strength** with little stretch — tendon, dermis, scar |
| **Elastin** | Cross-linked elastin + fibrillin | Stretch and **recoil** — artery walls, lung, skin |
| **Reticular** | Type III collagen, fine branches | Soft **internal scaffolding** — lymph nodes, spleen, liver |

**Ground substance** is a watery gel of **glycosaminoglycans** (hyaluronic acid) and **proteoglycans** binding water avidly: it **resists compression** — water squeezed from the gel is a hydraulic shock absorber, so cartilage and discs are pads — and is the **permeable medium** that nourishes avascular tissues.

### The types and what each matrix achieves

| Type | Resident cells | Fibres | What the matrix achieves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Loose (areolar)** | Fibroblasts, macrophages, mast cells, adipocytes, white cells | All three, loose | Packing; immune patrol; the vascular **lamina propria** feeding every epithelium |
| **Adipose** | **Adipocytes** — white (one droplet), brown (many) | Sparse | Storage, **cushioning**, insulation, endocrine signalling |
| **Dense regular** | Fibroblasts (tenocytes) | **Parallel collagen rows** | **Unidirectional** pull: **tendon**, **ligament** |
| **Dense irregular** | Fibroblasts | Bundles in **many directions** | Tension **in all directions**: **dermis**, organ capsules |
| **Cartilage — hyaline** | Chondrocytes in lacunae | Fine type II collagen | Smooth low-friction bearing: joints, trachea, foetal skeleton |
| **Cartilage — elastic** | Chondrocytes | **Abundant elastin** | Bends and springs back: **ear**, epiglottis |
| **Cartilage — fibro** | Chondrocytes in rows | Heavy type I collagen | **Compression and tension**: discs, menisci |
| **Bone** | Osteoblasts, osteocytes, osteoclasts | Collagen I in layers | **Mineralised osteoid**: hardness plus tensile strength; marrow houses blood cells |
| **Blood** | Erythrocytes, leukocytes, platelets | None | **Liquid matrix (plasma)**: transport, immunity, clotting |
| **Lymph** | Lymphocytes, macrophages | None | Immune surveillance; fluid return |
| **Reticular** | Reticular cells | Type III collagen | Delicate frame of **lymphoid organs** |

**Tension resistance** follows fibre *orientation* — parallel one axis, crossed every axis. **Cushioning** comes from adipose cells or proteoglycan water. **Hardness** comes from mineralisation, which only bone does.

Blood is a connective tissue once the definition is applied literally: scattered cells in a matrix they do not occupy (plasma). **Vascularity follows matrix density** — a dense matrix blocks capillary ingrowth, so cartilage is **avascular** and fed from the perichondrium (hence poor healing) while bone and areolar tissue are vascular: the same constraint governs epithelium ([02 — Passive transport](../03-cellular-processes/02-passive-transport.md)).

## Muscle tissue

Muscle tissue is built to **shorten**; all three kinds share one molecular engine, switched on and off differently.

### The shared motor: actin–myosin sliding

**Skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle all contract by the sliding-filament mechanism**: myosin heads walk along actin, pulling filaments past each other without either filament changing length (machinery: [05 — Structure and movement](../02-cell-biology/05-structure-and-movement.md); ATP: [04 — ATP and metabolism](../03-cellular-processes/04-atp-and-metabolism.md)):

```
nerve impulse (or pacemaker depolarisation)
        ↓ Ca2+ released into the cytoplasm (SR and/or outside the cell)
Ca2+ binds the regulatory switch → myosin-binding sites on ACTIN exposed
        ↓ myosin binds actin → POWER STROKE (ADP + Pi released) → filament slides
ATP binds myosin → head DETACHES → re-cocked (ATPase) → repeat
        ↓ contractile unit shortens → whole cell contracts
```

**ATP is needed both to swing the head and to release it**, so a cell without ATP cannot let go — the basis of **rigor mortis**. The nervous system supplies only the *signal* and Ca²⁺ release; **the energy is ATP hydrolysed by myosin itself**. Skeletal and cardiac muscle are **striated** because sarcomeres sit in register and shorten in synchrony; smooth muscle holds the same proteins in **dense bodies** across a lattice — no banding, slower and greater shortening, cheap to hold.

### Three regulators, three tissues

| Feature | **Skeletal** | **Cardiac** | **Smooth** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shape | Long fibres, **multinucleated, nuclei peripheral** | **Branched**, 1–2 central nuclei | **Spindle-shaped**, one central nucleus |
| Striations | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** |
| Control | **Voluntary** — somatic motor neuron, ACh at the neuromuscular junction | **Involuntary** — autorhythmic pacemaker, modulated by autonomic nerves | **Involuntary** — autonomic nerves, hormones, local stretch |
| Ca²⁺ and switch | SR via T-tubule action potential; **troponin–tropomyosin** | SR **plus** extracellular Ca²⁺ (L-type channels); **troponin–tropomyosin** | Extracellular entry plus SR; **calmodulin → MLCK** (no troponin) |
| Coupling | None — each fibre independently innervated | **Intercalated discs**: gap junctions (electrical) + desmosomes (mechanical) | Gap junctions in many sheets |
| Fatigue | Relatively **fatigable** (glycolytic bursts) | **Fatigue-resistant** — aerobic | **Fatigue-resistant** — **latch bridges** |
| Special | Fastest, strongest; multinucleated from fused embryonic cells | **Refractory period prevents tetanus** → one-unit contraction | Vessel/airway tone, peristalsis, sphincters, uterus — hours of contraction cheaply |

- **Skeletal**: large, fast, *chosen* forces → voluntary, multinucleated (a long cell needs local transcription), peripheral nuclei leaving the contractile apparatus clear.
- **Cardiac**: never fatigue, never tetanise, squeeze as one → **intercalated discs** give the mechanical weld (desmosomes) and electrical shortcut (gap junctions); one gap-junction defect can cause arrhythmia.
- **Smooth**: hold tone for hours → no sarcomeres, plus **latch bridges** (dephosphorylated myosin still bound to actin) holding force with almost no ATP.

## Nervous tissue

Nervous tissue **senses, integrates, and commands at speed and over distance**, point to point: excitable **neurons** carry signals, supportive **neuroglia** make signalling possible.

### The neuron

| Part | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dendrites** | Branched extensions studded with synapses | **Receive** input as **graded potentials** (proportional, decremental) |
| **Cell body (soma)** | Nucleus, Nissl bodies (rough ER), mitochondria | Metabolic centre; **integrates** summed input |
| **Axon** | Usually **one**, long, from the hillock | Carries the **action potential** away — all-or-none, non-decremental |
| **Myelin sheath** | **Schwann cells** (one segment per cell) in the PNS; **oligodendrocytes** (one cell, many segments) in the CNS | Insulation → **saltatory conduction** between **nodes of Ranvier**: faster, cheaper in ATP |
| **Terminals** | Boutons of vesicles and mitochondria | Release **neurotransmitter** when the impulse arrives |

### Neuroglia

| Glial cell | Location | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Astrocyte** | CNS | End-feet wrap capillaries → **blood–brain barrier**; buffer K⁺; clear transmitter; after injury form a **glial scar** |
| **Oligodendrocyte** | CNS | Forms **myelin** — one cell myelinates several axons |
| **Schwann cell** | PNS | Forms **myelin** for one segment; guides a **regenerating** axon |
| **Microglia** | CNS | Resident **phagocyte**; drives neuroinflammation |
| **Ependymal** | CNS | Lines ventricles; helps produce and circulate **cerebrospinal fluid** |

**Demyelinating diseases slow conduction** (multiple sclerosis in the CNS, Guillain–Barré in the PNS); a glioma is a tumour of support cells, not neurons.

### Excitability, conduction, and the synapse

Everything nervous tissue does rests on an ion gradient the cell spends ATP to maintain ([02 — Passive transport](../03-cellular-processes/02-passive-transport.md), [02 — Plasma membrane and nucleus](../02-cell-biology/02-plasma-membrane-and-nucleus.md)):

```
Na+/K+ ATPase pumps 3 Na+ OUT, 2 K+ IN → steep gradients maintained
        ↓ resting potential ≈ −70 mV (K+ leak dominates → inside negative)
stimulus opens voltage-gated Na+ → depolarisation → THRESHOLD
        ↓ POSITIVE FEEDBACK → action potential
local current depolarises the next patch → propagates WITHOUT fading
        ↓ myelinated axons: only the NODES exposed → SALTATORY conduction
terminal → Ca2+ enters → vesicles fuse → neurotransmitter into the cleft
        ↓ receptor binding → postsynaptic potential → summed by the next cell
```

So **speed** (up to ~120 m/s myelinated), **specificity** (one target, not every cell), **integration** (fires only when inputs sum past threshold — that arithmetic *is* the decision), and **no decay** (each point regenerates the signal). Hormonal signalling is the slower complementary broadcast, coordinated in [03 — Homeostasis control loop](03-homeostasis-control-loop.md).

## Tissue injury and repair

The response to damage is not passive, and the sequence is the same whether the insult is a cut, a burn, a crystal, or a bacterium.

### Inflammation

**Inflammation** is a **vascular and immune response to injury**, local in scope; each cardinal sign traces to a mechanism:

| Sign | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| **Redness** (rubor) | Local **vasodilation** → more blood in the superficial capillary bed |
| **Heat** (calor) | The same flow brings core-warm blood to the surface |
| **Swelling** (tumor) | Histamine makes postcapillary venules **leaky** → protein-rich exudate → oedema |
| **Pain** (dolor) | **Bradykinin** and **prostaglandins** sensitise nerve endings; oedema raises pressure |
| **Loss of function** | Pain, swelling, and damage make use painful and difficult |

```
injury, infection, or tissue death
        ↓ macrophages and mast cells release histamine, prostaglandins,
          leukotrienes, TNF-α, IL-1
ARTERIOLES DILATE → redness + heat; VENULES LEAK → swelling
        ↓ adhesion molecules → NEUTROPHILS adhere, crawl out (diapedesis)
          along a chemical gradient (chemotaxis)
        ↓ phagocytosis and oxidative burst clear pathogen and debris
bradykinin + prostaglandins + pressure → PAIN; tissue loses function
```

This local chain is the first half of [06 — Host–pathogen interaction](../07-microbiology/06-host-pathogen-interaction.md): if the same mediators act **systemically**, the result is widespread vasodilation and capillary leak, disseminated intravascular coagulation, shock, and **sepsis** — protective at its designed scale, lethal at the wrong one.

### Regeneration versus fibrosis

What happens next turns on two variables: **can the cells re-enter the cell cycle**, and **did the scaffold survive**?

| | **Regeneration** | **Fibrosis (scarring)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cells | **Labile cells** dividing throughout life: epidermis, gut lining, marrow | **Permanent cells** that never divide: cardiac myocytes, neurons — fibroblasts fill the gap |
| Scaffold | **Intact basement membrane** as template; architecture rebuilt | Destroyed → fibroblasts dump **collagen** in a disorganised bundle |
| Result | Original structure, **full function** | **Scar**: dense collagen, poor blood supply, no specialised cells, no contractility |
| Principle | Matches normal cell turnover | Rapid fill — **closure over reconstruction** |

```
injury → HAEMOSTASIS (clot seals the breach) → INFLAMMATION (debris cleared)
       → PROLIFERATION (fibroblasts lay type III collagen; capillaries bud in as
                         granulation tissue; epithelium migrates over if scaffold intact)
       → REMODELLING (type III → type I collagen, cross-linked along tension lines)
       → RESTORATION (architecture rebuilt) or REPLACEMENT (scar left behind)
```

A scar is **replacement, not repair**: collagen where functional tissue used to be — no alveoli, no myofibres, no nephrons, no neurons — hence scarred myocardium does not contract and cirrhotic liver cannot filter.

## Medical relevance

**The basement membrane is the most important line in cancer pathology.** Most lethal common cancers are **carcinomas** — epithelial in origin — because epithelium has the highest mitotic rate of the four tissue types. A malignant clone above an intact membrane is **carcinoma in situ**: dangerous, but unable to metastasise. **Invasion begins the moment the membrane is breached**, giving access to vessels beneath — hence reports state membrane status and margins, and screening aims to catch disease in situ — where excision is curative.

**Scar tissue is not repaired tissue; it is replaced tissue.** Collagen in a disorganised bundle restores continuity but not function: a healed infarct leaves a non-contractile patch, cirrhosis replaces filtering lobules with collagen nodules, burned skin loses sweat glands. Recovery of *function* depends on whether the basement membrane and specialised cells survived.

**Adipose tissue is an endocrine organ.** White fat secretes **leptin** (satiety signal to the hypothalamus), **adiponectin** (improves insulin sensitivity), and pro-inflammatory TNF-α and IL-6. Hypertrophic adipocytes outgrow their blood supply, die, and attract **macrophages** — the chronic low-grade inflammation linking obesity to insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular risk ([03 — Homeostasis control loop](03-homeostasis-control-loop.md)).

**Pressure ulcers are an avascular-epithelium problem.** Pressure between bone and an external surface collapses capillaries in the **connective tissue beneath the epithelium**; with no vessels of its own the epithelium starves while still intact. Risk is greatest where bone is near the surface (sacrum, heels), so prevention is *pressure redistribution*, not nutrition alone; later stages expose bone to osteomyelitis, and neuropathy removes the pain warning.

**Blood is a connective tissue, so transfusion is cells-plus-matrix replacement.** Packed red cells replace the cellular component, **fresh frozen plasma** the liquid matrix (albumin, clotting factors, fibrinogen), platelets the cells that start haemostasis. Because donor cells carry **antigens** (ABO, Rh, HLA), transfusion requires **cross-matching**. Haemophilia is the converse: normal cells, defective proteins ([03 — Proteins](../01-biochemistry/03-proteins.md)).

**Epithelia regenerate; neurons do not — the difference is cell class.** Cells are **labile** (skin, gut, marrow), **stable** (liver, kidney: re-enter the cycle on demand), or **permanent** (cardiac myocytes, neurons: effectively never). A skin wound heals in days while a stroke loses neurons permanently — so rehabilitation works by **neuroplasticity in surviving circuits**. Peripheral axons regrow along Schwann-cell tubes while the CNS fails under glial scarring; cytotoxic drugs hit marrow and gut mucosa.

**Agriculture runs on the same four tissues.** Meat quality is tissue quality: **muscle fibre type** (fast glycolytic white, slow oxidative red) sets colour, **collagen cross-linking** sets tenderness — hence long, moist cooking — and **marbling** is intramuscular adipose. Mastitis is a glandular epithelium infected while its connective tissue supplies immune cells, and rumen absorption is stratified squamous epithelium.

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "Epithelial tissue has its own blood supply" | Epithelia are **avascular**; nutrients diffuse from capillaries below — so they are thin where exchange matters, and cutting that supply kills the epithelium above. |
| "Only bone and cartilage are connective tissue" | The defining feature is **abundant extracellular matrix produced by resident cells**, not hardness — blood, adipose, areolar, and reticular tissue all qualify. |
| "Pseudostratified means several layers" | It is a **single layer**: every cell touches the basement membrane but only some reach the lumen, so nuclei sit at different heights and *look* layered. |
| "Microvilli and cilia are the same structure" | **Microvilli** are actin folds increasing surface area for absorption and moving nothing; **cilia** are 9+2 dynein-powered organelles that beat to move mucus past a cell. |
| "Cartilage heals like bone does" | Cartilage is **avascular**, fed by diffusion from the perichondrium, so it repairs poorly; bone is vascular and remodelled. |
| "The nervous system powers muscle contraction" | It supplies only the **signal** and the Ca²⁺ release; the energy is **ATP hydrolysed by myosin** — hence rigor mortis when ATP runs out. |
| "Cardiac muscle is just skeletal muscle with junctions" | Intercalated discs make a **functional syncytium** (gap junctions spread the potential, desmosomes stop tearing), and cardiac muscle self-excites, has a refractory period preventing tetanus, and resists fatigue. |
| "Scar tissue is healed tissue" | A scar is **replacement, not regeneration**: dense collagen, no specialised cells, no function. Regeneration rebuilds architecture on an intact basement membrane. |
| "Inflammation means infection" | Inflammation answers **injury or infection alike** — a burn, a crystal, and a bacterium give the same signs, because the mediators come from the host. |

## Key facts

- Every organ is **the same four tissues arranged differently**: epithelium (cover, exchange, secretion), connective tissue (support, matrix, transport), muscle (force), nervous tissue (signalling).
- Epithelia are classified by **layers** (simple, stratified, pseudostratified, transitional) × **shape** (squamous, cuboidal, columnar): **thin where substances cross, thick where surfaces are rubbed, tall and busy where secretion or absorption occurs.**
- Epithelia are **polarised, avascular, and highly mitotic**: fed from the lamina propria, replenished by basal cells as surface cells shed; **microvilli are actin folds for absorption, cilia are 9+2 dynein-powered movers of mucus**.
- The **basement membrane** anchors, filters, separates; its breach converts **carcinoma in situ** into **invasive carcinoma**. Junctions: **tight seals, desmosomes attach cell to cell, hemidesmosomes attach cell to matrix, gap junctions couple electrically.**
- **Exocrine glands have ducts and act locally (merocrine, apocrine, holocrine — the cell bursts in holocrine); endocrine glands are ductless and broadcast hormones in blood.**
- **Connective tissue is defined by abundant matrix secreted by resident cells** — matrix, not cell, sets the properties, so matrix-protein defects cause matrix diseases.
- Matrix = **fibres** (collagen tensile, elastin recoil, reticular scaffolding) + **ground substance** (proteoglycans hold water → compression resistance and a diffusion medium): **dense regular resists one-way pull, dense irregular resists pull every way, adipose cushions, osteoid mineralises to hardness, plasma makes blood fluid.**
- **Cartilage is avascular and heals poorly; bone is vascular and remodels** — the diffusion constraint shaping every epithelium.
- All muscle uses **actin–myosin sliding powered by ATP** (needed to swing *and* release the head → rigor mortis): **troponin–tropomyosin** governs skeletal and cardiac, **calmodulin–MLCK** smooth, and cardiac **intercalated discs** make a synchronised, non-tetanising syncytium.
- Nervous signalling runs **Na⁺/K⁺ gradient → resting potential → threshold → action potential → saltatory conduction → Ca²⁺-triggered transmitter release**, supported by neuroglia; **inflammation** (redness, heat, swelling, pain, lost function) is vasodilation plus vascular leak plus immune recruitment, and repair is **regeneration** (labile cells, intact scaffold) or **fibrosis** (collagen scar, function lost).

## Practice questions

**1. Gas exchange across the alveolus requires a tissue one cell thick. Which epithelium matches?**

A. Stratified squamous
B. Simple squamous — one flattened cell minimises diffusion distance
C. Simple columnar
D. Pseudostratified ciliated

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Diffusion rate falls as path length rises, so the sheet must be as thin as possible. Stratified tissue trades thinness for abrasion resistance.
---

**2. A tracheal section appears multilayered, yet every cell contacts the basement membrane. The tissue is**

A. Stratified columnar epithelium
B. Transitional epithelium
C. Pseudostratified ciliated columnar — one layer, nuclei at different heights
D. Stratified squamous epithelium

**Answer: C**

Explanation: All cells rest on the basement membrane but only some reach the lumen, so nuclei stagger. It stays one layer, letting the whole surface be ciliated.
---

**3. Which pairing of apical specialisation with function is correct?**

A. Cilia — increase absorptive surface area
B. Microvilli — a power stroke moves mucus toward the pharynx
C. Keratin — actively transports nutrients apically
D. Microvilli — actin folds that increase surface area for absorption

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Microvilli are actin folds amplifying membrane area for absorption, moving nothing. Cilia move mucus (A and B swap the two); keratin is structural (C).
---

**4. Which junction correctly matches its partners and function?**

A. Desmosome — cadherins linked to actin, sealing the space between cells
B. Tight junction — connexins forming channels
C. Gap junction — intermediate filaments resisting shear
D. Hemidesmosome — integrins attaching the cell to the basement membrane

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Hemidesmosomes use integrins to bolt a cell to matrix, not to a neighbour. Desmosomes link cadherins to intermediate filaments; gap junctions are electrical channels.
---

**5. What defines connective tissue as a class?**

A. It contains collagen in every case
B. It is always hard, such as bone or cartilage
C. It is always vascular
D. Abundant extracellular matrix secreted by resident cells

**Answer: D**

Explanation: The criterion is matrix, not hardness, collagen, or vascularity — blood has liquid matrix, cartilage is avascular. Bone and cartilage are two designs among many.
---

**6. Why does a tendon resist force along one axis but tear when twisted sideways?**

A. Its collagen is mineralised with hydroxyapatite
B. Its elastin allows recoil in all directions
C. Its collagen bundles run in parallel, giving strength only along that axis
D. Its ground substance is too sparse to hold fibres together

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Dense regular tissue aligns collagen with the line of pull, giving strength one way only. Dense irregular crosses bundles every way; elastin recoils; mineralisation hardens.
---

**7. Which statement about muscle tissue is correct?**

A. Skeletal muscle cells are uninucleated with central nuclei
B. Smooth muscle is regulated by troponin–tropomyosin
C. All three muscle types are under voluntary control
D. Cardiac muscle contracts as a unit because intercalated discs provide gap junctions for coupling and desmosomes for anchorage

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Intercalated discs weld cardiac cells electrically and mechanically, so the chamber squeezes as one, with a refractory period preventing tetanus. Skeletal fibres are multinucleated peripherally (A); smooth uses calmodulin–MLCK (B); only skeletal is voluntary (C).

**8. Saltatory conduction in a myelinated axon is faster and cheaper in ATP because**

A. Myelin increases sodium-channel density along the whole axon
B. Schwann cells generate the action potential instead of the neuron
C. Myelin allows potassium to leak continuously along the axon
D. The impulse is regenerated only at the nodes of Ranvier, where exposed membrane holds voltage-gated channels

**Answer: D**

Explanation: The sheath insulates internodal membrane, so channels open only at bare nodes and the signal jumps. Myelin reduces channel density (A), Schwann cells insulate rather than fire (B), leakage dissipates the gradient (C).

**9. The redness and heat of acute inflammation are best explained by**

A. Increased haemoglobin concentration in the damaged tissue
B. Blockage of lymphatic drainage by fibrin
C. Fibroblast proliferation during granulation tissue formation
D. Vasodilation of local arterioles increasing blood flow

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Mediators dilate local arterioles so more warm blood arrives — rubor and calor; venular leak gives swelling and mediators give pain. Fibroblast proliferation (C) is a later repair event.

**10. A biopsy shows malignant epithelial cells confined above an intact basement membrane. The correct interpretation is**

A. Invasive carcinoma — malignancy means spread
B. Sarcoma — the tumour arises from connective tissue
C. Benign tumour — the intact membrane proves non-invasive behaviour
D. Carcinoma in situ — no access to vessels, so metastasis is impossible

**Answer: D**

Explanation: While the membrane is intact the cells have no route into vessels, and excision is curative. Invasion begins at the breach (A); carcinoma means epithelial origin (B); cytology defines cancer (C).
