# 08 — Plant Biology

**Plant cells, the organs they build, the two transport systems, and how a sessile organism senses and reproduces.** The section takes cell biology and membranes into a kingdom whose engineering problems are unlike an animal's: support without a skeleton (wall plus turgor), transport without a pump (xylem tension and phloem pressure), response without a nervous system (hormones and differential growth), and reproduction through an **alternation of generations** that animals abandoned hundreds of millions of years ago. Each chapter follows structure → mechanism → consequence, because plant anatomy only becomes memorable when it is read as a solution to a physical problem.

**What this section gives you that a labelled diagram does not:** why water is *pulled* up a tree rather than pushed from the roots; why the Casparian strip makes the root a selective barrier rather than a sieve; why guard cells open the pore they are guarding; why a plant redirects auxin instead of moving a muscle; and why a seed is not a spore — the distinctions that turn plant anatomy from memorisation into engineering.
