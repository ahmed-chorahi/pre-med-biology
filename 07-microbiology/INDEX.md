# 07 — Microbiology

**Bacteria, archaea, viruses, fungi, protozoa — and the relationship between a microbe and its host.** The section takes cell biology and nucleic acids and asks what follows when a cell has no nucleus, no mitochondria, or no cell at all. It proceeds in a deliberate order: how these organisms are built and how they survive (01–04), how they multiply and move between hosts (05), what a pathogen does to the body and what the body does back (06), how humans have used microorganisms rather than merely fought them (07) — and finally the comparison tables that consolidate every distinction the section builds into a decision tool.

**Prerequisites:** [02 — Cell Biology](../02-cell-biology/), where the prokaryote/eukaryote split, the membrane, and the organelle logic are established — without them there is nothing to compare bacteria against — plus [01 — Nucleic acids](../01-biochemistry/04-nucleic-acids.md) and [05 — Molecular Biology](../05-molecular-biology/), because every viral genome type, resistance gene, and detection test is a consequence of DNA and RNA chemistry.

**What this section gives you that a list of pathogens does not:** why the Gram stain changes the antibiotic prescription within an hour; why killing Gram-negative bacteria too quickly can worsen a patient; why an antiviral must target an enzyme the host does not have; why antifungal therapy is slower and more toxic than antibacterial therapy; why an autoclave — not a disinfectant — is required for anything that has touched a spore; why normal flora are protective until they are displaced; and how to tell a spore, a cyst, an oocyst, and a capsule apart at a glance when the answer decides the control measure.
