# 04 — Genetics

The chromosomes produced by meiosis, put to work: **how alleles are named, how they are transmitted, and what happens when the simple ratios fail.** Two threads run through the section — **pattern recognition** (a chart, a ratio, a family history → mode of inheritance) and **arithmetic** (risks, carrier frequencies, and the one equation that links a disease to a whole population).

**Prerequisites:** [12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md) — segregation and independent assortment are the physical basis of both laws — plus [04 — Nucleic acids](../01-biochemistry/04-nucleic-acids.md) for the gene-to-protein chain that turns a genotype into a phenotype.

**Next:** [05 — Molecular Biology](../05-molecular-biology/) opens the gene itself — DNA structure, replication, transcription, translation, regulation, and mutation.

**What this section gives you that a list of diseases does not:** the reasoning that turns "unaffected parents with an affected child" into a one-in-four risk you can quote to a family, the confidence to tell X-linked recessive from autosomal recessive before any test result exists, the arithmetic that converts a disease incidence into the number of carriers sitting undetected in a population, and the recognition that every departure from Hardy–Weinberg expectations is evolution announcing itself.
