# Comparison Tables

## Why it matters

Almost every exam question in cell biology is secretly a **comparison question**: *which of these is found in a plant cell but not an animal cell?* which *organelle has its own DNA?* which *structure is prokaryotic?* — and the fastest way to answer is to hold a small number of organised tables rather than a large pile of isolated facts.

This chapter is the section's consolidation point. Nothing new is introduced; everything from chapters 01–05 is compressed into reference tables you should be able to rebuild from memory. Work through them by covering a column and recalling it — the act of reconstruction is the study.

## Table 1 — Prokaryote vs eukaryote

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nucleus** | Absent; DNA in a **nucleoid** | Membrane-bound nucleus |
| **DNA form** | Usually one circular chromosome; plasmids extra | Linear chromosomes with histones |
| **Membrane-bound organelles** | Absent | ER, Golgi, lysosomes, mitochondria |
| **Ribosomes** | **70S** | **80S** cytosolic; 70S in mitochondria/chloroplasts |
| **Cell division** | **Binary fission** | Mitosis / meiosis + cytokinesis |
| **Size** | 0.1–5 μm | 10–100 μm |
| **Gene expression** | Transcription and translation **coupled** in cytoplasm | Transcription in nucleus; translation in cytoplasm — **separated** |
| **Introns** | Rare | Common |
| **Cell wall** | Usually peptidoglycan (bacteria); none in archaea | Plants: cellulose; fungi: chitin; **animals: none** |
| **Membrane lipids** | Bacteria: ester-linked; archaea: ether-linked, branched | Ester-linked fatty acids |
| **Cytoskeleton** | FtsZ, MreB (homologues) | Tubulin, actin, intermediate filaments — elaborate |
| **Reproduction** | Asexual; genetic exchange by conjugation, transformation, transduction | Sexual or asexual; meiosis |
| **Energy conservation** | Plasma membrane (ETC located there) | **Mitochondria** |
| **Copy number of genome** | 1–few | Many (per nucleus) |

**The three differences that answer the most questions:**

1. **Nucleus present/absent** — the defining split.
2. **70S vs 80S** — the basis of antibiotic selectivity and the first thing a question about "why do antibiotics not harm human cells" is really asking.
3. **Coupled vs separated gene expression** — why eukaryotes can regulate, splice, and quality-control transcripts and prokaryotes cannot.

**Archaea inside the prokaryote column:** same cell plan as bacteria (no nucleus), but ether-linked membranes, no peptidoglycan, and no sensitivity to penicillin — see [00 — Foundations](../00-foundations/07-cells-prokaryotes-and-eukaryotes.md).

## Table 2 — Plant vs animal cell

| Feature | Plant cell | Animal cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cell wall** | **Present** — cellulose, outside the membrane | Absent |
| **Chloroplasts** | **Present** (in green tissue) | Absent |
| **Central vacuole** | **Large, up to 90% of volume** — turgor and storage | Small, temporary vacuoles only |
| **Lysosomes** | Hydrolytic role delivered to the **vacuole** | **Present** — M6P-targeted |
| **Centrioles / centrosome** | Absent (in higher plants) | **Present** |
| **Shape** | Fixed, rectangular (wall-supported) | Variable; deformable |
| **Cell junctions** | Plasmodesmata (cytoplasmic channels) | Gap junctions, desmosomes |
| **Stored carbohydrate** | **Starch** (in plastids) | **Glycogen** (cytoplasm) |
| **Cytokinesis** | **Cell plate** forms outward | **Cleavage furrow** pinches inward |
| **Energy organelles** | Mitochondria **and** chloroplasts | Mitochondria only |
| **Peroxisomes** | Photorespiration roles (glycolate metabolism) | Fatty acid oxidation, detoxification |
| **Steroid synthesis** | Lesser role | Active SER in endocrine cells |

**The exam pattern:** questions usually test one of three rows — **wall, chloroplast, vacuole** — or the cytokinesis difference. The logic behind each row is structural:

```
cell wall    → shape is fixed → tissue support without skeleton
chloroplast  → autotrophic nutrition → no need to ingest
large vacuole → turgor replaces hydrostatic skeleton, storage in bulk
cell plate   → a wall must be BUILT between daughters, so it grows outward
furrow       → no wall to build, so the membrane pinches like a purse string
```

**A common trap:** "plant cells have no lysosomes." The accurate statement is that plants perform lysosomal hydrolysis in the **vacuole**, using enzymes delivered with an equivalent address tag. The chemistry is identical; the compartment's name differs.

### Fungi and protists as the third case

Comparison questions sometimes use organisms outside the plant/animal split:

| Organism | Wall | Chloroplasts | Vacuole | Lysosome-like compartment | Special |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Yeast (fungus)** | Chitin | No | Large vacuole | Yes | Ferments; budding division |
| **Amoeba** | No | No | Food vacuoles, contractile vacuole | Phagolysosome | Crawls on actin |
| **Paramecium** | Pellicle | No | Contractile vacuole | Oral groove → vacuole | Ciliated |
| **Euglena** | Pellicle | **Yes** | Contractile vacuole | — | Mixotrophic |

## Table 3 — Organelle master summary

The single most useful table in the section. For each organelle: membranes, contents, job, and the failure mode.

| Organelle | Membranes | Own DNA? | Ribosomes | Main job | Medical / failure link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nucleus** | 2 (envelope) | Yes — linear chromosomes | 80S (in cytoplasm) | Store and protect genome; control access | Laminopathies, progeria |
| **Nucleolus** | 0 (region) | — | — | rRNA synthesis, ribosome assembly | Prominence marks synthetic activity |
| **Rough ER** | 1 | No | Bound 80S | Secreted/membrane/lysosomal protein synthesis, folding, N-glycosylation | α₁-antitrypsin retention; misfolding disease |
| **Smooth ER** | 1 | No | None | Lipid/steroid synthesis, detox, Ca²⁺ storage, glycogen metabolism | Alcohol-induced expansion; altered drug metabolism |
| **Golgi** | 1 (cisternae) | No | None | Modify, sort, tag (M6P), dispatch | I-cell disease |
| **Lysosome** | 1 | No | None | Acid hydrolysis at pH ~4.5; digestion, autophagy | Storage diseases (Tay–Sachs, Gaucher, Pompe) |
| **Peroxisome** | 1 | No | None | β-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids; H₂O₂ breakdown by catalase | Zellweger syndrome |
| **Mitochondrion** | **2** | **Yes — circular** | **70S** | Krebs cycle, ETC, ATP; apoptosis trigger | MELAS, MERRF, Leber; lactic acidosis |
| **Chloroplast** | **2** | **Yes — circular** | **70S** | Light reactions + Calvin cycle | — |
| **Ribosome** | 0 | — | — | Translation (protein synthesis) | Target of antibiotics (70S vs 80S) |
| **Centrosome** | 0 (+ centrioles) | No | — | MTOC; spindle poles; basal bodies | Aneuploidy; ciliopathies |
| **Cytoskeleton** | 0 | No | — | Shape, tracks, movement, division | Epidermolysis bullosa; metastasis |
| **Vacuole** | 1 | No | — | Storage, turgor, osmoregulation | Wilting; contractile vacuole in protists |
| **Secretory vesicle** | 1 | No | — | Regulated transport to membrane | Zymogen misactivation; pancreatitis |
| **Peroxisome vs lysosome** | see chapter 03 | — | — | oxidation vs hydrolysis | — |

### The membrane-count shortcut

Questions about "double-membrane organelles" have exactly two answers: **mitochondrion and chloroplast** — plus the nuclear envelope, which is also double but is continuous with the ER. If a question says *double membrane AND own DNA AND 70S ribosomes*, the answer is mitochondrion or chloroplast, and the giveaway for which is the tissue context.

```
DOUBLE MEMBRANE          NUCLEAR ENVELOPE (continuous with ER)
   │                     MITOCHONDRION (own DNA, 70S)
   └─────────────────▶   CHLOROPLAST (own DNA, 70S)

SINGLE MEMBRANE          ER, Golgi, lysosome, peroxisome,
                         vacuole, secretory vesicles,
                         endosomes, plasma membrane

NO MEMBRANE              ribosomes, centrosome, cytoskeleton,
                         nucleolus
```

## Table 4 — Which organelle makes, imports, or degrades what

A process-centred view, which is how higher-mark questions are usually framed.

| Process | Where | Key machinery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Transcription** | Nucleus (mitochondria/chloroplasts have their own) | RNA polymerase |
| **Translation** | Free ribosomes; rough ER | Ribosome, tRNA, mRNA |
| **Protein folding** | ER lumen | Chaperones (BiP), disulfide formation |
| **Glycosylation** | ER (initial), Golgi (completion) | Glycosyltransferases |
| **Lysosomal enzyme tagging** | Golgi | M6P phosphotransferase |
| **Secretion** | ER → Golgi → vesicles → membrane | COPII, COPI, SNAREs |
| **Digestion of debris** | Lysosome | Acid hydrolases, V-ATPase |
| **Self-cleanup** | Autophagosome → lysosome | Autophagy proteins |
| **Fatty acid oxidation** | Mitochondrial matrix + peroxisome | β-oxidation enzymes |
| **ATP generation** | Mitochondrion (glycolysis: cytoplasm) | ETC, ATP synthase |
| **Carbon fixation** | Chloroplast stroma | RuBisCO |
| **Calcium release (muscle)** | Sarcoplasmic reticulum | Ryanodine/DHPR channels |
| **Apoptosis execution** | Mitochondrion → cytosol | Cytochrome c, caspases |
| **Chromosome segregation** | Spindle (from centrosomes) | Microtubules, kinetochore |

## Common confusions this section retires

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "Ribosomes are organelles" | Ribosomes have **no membrane**; they are ribonucleoprotein machines. They are counted as organelles only loosely. |
| "Plants have no lysosomes" | Plants run hydrolysis in the **vacuole**; same enzymes, different address. |
| "Free and bound ribosomes are different types" | Same ribosome; location set by the **signal sequence** of the protein being made. |
| "Prokaryotes are simpler/less advanced" | Different solutions, equal age, greater environmental range — see 00 — Foundations. |
| "Mitochondria make ATP for photosynthesis" | Chloroplasts make sugar; mitochondria make the ATP the cell actually spends. |
| "Chloroplasts produce the O₂ animals need, so plants don't respire" | Plants respire continuously; photosynthesis is fuel production, respiration is spending. |
| "The nucleus is the largest organelle so it must be most important" | Size reflects DNA volume; the ER network has more total membrane and more proteins. |
| "Lysosome and peroxisome are interchangeable" | Different origin (Golgi vs cytosolic import), pH (acid vs neutral), and chemistry (hydrolysis vs oxidation). |
| "Cilia and flagella are the same in all cells" | Eukaryotic = 9+2 dynein-driven sliding; **prokaryotic flagellum = rotating flagellin filament**, unrelated. |
| "Intermediate filaments move things" | They bear tension and have **no motors**. |

## How to use these tables in an exam

1. **Identify which axis the question is testing** — prokaryote/eukaryote, plant/animal, organelle identity, or membrane count.
2. **Eliminate by structure first, function second.** "Which has 70S ribosomes?" eliminates eukaryotic cytosol immediately, before any functional reasoning.
3. **For "which organelle" questions, use the failure mode.** Storage disease → lysosome; peroxide → peroxisome; lactic acidosis → mitochondrion; M6P → Golgi; tubulin drug → centrosome/spindle.
4. **Rebuild the table from blank.** If you cannot regenerate Table 3 from memory, reread the chapter it came from rather than re-reading the table.

## Key facts

- Prokaryote/eukaryote axes: **nucleus, 70S vs 80S, coupled vs separated transcription/translation, binary fission vs mitosis, membrane-bound organelles.**
- Plant/animal axes: **wall, chloroplast, large central vacuole, cell plate vs cleavage furrow, centrioles present in animals, starch vs glycogen.**
- Double membrane + own DNA + 70S = **mitochondrion or chloroplast**.
- Single membrane: ER, Golgi, lysosome, peroxisome, vacuole, vesicles, plasma membrane. No membrane: ribosome, centrosome, cytoskeleton.
- Organelle identification by failure: **M6P → Golgi; substrate storage → lysosome; VLCFA/peroxide → peroxisome; lactic acidosis → mitochondrion; MTOC/spindle → centrosome.**
- Plants achieve lysosomal digestion via the vacuole; they lack centrioles and build a cell plate at cytokinesis.

## Practice questions

**1. Which combination is found in a plant cell but absent from an animal cell?**

A. Cell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuole
B. Nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes
C. Centrioles, lysosomes, gap junctions
D. Plasma membrane, cytoskeleton, Golgi

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Wall (cellulose), chloroplasts, and the large central vacuole are the plant-defining features. B is present in both — it is the shared eukaryotic core. C is the animal-leaning set (animals have centrioles; higher plants lack them). D is common to both.

---

**2. Which organelle has its own DNA, a double membrane, and 70S ribosomes?**

A. Nucleus
B. Golgi apparatus
C. Mitochondrion
D. Lysosome

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The combination — double membrane, circular genome, bacterial-type 70S ribosomes, division by fission — is the endosymbiosis signature, held by mitochondria and chloroplasts. The nucleus has a double membrane but linear chromosomes and no ribosomes of its own; Golgi and lysosome are single-membrane with neither DNA nor ribosomes.

---

**3. Which pair correctly matches an organelle with its identifying failure mode?**

A. Golgi — M6P tagging failure
B. Mitochondrion — substrate accumulation of ganglioside
C. Lysosome — lactic acidosis
D. Peroxisome — misfolded secreted protein retention

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Failure of the M6P phosphotransferase causes I-cell disease, where lysosomal enzymes are secreted instead of delivered — a Golgi sorting failure. Ganglioside storage is Tay–Sachs (lysosome); lactic acidosis marks ETC failure (mitochondrion); secreted-protein retention in the ER describes misfolding disease.

---

**4. A prokaryotic cell differs from a eukaryotic cell in that it**

A. Lacks ribosomes entirely
B. Performs transcription and translation in the same compartment, simultaneously
C. Has linear chromosomes with histones
D. Contains membrane-bound mitochondria

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Without a nuclear envelope, bacterial transcription and translation are spatially and temporally coupled — mRNA can be translated while still being transcribed. Eukaryotes separate them in space and time. Prokaryotes do have ribosomes (70S, A), their chromosomes are circular and poorly packaged relative to eukaryotic histones (C), and they have no mitochondria (D).

---

**5. Why does a plant cell build a cell plate during cytokinesis while an animal cell forms a cleavage furrow?**

A. Plant cells divide faster
B. The plant cell wall must be constructed between the daughter cells, so vesicles fuse outward; animal cells have no wall to build and pinch the membrane instead
C. Animal cells lack vesicles
D. The difference is random and has no functional basis

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The presence of a rigid wall determines the mechanics: Golgi-derived vesicles carrying wall material align at the cell plate and fuse outward into a new wall, while an animal cell uses an actin–myosin ring to constrict the membrane. Each mechanism is dictated by whether a wall must be created — structure determining process, not speed or chance.

---

**6. Which structure is correctly described as membrane-bound?**

A. Ribosome
B. Centrosome
C. Peroxisome
D. Cytoskeleton

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The peroxisome is enclosed by a single membrane, as are the ER, Golgi, lysosome, vacuole, vesicles, and plasma membrane. Ribosomes, centrosomes, and the cytoskeleton have no membranes — they are protein (and RNA) assemblies. The membrane-count classification is the fastest eliminator in organelle questions.

---

**7. Which statement about free and bound ribosomes is correct?**

A. Bound ribosomes are structurally larger than free ribosomes
B. Bound ribosomes make only membrane and secretory proteins; free ribosomes make only cytosolic proteins, and the ribosomes themselves differ
C. They are the same machine — a ribosome becomes bound when the emerging protein carries a signal sequence for the ER
D. Bound ribosomes are attached permanently and cannot return to the cytoplasm

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The ribosomes are identical and interchangeable; targeting is dictated by the nascent chain's signal sequence recognised by SRP. After synthesis the ribosome dissociates and can be reused. The *populations* differ in what they happen to be making, but the machines do not — which is what B gets wrong.

---

**8. In a Gram stain, the structural difference detected is found in which structure?**

A. The plasma membrane
B. The peptidoglycan cell wall
C. The nucleoid
D. The 70S ribosome

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Gram-positive bacteria retain crystal violet because of a thick peptidoglycan layer; Gram-negative bacteria have a thin layer plus an outer membrane and decolourise. The stain reads the wall, which is also why the result predicts antibiotic access — wall-targeting drugs behave differently against the two groups.

---

**9. A student claims that because plants photosynthesise, they do not need mitochondria. Which fact disproves this?**

A. Plant cells lack cytoplasm
B. Plant mitochondria perform the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation to supply ATP for all non-photosynthetic cellular work, and plant cells respire continuously
C. Chloroplasts produce all the ATP a plant cell uses
D. Plant mitochondria are only present at night

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Photosynthesis stores energy in sugar; it does not directly generate the ATP used for transport, division, and biosynthesis throughout the cell. Chloroplast ATP is used mainly within the chloroplast's own carbon fixation. Plant cells respire all the time — and non-photosynthetic tissues (roots, meristems) depend entirely on mitochondrial ATP.

---

**10. Which group correctly contains only single-membrane organelles?**

A. Nucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast
B. ER, Golgi, lysosome, peroxisome, vacuole
C. Ribosome, centrosome, cytoskeleton
D. Plasma membrane, nuclear envelope, mitochondrion

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The entire endomembrane family plus peroxisomes and vacuoles are single-membrane structures. A holds the double-membrane set (nucleus included), C holds the membrane-less structures, and D mixes a single membrane, a double membrane, and a double membrane.
