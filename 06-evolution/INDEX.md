# 06 — Evolution

**Variation, selection, drift, isolation, and the evidence that ties it together.** The section takes the genetic variation produced by meiosis and mutation and asks what happens to it across generations — through the four forces (selection, drift, flow, mutation), through the splitting of populations into species, and finally through the independent streams of evidence that show the whole process has actually occurred, and that all life shares descent from common ancestors.

**Prerequisites:** [12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md) — crossing over and independent assortment are the engine that generates the variation everything else acts on — plus [04 — Genetics](../04-genetics/), where alleles, inheritance patterns, and population-level genotype frequencies are established before they are put under selection here.

**What this section gives you that a timeline diagram does not:** why a population adapts while an individual never does, why antibiotic resistance is selection rather than instruction, why rare diseases cluster in specific founding populations, what actually has to happen for two populations to stop interbreeding, and how to look at a shared genetic code, a whale's vestigial hip bones, or a phylogenetic tree and read the single historical claim they all support.
