# Evidence for Evolution and Common Ancestry

## Why it matters

Evolution is often taught as a conclusion before it is taught as an inference, and the order matters. **No single experiment proves evolution** — instead, several independent lines of evidence converge on the same conclusion, and their strength lies precisely in their independence: fossils, anatomy, embryos, biogeography, molecules, and direct observation are gathered by different methods, in different organisms, over different timescales, and they agree. Any one line could be questioned; all of them being wrong in the same direction is not a plausible scenario.

The deeper claim is not just that populations change, but that **all life is related by descent with modification** — that the universal genetic code, the shared 70S ribosomes of mitochondria, and the whale's vestigial pelvis are the same fact seen from different angles. Common ancestry is what turns a collection of biological facts into a single historical narrative, and it is also what makes medicine possible: model organisms model us because we inherited the same machinery, and antibiotics hit bacterial ribosomes rather than ours for a reason that is a chapter of evolutionary history.

This chapter equips you to do two examinable things: **weigh a piece of evidence** (what does this actually show, and what does it not show?), and **read a phylogenetic tree** — a figure type that appears throughout genetics, microbiology, and epidemiology. It also retires the most persistent objections: that evolution is "just a theory", that there are no transitional fossils, and that human evolution is a linear march from ape to modern.

## The fossil record

Fossils provide the only **direct** evidence of organisms that no longer exist, and their distribution in rock strata follows a pattern that common ancestry predicts: simple forms in the oldest rocks, no modern mammals before the end of the Permian, flowering plants only after the Triassic.

**Dating** is what makes the record a chronology rather than a pile. **Relative dating** places layers by superposition (older below younger) and by index fossils; **radiometric dating** measures the decay of radioactive isotopes with known half-lives (uranium–lead, potassium–argon, carbon-14 for recent material) and gives absolute ages. The method does not assume the age of the rock to be dated — it measures daughter-product accumulation and derives the age from the measured ratio.

### Transitional forms

A transitional (intermediate) form shows a mosaic of ancestral and derived features — precisely what descent with modification predicts at the boundary between two groups.

| Transitional form | Between | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***Archaeopteryx*** (Late Jurassic) | Non-avian dinosaurs ↔ birds | Feathered wings and a furcula, but **teeth, clawed fingers, and a bony tail** — a dinosaur body plan with flight apparatus added |
| ***Tiktaalik roseae*** (Late Devonian), ~375 Mya | Lobe-finned fish ↔ tetrapods | Fish-like fins with **internal bones homologous to the limb bones of tetrapods**, a neck, ribs, and a flattened skull — predicted by evolutionary theory and found in exactly the right-aged freshwater rock |
| **Whale series** (Eocene) | Terrestrial mammals ↔ cetaceans | ***Pakicetus*** (four legs, nostrils at snout tip) → ***Ambulocetus*** (swimming, hind limbs) → ***Rodhocetus*** (reduced hind limbs, nostrils migrating back) → ***Basilosaurus*** (tiny vestigial pelvis, flukes) |
| ***Australopithecus*** and other hominins | Apes ↔ humans | Mosaic anatomy: bipedal pelvis and feet with a non-opposable big toe, still with ape-sized brains |

Two points about "missing links" are worth internalising. **Gaps are predicted, not embarrassing**: speciation happens in small populations over short spans, which are precisely the conditions least likely to be preserved or discovered — so a fossil record is a biased sample, weighted toward abundant, hard-bodied, lowland organisms. And **every transitional form discovered becomes "not missing" while opening two new gaps** on either side, which is why the objection "where is the missing link?" is a request about sampling, not a refutation of the pattern. Tiktaalik is the methodological high point: palaeontologists predicted from theory *where* (Devonian, freshwater, ~375 Mya) an intermediate should be, and found it there.

## Comparative anatomy

### Homology, analogy, and convergence

| Relation | Definition | Cause | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Homology** | Similar structure, different function, **shared ancestry** | Descent from a common ancestor with that structure | Forelimbs of human, bat, whale, cat — same bones, different jobs |
| **Analogy (homoplasy)** | Similar function, different structure, **no shared ancestry** of the structure | Independent evolution under similar selection | Bird wing and insect wing; camera eye of vertebrates and cephalopods |
| **Convergent evolution** | The process producing analogies | Similar environments select similar solutions | Wings (flight), flippers (swimming), streamlined body shapes (sharks/dolphins/ichthyosaurs) |

The test is developmental and structural, not superficial: **homologous structures are built from the same embryonic tissue in the same relative position**, whatever they end up doing. A whale's flipper and your arm have one humerus, one pair of forearm bones, and five digit rays — a pattern inherited from a common limbed ancestor, not one a swimmer would design from scratch (functional engineering would give flippers no need for five fingers).

The **vertebrate eye** is the standard two-sided example: octopus and human both have camera-style eyes with a lens and retina — that is **convergence** (analogy of the whole apparatus), because the octopus retina is wired the right way round with no blind spot while the vertebrate retina is inverted. The **insect vs bird wing** is analogy; the **bat wing vs whale flipper** is homology (both mammalian forelimbs) despite serving utterly different functions.

**Vestigial structures** are homologies whose original function has been lost — the human appendix and coccyx, whale hind-limb bones, flightless birds' reduced wings, pelvic spurs in pythons. They are evidence of ancestry precisely because they are useless in the present: no design process produces a vestigial pelvis in an animal that never uses hind limbs.

## Embryology

The embryos of related species resemble each other most during early development, and vertebrate embryos share a **phylotypic stage** — a stage with pharyngeal arches, a post-anal tail, and limb buds — before they diverge into fish, reptile, bird, or mammal forms.

**Von Baer's laws** state the accurate version: early embryos of related species resemble each other; divergence increases with developmental age. **Haeckel's "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny"** — the claim that each embryo replays its ancestors' adult forms — is **wrong as stated**: a human embryo never passes through a fish stage, it passes through a stage shared *with* fish ancestors' embryos.

| Claim | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Closely related species have similar early embryos | **True** — supported, and a genuine prediction of common ancestry |
| Pharyngeal arches in the vertebrate embryo are homologous (gills in fish, jaw/ear structures in humans) | **True** — same developmental origin, co-opted for different uses |
| "The embryo replays its evolutionary adult ancestors" (recapitulation) | **False** — Haeckel overstated; sometimes used as a caution about over-reading embryological evidence |

Embryological similarity also has a mechanical explanation worth knowing: **the same regulatory genes (Hox genes, and the Pax6 eye-development cascade) are deployed in both cases**, because the common ancestor's developmental toolkit was inherited, not rebuilt.

## Biogeography

The distribution of organisms on Earth is what you expect if species arise locally and spread with difficulty — and is hard to reconcile with any other hypothesis.

- **Marsupials in Australia** — placental mammals are dominant nearly everywhere else, but Australia's long separation (it split from Antarctica ~45 Mya) left marsupials to diversify in isolation, producing parallel forms (marsupial mole, marsupial "wolf", flying squirrel equivalents) with no placentals to compete with. The pattern follows the continent's geological history exactly.
- **Island endemics** — oceanic islands are colonised by whatever can cross the water, then radiate: Darwin's finches in the Galápagos (one mainland ancestor), honeycreepers in Hawaii, lemurs in Madagascar (which has been isolated ~88 Mya). Islands lack the source-continent species pool, which is why their faunas are *subset* and *derived* patterns rather than random draws.
- **Continental drift** — *Glossopteris* fern fossils and *Mesosaurus* remains occur on South America, Africa, India, Australia, and Antarctica — impossible if those landmasses had always been separate, exactly as the Gondwana reconstruction requires. Fossil distributions are a test that plate tectonics passed independently.

## Molecular evidence

Molecules turned the comparison of life from shapes to **quantifiable sequences**, and every molecular prediction follows from common ancestry.

**The universal genetic code.** The same codons mean the same amino acids in almost every organism — a human, a tulip, and *E. coli* all read UUU as phenylalanine. The handful of known variations (mitochondrial codes, some ciliates) are exceptions that fit the rule: they are *modifications* of a shared system, not independent inventions. Two unrelated origins of life would not produce the same arbitrary code; an inherited one would, and does.

**Cytochrome c comparisons.** Cytochrome c is a 104-amino-acid electron carrier in the respiratory chain — functionally identical across aerobic life, so every difference is a divergence that has accumulated since the lineages split. Classic approximate differences out of 104 residues:

| Species compared with human | Approx. differing amino acids |
| --- | --- |
| Chimpanzee | 0 |
| Rhesus monkey | 1 |
| Rabbit | 9 |
| Horse | ~12 |
| Yeast | ~44 |

The numbers track the anatomical tree: **the more closely related two species, the fewer differences**, and they accumulate roughly with elapsed time. **DNA–DNA hybridisation** gave the same signal at the genome level — single-stranded DNA from two species will hybridise, and the stability of the hybrid (measured as melting temperature) reflects sequence complementarity. Human and chimpanzee DNA hybridises so well that the classic figure of ~98–99% similarity emerged from it; sequencing has since confirmed and refined that estimate.

**Shared endogenous retroviruses (ERVs).** About 8% of the human genome consists of ancient retroviral DNA inserted by infections in our ancestors. **The same ERV sequences sit at the same chromosomal positions in humans and chimpanzees** (and in other primates at nested subsets of those positions). An insertion at a specific site is a one-in-many-billions chance event; sharing thousands of them, in order, at identical loci, is only parsimoniously explained by inheritance from a common ancestor that was infected before the lineages split. The nested pattern — some insertions shared by all great apes, others only by African apes, others only by humans — is a **molecular fossil record with its own dates**.

**Shared pseudogenes** work the same way: humans and other primates carry a broken, non-functional *GULO* gene (so we cannot synthesise vitamin C) at the same locus with the same inactivating mutations — a shared accident, not parallel failure.

## Direct observation

Evolution is not only historical inference; it has been watched.

- **Lenski's long-term evolution experiment** — *Escherichia coli* propagated by daily transfer since 1988, now past 75,000 generations across 12 identical populations. All populations improved; **one** (the Ara-3 population) evolved the ability to use citrate aerobically — a trait *E. coli* is defined by lacking. The innovation depended on earlier "potentiated" mutations (historical contingency): replaying from frozen ancestors showed the step only succeeded in lineages that had already passed through a particular state. Selection, drift, contingency, and historical inheritance, all directly visible in freezer stocks.
- **Peppered moth industrial melanism** — measured predation and allele frequency change over a century ([02 — Natural selection](02-natural-selection.md)).
- **Antibiotic resistance** — multi-drug-resistant tuberculosis, vancomycin-resistant enterococci, and methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* are selection on standing variation observed within a patient's lifetime ([07 — Microbiology](../07-microbiology/)).
- **Darwin's finch beaks** — Peter and Rosemary Grant's ~40-year record of Daphne Major showed beak depth shifting within single generations after drought, and reverting after wet years.

## Phylogenetic trees: how to read them

A phylogenetic tree is a **hypothesis of relationship**, usually built from molecular or morphological data.

```
                ┌── human ─┐
           ┌────┤          ├──── common ancestor of humans
     ┌─────┤    └── chimp ─┘     and chimps (NODE = ancestor)
     │     │
     │     └────── gorilla
     │
─────┤  OUTGROUP (distant relative used to root the tree)
     │
     └────── orangutan
```

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Node** | A branching point — a **common ancestor** of everything descending from it |
| **Tip (taxon)** | An existing (or recently extinct) species or sequence |
| **Clade** | A node plus everything descending from it — a complete branch, a monophyletic group |
| **Sister taxa** | The two lineages sharing each other's most recent node |
| **Outgroup** | A distantly related taxon used to **root** the tree and establish which state is ancestral |
| **Branch length** | Often proportional to accumulated change (substitutions, or time) |

Rules that exam questions test:

- **Trees do not read left to right.** The tips are not ordered by advancement; rotating branches around a node does not change the relationships shown.
- **Sister taxa are equally related to each other** as to anything else in their clade — humans and chimps are each other's closest relatives *within* the primates shown, and neither is "more evolved".
- **A clade must include the ancestor and all its descendants** — a group missing some descendants (reptiles without birds, say) is paraphyletic and not a clade.

**Molecular clocks** convert genetic difference into time: if substitutions accumulate at an approximately constant rate, then

```
divergence time ≈ (number of sequence differences) / (2 × mutation rate per year)
                          calibrated against fossil-dated split points
```

The assumptions (rough constancy of rate) can fail, which is why clocks are calibrated with fossils rather than trusted raw — but they have independently dated the human–chimp split to roughly 6–7 million years ago, matching the palaeontological estimate.

## Endosymbiosis: common ancestry inside your own cells

The strongest self-contained case study of common ancestry is the origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts — presented mechanistically in [04 — Energy and containment organelles](../02-cell-biology/04-energy-and-containment-organelles.md). The evidence recap here is the point: **every line is shared ancestry expressed as cellular machinery.**

```
free-living α-proteobacterium ──► engulfed by an archaeal host cell
        │                              (no digestion; mutual benefit)
        ▼
permanent endosymbiont → lost redundant genes to the nucleus
        ▼
mitochondrion: double membrane, circular DNA, 70S ribosomes,
               division by binary fission, bacterial-type lipids
        = a bacterial genome living inside a eukaryotic cell
```

Chloroplasts repeat the pattern with a cyanobacterial ancestor. The signature features — **70S ribosomes (the bacterial type), circular DNA, fission, and their own transfer RNAs** — constitute a *chondromitochondrial* system: mitochondria retain a small, self-contained translation apparatus whose components (rRNA sequences, antibiotic sensitivity, codon usage) group with bacteria in every comparison. This is why tetracyclines and chloramphenicol, which hit bacterial 70S ribosomes, can also impair mitochondrial translation — the clinical echo of an endosymbiotic event ~2 billion years ago ([00 — Foundations](../00-foundations/07-cells-prokaryotes-and-eukaryotes.md)).

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "Evolution is just a theory" | In science a **theory** is a well-substantiated explanatory framework (germ theory, cell theory, theory of general relativity). Evolution is both a *fact* (change occurs, observed) and a *theory* (the explanation covering all the evidence). |
| "There are no transitional fossils" | *Archaeopteryx*, *Tiktaalik*, the whale series, hominins, and thousands more are transitional — **gaps reflect sampling**, and are predicted by how rarely speciation is preserved. |
| "Human evolution is a straight line" | It was a **bush**: multiple hominin species coexisted, most went extinct, and modern humans are one surviving twig — not the "goal" of the sequence. |
| "Humans evolved from modern chimpanzees" | We share a **common ancestor** with chimpanzees, which was neither human nor chimpanzee. |
| "Similar embryos prove recapitulation" | Embryos show **shared stages**, not a replay of adult ancestors — von Baer, not Haeckel. |
| "A theory is a guess" | A hypothesis is a guess; a theory is what a hypothesis becomes after surviving every test thrown at it. |
| "Microevolution cannot add up to macroevolution" | Macroevolution is microevolution plus speciation plus time — no new mechanism is required ([04 — Speciation](04-speciation.md)). |
| "Vestigial organs prove nothing — they may have undiscovered functions" | Some have minor functions; the claim is that their **primary design** is ancestral (the appendix's role in gut flora does not explain a whale's hip bones). |

## Why it matters in medicine and the real world

**Phylogenetics is outbreak forensics.** Molecular trees reconstructed from pathogen genomes now track transmission chains in real time — SARS-CoV-2 lineage designation, HIV drug-resistance transmission clusters, Ebola outbreak tracing, and foodborne *Salmonella* investigations all work by reading branch lengths as elapsed mutations. Reading a tree correctly is a clinical-epidemiological skill, not a purely academic one.

**Shared ancestry is the logic of every drug target and model organism.** Antibiotics exploit differences between bacterial and human machinery — but the *similarities* (mitochondrial 70S ribosomes, conserved enzymes) are what produce off-target toxicity. Likewise, mice model human disease because we share the same genes, pathways, and (mostly) the same quirks of biochemistry; the deeper the common ancestry, the better the model.

**Molecular homology explains human genetic disease.** The vitamin C–synthesis pseudogene shared with other primates, shared ERV-derived genes now co-opted for placental function (syncytin, an envelope protein repurposed for cell fusion), and conserved regulatory elements all come from common ancestry — and pseudogenes regularly complicate genetic testing, since a functional gene's **pseudogene** can be co-amplified unless primers are designed to avoid it (the β-globin pseudogene is the classic example).

**Evolutionary thinking predicts resistance before it appears.** Knowing that selection acts on standing variation, and that conserved targets are the ones resistance mutations must alter (sometimes at a fitness cost), is the basis of combination therapy, cycling strategies, and stewardship programmes — the applied form of [02 — Natural selection](02-natural-selection.md).

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Homology vs analogy | Homology = shared **ancestry**, same structure, different function. Analogy = shared **function**, independent origin. |
| Convergence vs divergence | Convergence: unrelated lineages become **similar**. Divergence: related lineages become **different** after splitting. |
| "Looking alike means closely related" | Convergence produces look-alikes without ancestry; **relationship is measured by shared inherited structure and shared sequence**, not overall appearance. |
| A tree's left-to-right order | Tips are not ranked; **nodes mark ancestors**, and rotating branches around a node changes nothing. |
| Node vs tip | A **node** is a reconstructed common ancestor; a **tip** is a sampled taxon (living or fossil). |
| "Fossils are the only real evidence" | Anatomy, embryos, biogeography, molecules, and direct observation are **independent streams** that agree — fossils are one of several. |
| "Radiometric dating assumes the age it reports" | It measures daughter-product/isotope ratios and derives age from the **measured** ratio and the isotope's known half-life. |
| Molecular similarity is coincidental | Shared code, shared ERVs at identical loci, and graded cytochrome c differences are **patterned**, not coincidental — the pattern is relatedness. |

## Key facts

- Evolution is supported by **independent, converging evidence streams**: fossils, anatomy, embryos, biogeography, molecules, and direct observation.
- **Transitional forms**: *Archaeopteryx* (dinosaur→bird), *Tiktaalik* (fish→tetrapod), the whale series (*Pakicetus* → *Ambulocetus* → *Rodhocetus* → *Basilosaurus*), hominin mosaics.
- Fossils are dated by **superposition and index fossils** (relative) and **radiometric decay of isotopes** (absolute).
- **Homology** = shared ancestry (vertebrate forelimbs); **analogy/convergence** = shared function, independent origin (insect and bird wings).
- **Vestigial structures** (whale hind limbs, coccyx) are inherited leftovers — evidence of ancestry, not of poor design.
- Embryos share a **phylotypic stage**; **von Baer** is correct, **Haeckel's recapitulation** is not.
- Biogeography: **Australian marsupials, island endemics, Gondwana fossils** — distributions follow continental history.
- Molecules: **universal genetic code**, graded **cytochrome c** differences, **DNA–DNA hybridisation**, **shared ERVs at identical loci**, shared pseudogenes.
- **Lenski's *E. coli*** (>75,000 generations) directly observed innovation (citrate use) with historical contingency; moths, finches, and antibiotic resistance show selection in the wild.
- A tree's **node = ancestor**, **clade = ancestor + all descendants**, **outgroup roots it**; **molecular clocks** estimate divergence times from calibrated substitution rates.
- **Endosymbiosis**: mitochondria/chloroplasts' **70S ribosomes, circular DNA, fission** mark them as descendants of engulfed bacteria — and explain antibiotic mitochondrial toxicity.
- Evolution is a **fact** (change observed) and a **theory** (explanatory framework); human evolution is a **bush, not a ladder**.

## Practice questions

**1. Archaeopteryx has feathers and wings like a bird, but teeth, clawed fingers, and a bony tail like a reptile. This is significant because it**

A. Proves birds are reptiles in name only
B. Shows a mosaic of ancestral and derived features, as predicted for a transitional form
C. Demonstrates convergent evolution of feathers
D. Shows that feathers evolved for flight

**Answer: B**

Explanation: A transitional form sits between two groups and therefore combines features of both — here, the dinosaurian body plan with flight-related additions layered on. That mosaic pattern is the specific prediction of descent with modification, and it is why the fossil is informative about *how* birds arose rather than merely *that* they exist. It is homology, not convergence (C), and feathers most plausibly evolved before flight (for insulation or display) (D).

---

**2. The forelimbs of a human, a bat, and a whale are built from the same bones in the same arrangement despite performing different functions. This is evidence of**

A. Analogy, because the functions differ
B. Convergent evolution of limbs
C. Homology — inheritance from a common limbed ancestor
D. Identical selective pressures

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Homology is similarity of structure with different function owing to shared ancestry; the one-humerus/two-forearm-bones/five-digits pattern is the tetrapod limb plan inherited from a common ancestor. Differing function does not make a structure analogous (A) — analogy requires independent origin (as with insect and bird wings). Convergent evolution produces similar *functions* from different structures, the opposite of what is seen here.

---

**3. Which observation from molecular biology is the strongest evidence for common ancestry of all life?**

A. All organisms need energy
B. The same codons specify the same amino acids across nearly all organisms
C. All organisms contain carbon
D. All organisms are made of cells

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The genetic code is arbitrary — there is no chemical reason UUU must mean phenylalanine — so its near-universality is best explained by inheritance from a single ancestral system, with the few known variants being later modifications. A, C, and D are properties of chemistry and of what life *is*; they would be shared by any biochemistry and do not require descent. The code is shared *information*, not just shared material.

**4. A researcher finds the same endogenous retrovirus inserted at the identical chromosomal locus in humans and chimpanzees but not in gorillas. The most parsimonious interpretation is**

A. The virus independently inserted at exactly the same position in both species
B. The insertion occurred in the common ancestor of humans and chimpanzees after that lineage split from gorillas
C. Retroviruses prefer to insert at that particular location in all primates
D. The insertion was caused by convergent evolution

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Identical insertion at an exact nucleotide position is an enormously improbable coincidence, so the standard inference is a single insertion event inherited by both descendants — and its absence in gorillas dates the event after the human–chimp lineage split. This nested sharing pattern is why ERVs function as molecular fossils. Independent insertion (A) and universal preference (C) would have to be astronomically lucky and would predict the insertion in gorillas too.

---

**5. In a phylogenetic tree, a node represents**

A. A species that went extinct
B. A common ancestor of the lineages branching from it
C. The most "advanced" species on the tree
D. A point where two species interbreed

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Each branching point is where one lineage split into two descendant lineages — the ancestor is reconstructed at the node, not shown as a tip unless it happens to be sampled. Tips are extant or fossil taxa; nothing on a tree is ranked as more advanced, because every surviving tip has been evolving for exactly as long as its lineage exists (C wrong). Extinction leaves a lineage simply ending, without a node (A).

---

**6. Which statement about Haeckel's "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" is correct?**

A. It is fully supported — a human embryo passes through a fish stage
B. It is false as stated, but the weaker claim that related species share similar early embryonic stages is well supported
C. It explains why vestigial structures exist
D. It proves that embryos evolve during their lifetime

**Answer: B**

Explanation: A human embryo never replays the adult forms of its ancestors — that is Haeckel's overstatement. What is true (von Baer) is that embryos of related species resemble one another most early on, sharing stages like pharyngeal arches and a post-anal tail, because they inherit the same developmental toolkit. Embryos do not evolve within a lifetime (D), and recapitulation does not explain vestigial structures (C).

---

**7. Marsupials dominating Australia while placentals dominate elsewhere is best explained by**

A. Marsupials being inherently superior competitors
B. Australia's long geographic isolation allowing marsupials to diversify without placental competition
C. Marsupials evolving independently of all other mammals
D. Continental drift having no effect on species distributions

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Australia separated early and carried its founding marsupial fauna into isolation, where empty ecological space allowed adaptive radiation — the same island logic at continental scale. C is wrong because marsupials share a common ancestor with placentals, and D contradicts the very geological history that produced the isolation (A is unsupported and unnecessary once geography is known).

---

**8. The strongest evidence that endosymbiosis occurred is that mitochondria**

A. Produce ATP
B. Have their own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes, and divide by binary fission — bacterial features retained inside a eukaryotic cell
C. Are found in all eukaryotes
D. Have a double membrane

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The combination of features — bacterial-type 70S ribosomes, a circular genome, division by fission, and bacterial-type membrane lipids — is exactly what an engulfed α-proteobacterium would retain. Producing ATP (A) is a function, not evidence of origin; a double membrane alone (D) could arise by envelopment without symbiosis; being universal (C) only shows the event was early.

---

**9. Why is evolution described as "just a theory" a category error?**

A. Evolution has never been tested
B. In science, a theory is a comprehensive, well-substantiated explanation supported by multiple independent lines of evidence — not a guess
C. Theories always remain unproven and can never be relied on
D. Facts and theories are the same thing

**Answer: B**

Explanation: "Theory" in everyday speech means a hunch; in science it means an explanatory framework that has survived extensive testing — germ theory and cell theory are comparable examples. Evolution is simultaneously a fact (heritable change in populations is directly observed) and a theory (the framework explaining it). It has been tested repeatedly (A false), and facts and theories are distinct: facts are observations, theories explain them (D false).

---

**10. A molecular clock estimate places the human–chimpanzee divergence at 6–7 million years. Which assumption must hold for this to work?**

A. All genes evolve at exactly the same rate in every lineage
B. Substitutions accumulate at an approximately constant rate, calibrated against independently dated fossil splits
C. The mutation rate changes with each species' body size
D. Chimpanzees stopped evolving after the split

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The clock converts genetic difference into time by dividing by a substitution rate, so that rate must be roughly stable over the interval — and it is anchored (calibrated) against split points dated by radiometric methods and fossils rather than assumed. Rates need not be identical across genes or lineages (A is too strong, C would invalidate rather than enable the method), and chimpanzees have plainly continued evolving (D).
