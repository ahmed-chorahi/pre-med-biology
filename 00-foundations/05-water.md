# Water

## Core idea

Water is not an inert solvent that happens to be present. It is the medium in which nearly every biological reaction occurs, and its unusual properties — all traceable to molecular polarity — are why life works the way it does.

## Structure of water

```
        δ−
        O          oxygen: more electronegative,
     /     \       pulls both shared electron pairs
    H       H
    δ+  δ+         hydrogens: partially positive
```

Two facts about water explain everything else:

1. **Water is polar.** Oxygen pulls the shared electrons of both O–H bonds toward itself, leaving oxygen partially negative ($\delta^-$) and both hydrogens partially positive ($\delta^+$).
2. **Water is bent, not linear.** Oxygen has two lone pairs of electrons, and the four electron pairs around it arrange themselves tetrahedrally. That geometry produces a ~104.5° angle between the O–H bonds.

If water were linear, the two bond dipoles would cancel and the molecule would be nonpolar. **The bent shape is what makes polarity possible.** Polarity then produces hydrogen bonding, and hydrogen bonding produces almost every property that follows.

## Properties, and the mechanism behind each

This is the section worth learning structurally rather than as a list.

| Property | Mechanism | Consequence for life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High specific heat** | Hydrogen bonds must be broken to heat the water; energy goes into breaking weak bonds rather than raising temperature | A large body changes temperature slowly. Water in cells buffers temperature swings. |
| **High heat of vaporization** | Water molecules in the gas phase are entirely surrounded by other molecules, so each must break all its hydrogen bonds to leave the liquid | Evaporation consumes a lot of heat. This is the basis of evaporative cooling, including sweating. |
| **High surface tension** | Hydrogen bonding produces unusually strong cohesion between water molecules at the surface | Surface water forms droplets, not a thin film. Surface tension lets small organisms move on the water surface. |
| **Cohesion** | Hydrogen bonding holds water molecules to each other | Water forms droplets and flows rather than dispersing; helps explain why a wound holds together. |
| **Adhesion** | Hydrogen bonding to other polar surfaces (glass, capillary walls) | Water rises in narrow tubes — capillary action, which moves water up plant stems. |
| **High surface tension + adhesion** | The two together | Water rises to the top of narrow tubes; and when you cut a tree, the water is still pulled upward. |
| **Density anomaly** | Ice forces each molecule into an open hexagonal lattice with four hydrogen bonds | Ice is less dense and floats. Lakes freeze top-down, insulating the water beneath. |
| **Solvent power** | Dipole–dipole and hydrogen-bond interactions with other polar molecules | Ions and polar molecules disperse; biochemical reactions can occur in the same phase |

## Water as a reactant

Water is a participant in more than just a solvent. Two reactions matter enough to know now.

### Hydrolysis

**Hydrolysis** means breaking a bond by adding water.

```
GLYCOSIDIC BOND + H₂O  →  SUGAR A + SUGAR B
PEPTIDE BOND     + H₂O  →  AMINO ACID A + AMINO ACID B
```

Note the direction: hydrolysis *requires* water as a reactant. This is the mirror image of a dehydration (condensation) reaction, which *releases* water:

```
AMINO ACID A + AMINO ACID B  →  DIPEPTIDE + H₂O
```

Both directions matter. Digestion is hydrolysis. Building a protein from amino acids is condensation, and the peptide bond it forms is covalent — which is why it needs real enzymatic energy rather than a gentle pH change. Compare the difference in difficulty with the hydrogen bonds that hold a protein's *shape*, discussed in [04 — Chemical bonds](04-chemical-bonds.md).

### Water as the product of aerobic respiration

In cells, **oxygen** is the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain. Water is what forms when that oxygen is reduced, so water is the final **product** of aerobic respiration:

```
ELECTRON TRANSPORT CHAIN → O₂ + 4e⁻ + 4H⁺ → 2 H₂O
```

Oxygen is required for this reaction. Without it the chain backs up, NADH accumulates, ATP generation from respiration stops, and the organism dies within minutes. That is why drowning is an emergency within seconds while the brain is still viable, and why sustained oxygen deprivation in a tissue causes rapid irreversible damage.

## Hydrogen bonding in DNA

DNA's two strands are held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases, not by the covalent bonds that form each strand.

```
5'—A ═ T—3'   2 hydrogen bonds
5'—G ≡ C—3'   3 hydrogen bonds
```

Two consequences follow directly:

- **Separation is cheap.** Heating to ~70–80 °C breaks the hydrogen bonds while the covalent phosphodiester backbones survive, so the two strands can be pulled apart — which is the basis of PCR and of DNA sequencing methods.
- **Re-separation is specific.** When strands re-anneal in a solution of free nucleotides, A pairs with T and G pairs with C, because those are the pairs that satisfy the donors' and acceptors' hydrogen-bonding requirements. Specificity is built into the chemistry of the bases.

**Common confusion:** DNA cannot be unwound by breaking the backbone. Strand separation is a hydrogen-bonding event, not a covalent one.

## Solubility and the hydrophobic effect

Water dissolves polar and charged substances, and refuses nonpolar ones. This single behaviour explains a huge amount of biology.

```
WATER + ION OR POLAR MOLECULE  →  solvation (hydration shell, dissolves)
WATER + NONPOLAR MOLECULE      →  aggregation, avoids contact
```

The consequence most worth remembering is the **hydrophobic effect**, and it is not about water actively repelling nonpolar molecules. Water molecules are not repelled; nonpolar molecules cannot hydrogen-bond, so they force surrounding water into an energetically unfavourable ordered arrangement. Nonpolar molecules therefore cluster together, which minimises how much ordered water is required.

```
OIL DROPLET IN WATER
   ↓ nonpolar molecules cluster
single droplet
   ↓ less ordered water around the boundary
LOWER ENERGY
```

This is why:

- phospholipids self-assemble into bilayers with polar heads out and tails in (02 — Cell Biology)
- globular proteins fold so nonpolar side chains are buried inside ([01 — Proteins](../01-biochemistry/03-proteins.md))
- fats separate from water-based digestive fluids

**Common confusion:** it is common to hear that water "repels" nonpolar molecules. The correct statement is that water does not interact favourably with them, so it excludes them. The mechanism is the energy cost of ordering water, not repulsion.

## Medical relevance

**Fluid balance is water balance.** Total body water is roughly 60% of body mass in an adult male. Water moves between compartments — intracellular, interstitial, and the intravascular plasma fraction — and the movement is governed by osmosis, the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane toward the higher solute concentration. Disorders of this balance change cell volume, and cell volume changes function: a neuron that swells or shrinks does not fire normally. This is the mechanism behind both dehydration and cerebral oedema.

**Dehydration impairs cognition before it impairs sport.** Mild dehydration measurably worsens concentration, short-term memory, and decision-making, at a time when thirst is often not yet felt. Because the brain is about 80% water, and because skull volume is fixed, any fluid change inside the cranial cavity changes pressure. That is the reasoning behind treating head injury with care — not the specific medicine, but why swelling is dangerous.

**Water is required for the chemistry of digestion.** Hydrolysis needs water as a reactant, so luminal contents in the digestive tract must remain aqueous. The acidity of the stomach also works by denaturing ingested protein — including acid denaturation, which breaks weak interactions without breaking covalent bonds.

**Infant and elderly vulnerability.** Total body water as a proportion of body mass is about 75–80% in a newborn and lower in the elderly, with less total body water to draw on. The same proportional water loss produces a larger physiological change in both. This is a fluid-and-electrolyte reasoning point, not a treatment instruction.

**Edema has a mechanism in this chapter.** Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure, or decreased plasma protein (and therefore decreased plasma oncotic pressure), shifts fluid out of capillaries into the interstitial space. Both are described in 10 — Urinary and 10 — Cardiovascular.

## Key facts

- Water is **polar** because oxygen pulls shared electrons away; water is **bent** because of two lone pairs on oxygen. Both facts are required for polarity.
- Hydrogen bonding produces high specific heat, high heat of vaporisation, high surface tension, cohesion, and adhesion.
- **Ice is less dense than liquid water** because its hydrogen-bond lattice is open and space-containing.
- **Hydrolysis** uses water to break a bond; **condensation** releases water to form one.
- **Oxygen** is the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration, and water is the product that forms when it is reduced — the basis of oxygen's biological necessity.
- Solubility of polar and charged substances, and insolubility of nonpolar substances, drive the **hydrophobic effect** — and hence bilayer formation and protein folding.
- Water is a reactant, not just a medium.

## Practice questions

**1. Water is a bent molecule rather than a straight line. What is the direct consequence of this geometry?**

A. Water is the most abundant molecule in the universe
B. Water can hydrogen-bond with other polar and charged substances
C. Water can dissolve carbon dioxide
D. Water can freeze at 0 °C

**Answer: B**

Explanation: In a linear geometry the two O–H bond dipoles would point in opposite directions and cancel, giving a nonpolar molecule. The bend creates a net dipole, with oxygen partially negative and the hydrogens partially positive, which is what allows water to hydrogen-bond. A, C, and D describe properties of water that do not follow from its shape.

---

**2. A person exercising on a hot day begins to sweat and feels cooler. Which property of water explains this?**

A. High density
B. High surface tension
C. High specific heat
D. High heat of vaporisation

**Answer: D**

Explanation: For water to become vapour, each molecule must break essentially all of its hydrogen bonds, which takes a large amount of energy. That energy is drawn from the skin, producing cooling. Specific heat concerns how much energy is needed to raise temperature, which is why it explains why water bodies warm slowly — not why sweating cools. B and C are irrelevant to evaporative cooling.

---

**3. Why does a lake freeze from the surface downward rather than from the bottom up?**

A. Ice is less dense than liquid water and therefore floats, forming an insulating layer
B. Wind forces water to the surface
C. Cold water is denser than warm water
D. The bottom is warmer because it receives heat from the earth

**Answer: A**

Explanation: The hydrogen-bond lattice in ice is open, so the same mass of water occupies more volume and ice floats. Floating ice insulates the water beneath it, allowing liquid water to remain beneath the surface. Cold water being denser is false — warm water is denser, which is the reason water bodies stratify. D is not the reason; the relevant point is the density anomaly.

**4. Lipids in water spontaneously assemble into a bilayer with polar heads facing outward. What drives this?**

A. Hydrophobic exclusion of the nonpolar tails by water
B. An electric charge on the lipid tails
C. Enzymes arranging the lipids deliberately
D. Covalent bonds between adjacent lipid molecules

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Nonpolar hydrocarbon tails cannot hydrogen-bond with water, so exposing them requires surrounding water to adopt an energetically unfavourable ordered arrangement. Clustering the tails together minimises that cost, while the polar heads remain in contact with water on either side. No covalent bonds form between lipids, and no charge exists on the tails. This is the hydrophobic effect, not active arranging by enzymes — bilayers self-assemble spontaneously.

**5. Why does heating DNA to about 75 °C separate its two strands while the covalent backbone of each strand stays intact?**

A. The bases evaporate at that temperature
B. The strands dissolve into solution
C. The heat supplies enough energy to overcome hydrogen bonding between the bases
D. The heat chemically cleaves the phosphodiester bonds in the backbone

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The two strands are joined by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases, which are individually weak and collectively breakable by the increased molecular motion at that temperature. The covalent phosphodiester bonds within each strand are far stronger and remain intact, so each strand survives as a single chain. This distinction is what makes strand separation usable as a laboratory technique.

**6. Digestion of a protein in the stomach involves adding water across a bond. What is this reaction called, and what distinguishes it from denaturation?**

A. Hydrolysis — a covalent bond is cleaved by adding water, whereas denaturation disrupts weak interactions and leaves the covalent backbone intact
B. Phosphorylation — a phosphate group is added
C. Condensation — water is released
D. Denaturation — hydrogen bonds are broken

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Hydrolysis uses water to split a covalent bond, such as a peptide bond, producing two smaller molecules. Denaturation breaks the noncovalent interactions that maintain shape — the protein unfolds but its peptide bonds remain. The distinction matters because denaturation is usually reversible, whereas hydrolysis destroys the molecule.

**7. A solution with a high concentration of dissolved salt is placed on one side of a selectively permeable membrane, pure water on the other. What happens, and what is the mechanism?**

A. Salt diffuses through the membrane until the concentration is equal
B. Water moves toward the salt side by osmosis, because water moves to equalise solute concentration across a selectively permeable membrane
C. Neither molecule can cross the membrane
D. Water moves toward the pure water side, raising the level on the salt side

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Water moves passively down its own concentration gradient — toward the side with the higher solute concentration — because that equalises solute concentration on both sides. The solute itself is assumed not to pass. Salt does diffuse, but that is a separate process and does not explain the movement of water, which is what the question asks about.

**8. Water is called the universal solvent. What is the reason for this?**

A. Water has a low molecular mass
B. Water boils at 100 °C
C. Water can dissolve more types of substance than any other known liquid
D. Its polarity allows favourable electrostatic interactions with many ionic and polar solutes

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Because water has a partial charge on oxygen and on its hydrogens, it can interact with a wide range of ions and polar molecules, dispersing them rather than allowing them to aggregate. A overstates it — water does not dissolve everything; it dissolves most ionic and polar substances. B and C are not the basis of solvent power.

**9. Why is it chemically difficult to boil a protein molecule apart in water, but relatively easy to unfold it?**

A. Unfolding requires more energy than bond cleavage
B. Peptide bonds are held together by stronger covalent bonds than the hydrogen bonds that maintain shape
C. Peptide bonds are weaker than hydrogen bonds
D. Water is a poor solvent for proteins

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Peptide bonds are covalent and require substantial energy to break, so heating breaks the weaker hydrogen bonds and van der Waals contacts that maintain three-dimensional shape first. The polypeptide therefore unfolds into a long chain while remaining chemically intact — which is why boiled collagen becomes gelatin, and why gelatin later dissolves and can be re-formed. Unfolding requires less energy than cleavage, so A is backwards.

**10. An organism dies within minutes when oxygen supply is interrupted. Which biological role of water does this directly reflect?**

A. Water transports heat away from the body
B. Water acts as a solvent for metabolic reactions
C. Water is the end product of aerobic respiration, and its formation requires oxygen
D. Water provides mechanical support to tissues

**Answer: C**

Explanation: In aerobic respiration the electron transport chain transfers electrons to oxygen, which combines with protons and electrons to form water. Remove oxygen and the chain cannot accept electrons; NADH accumulates, oxidative phosphorylation stops, and ATP production from respiration ceases. The other functions of water are real but are not what fails immediately in oxygen deprivation.
