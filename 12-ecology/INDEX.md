# 12 — Ecology

**Levels, energy, cycles, populations, interactions — and the diversity that results.** The closing section takes everything built so far — cell biology, evolution, microbiology, plant and human physiology — and asks how it all works at planetary scale. Energy enters once and drains away at every step; matter cycles endlessly, driven overwhelmingly by microorganisms; populations grow until something stops them; species sort themselves into competitive, predatory, parasitic, and mutualistic roles; and the accumulated product is the biodiversity that runs the services everything else, medicine included, depends on. The section is also the curriculum's last word on why biology matters outside the laboratory: nutrient cycles explain fertiliser and dead zones, population models explain epidemics, and biodiversity loss explains both lost drugs and emerging infections.

**Prerequisites:** [06 — Evolution](../06-evolution/), which is the source of the variation and adaptation every interaction and population response acts on; [07 — Microbiology](../07-microbiology/), because microorganisms decompose, fix nitrogen, cause disease, and run the cycles; and [03 — Cellular Processes](../03-cellular-processes/09-photosynthesis.md), because photosynthesis is the single entry point for almost all energy into the web.

**What this section gives you that a nature documentary does not:** why food chains stop at four or five levels (the second law, not convention); why an energy pyramid can never invert even when a biomass pyramid can; how Haber–Bosch now fixes more nitrogen than all living things combined and what the surplus does to waterways; why the logistic equation and the basic reproduction number are the same mathematics; why two species cannot share one niche indefinitely and what replaces exclusion; and why losing biodiversity changes human disease risk, not just scenery.
