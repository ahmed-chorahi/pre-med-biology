# Levels of Ecological Organization

## Why it matters

Ecology is organised as a ladder: each rung is built from the one below and carries a property the one below lacks — the rule of emergence set out in [00 — Biological organization](../00-foundations/02-biological-organization.md). What changes as you climb is not the *size* of the thing studied but the *kind of question* asked of it.

That is why **misidentifying the level is misidentifying the problem**: each rung owns its own measurements, causal arrows and intervention points, and an intervention pitched at the wrong rung does nothing.

```
wrong level → wrong question → wrong measurement → wrong intervention → no effect
```

| Situation | Level it operates at | The question that level owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Influenza outbreak in a city | **Population** | How many susceptibles, what is R₀, when does immunity stop it? |
| Bat virus reaching a village after forest clearing | **Community** | Which species now mix, which is the reservoir, where is the contact? |
| Mercury in drinking water | **Ecosystem** | How does an abiotic pool enter food webs and concentrate? |
| Treeline advancing northwards | **Biome** | How does a shifted climate envelope redraw vegetation continent-wide? |
| Rising CO₂, planetary energy budget | **Biosphere** | What happens to a global cycle when its input rate changes? |

So an **epidemic** is a population-level event — one species in one area — measured by incidence, transmission and immunity. A **zoonosis** is a community-level event: the pathogen is half the story, the other half is which species live together. **Climate** is a biosphere-level driver propagating downwards through every rung. Name the rung first.

## The ladder: six rungs and what each one adds

### The rule carried forward

Two habits transfer directly from the foundations chapter:

1. **Emergent properties belong to the arrangement, not to any part.** No individual has an age structure; a population does. No single organism cycles nutrients; an ecosystem does.
2. **Each boundary is an inclusion rule.** "What is inside this level?" is always answerable, and the answer separates the rungs.

```
ORGANISM ──▶ POPULATION ──▶ COMMUNITY ──▶ ECOSYSTEM ──▶ BIOME ──▶ BIOSPHERE
  one          ONE species   ALL species    community +   climate-    the zone
 individual   one area,     one area,      ABIOTIC       defined     where life
              one time      biotic only    factors       region      exists
```

Everything above the organism groups organisms, and each grouping introduces quantities no member possesses. All of it is still cellular ([01 — Cell theory and cell types](../02-cell-biology/01-cell-theory-and-cell-types.md)), yet no single cell predicts an age structure.

### The six rungs at a glance

| Level | Definition | Includes / excludes | Emergent property studied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Organism** | One complete individual | Own boundary; excludes other individuals | Physiology, behaviour, **fitness** |
| **Population** | All individuals of **one species** in **one area** at **one time** | Excludes other species and abiotic factors | Size, density, **distribution**, age structure, growth rate |
| **Community** | All the populations of different species in one area | **Biotic only** — excludes soil, water, light, temperature | Species composition, richness, trophic structure, interactions |
| **Ecosystem** | A community **plus the abiotic factors** it exchanges matter and energy with | Adds rock, water, gases, sunlight, heat | **Energy flow**, **nutrient cycling**, productivity |
| **Biome** | A continental region with a characteristic climate and the community it supports | Excludes local anomalies (a valley, a city) | Climate-set vegetation structure; biome boundaries |
| **Biosphere** | The zone of Earth in which life exists | Only the inhabited shell — not the whole planet | Global energy budget, biogeochemical cycles |

### Worked: which rung is this?

| Question | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is a population a group of interacting individuals? | **No** | One species in one area. Interaction *between* species defines a **community**; *within* it is density dependence — [04 — Population growth](04-population-growth.md), [05 — Ecological relationships](05-ecological-relationships.md). |
| A pond's fish, insects and weeds: community or ecosystem? | **Community** | All biotic. Add the water, temperature, dissolved oxygen and light and you have an ecosystem. |
| Is sunlight part of a forest community? | **No** | Sunlight is abiotic; it belongs to the **ecosystem**, because producers channel it into biomass. |
| Is the Sahara a biome or a community? | **Biome** | A continental category defined by climate (extremely low rain) containing many communities. |

## The population level

### Definition and boundary

A **population** is all the individuals of one species in one area at one time. Three conditions, all necessary: **one species**, **one area**, **one time**. Drop one and you have a different object: two species makes a community, an undefined area a range, an undefined time a fossil record.

It is the smallest level at which **evolution** becomes visible, because allele frequencies belong to populations, never individuals ([01 — Variation, fitness and adaptation](../06-evolution/01-variation-fitness-adaptation.md)). Epidemiology counts here too: incidence, prevalence, attack rate and herd immunity are all population quantities.

### The properties, previewed

| Property | What it measures | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Size (N)** | Number of individuals | Sets vulnerability to drift and extinction |
| **Density** | Individuals per unit area or volume | Sets encounter rates with mates, food, pathogens |
| **Distribution** | How individuals are spaced | Shows what environment and species do to each other |
| **Age structure** | Proportion pre-reproductive, reproductive, post-reproductive | Forecasts future growth |

Formulae, λ and density dependence belong to [04 — Population growth](04-population-growth.md).

### Distribution patterns

Three patterns, each with a mechanism. **Clumped** is by far the commonest, because resources usually are.

| Pattern | Spacing | Mechanism | Typical example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Clumped** | Patches with gaps | Patchy resources, social grouping, limited dispersal | Wildebeest at waterholes; weeds near run-off; humans in cities |
| **Uniform** | Evenly spaced | Direct interaction: **competition**, territoriality, chemical inhibition | Nesting seabirds; desert shrubs with inhibitory roots; barnacles |
| **Random** | Irregular | Neither attraction nor repulsion, evenly supplied environment | Wind-blown seeds on uniform ground — genuinely rare |

### Age structure

The proportions in three classes — **pre-reproductive**, **reproductive**, **post-reproductive** — are a snapshot of the past and a forecast of the future. A wide base commits growth for a full generation whatever the birth rate says; a narrow base commits decline. That is why **population momentum** persists for decades after fertility falls. Diagrams: [04 — Population growth](04-population-growth.md).

## The community level

### Definition and boundary

A **community** is all the populations of different species living together in one area. It is defined by **interaction**: predation, competition, parasitism, mutualism and commensalism exist only *between* species, so they are community-level by construction.

What it excludes is decisive: **soil, water, light, temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen** — those belong to the ecosystem. A community lists who is present and how they relate; an ecosystem adds the stage.

### What community ecology measures

| Property | Definition | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Species composition** | Which species are present | The identity list — two communities can share a richness and share nothing else |
| **Species richness** | The **number** of different species | Inflated by rare species; depends on what counts as a species ([08 — Taxonomy and classification](../00-foundations/08-taxonomy-and-classification.md)) |
| **Relative abundance** | How many individuals of each species | Evenness: ten species with one dominant is far less diverse than the count suggests |
| **Trophic structure** | Who eats whom — producers, consumers, decomposers | Governs how energy and matter move ([02 — Food chains, webs and trophic levels](02-food-chains-webs-and-trophic-levels.md)) |
| **Interactions** | Competition, predation, parasitism, mutualism, commensalism | The subject of [05 — Ecological relationships](05-ecological-relationships.md) |

**Worked:** a forest community inventory lists oak, maple, deer, beetle, fungus, robin — not soil pH or January temperature. Include an abiotic variable and you have converted the object into an ecosystem: the reliable test of the boundary. Richness indices: [06 — Biodiversity](06-biodiversity.md).

## The ecosystem level

### The decisive addition: abiotic factors

An **ecosystem** is a community together with the abiotic factors with which it exchanges matter and energy. The addition is what makes two new processes definable, and those two processes *are* the level.

| | Energy flow | Nutrient cycling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What moves** | Energy (sunlight → chemical bonds → heat) | Atoms (C, N, P, S, H₂O) |
| **Direction** | **One-way** — enters, transformed, leaves as heat | **Circular** — reused indefinitely |
| **Conserved?** | No — dissipated at every transfer | Yes — the same atoms return |
| **Driven by** | Photosynthesis, then respiration | Decomposition, microbial change, weathering |
| **Studied in** | [02 — Food chains, webs and trophic levels](02-food-chains-webs-and-trophic-levels.md) | [03 — Nutrient cycles](03-nutrient-cycles.md) |

### The asymmetry: energy flows, matter cycles

This is the deep idea of ecosystem ecology; everything else at this level follows from it.

```
ENERGY
  sunlight ──▶ producers ──▶ consumers ──▶ decomposers
                        │ ~90% lost as heat at every transfer
                        ▼
            HEAT radiates away — NEVER RETURNS

MATTER
  atmospheric CO₂ ──▶ producer ──▶ consumer ──▶ decomposer ──▶ CO₂
        ▲                                                    │
        └────────────── the same carbon atoms ───────────────┘
```

1. **Producers** capture light and lock it into chemical bonds ([09 — Photosynthesis](../03-cellular-processes/09-photosynthesis.md)); each transfer up a trophic level loses ~90% as **respiratory heat**, which is why food chains are short and webs broad.
2. Heat cannot be recaptured, so energy must be **continuously supplied**, while **decomposers** return atoms to abiotic pools — impossible without an abiotic environment to return them to.
3. Atoms are conserved, so **matter cycles indefinitely** while energy must be replaced.

An ecosystem cut off from energy inflow goes cold and still; one that "lost its atoms" is incoherent, because they do not leave. **Worked:** is a forest plus its leaf litter a community or an ecosystem? Litter awaits decomposition, needing decomposers (biotic) *and* oxygen, moisture, temperature (abiotic) — the moment an abiotic variable enters, you are at ecosystem level.

## The biome level

### Climate selects the community

A **biome** is a continent-sized community type determined principally by **climate** — above all **temperature and precipitation** and their seasonality. The causal direction is the whole point:

```
LATITUDE + ALTITUDE + RAIN-SHADOW + OCEAN CURRENTS
                    │ determine
                    ▼
   TEMPERATURE × PRECIPITATION (and seasonality)
                    │ selects plant growth form → sets animal and microbial community
                    ▼
   tree / needle leaf / grass / succulent / moss ──▶ BIOME
```

Abiotic → biotic, always. Trees do not make a place wet; wet places make trees possible. Because biome boundaries are **climate boundaries**, they move when climate moves.

### The major terrestrial biomes

| Biome | Climate that selects it | Why that climate produces this community |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tundra** | Very cold, dry, permafrost, short season | Too cold and waterlogged for trees: only mosses, lichens, sedges and dwarf shrubs rooted above permafrost |
| **Taiga (boreal coniferous forest)** | Long severe winters, moderate rain, cool summers | Needle leaves, conical crowns and evergreen habit shed snow and photosynthesise the moment the season opens |
| **Temperate deciduous forest** | Moderate temperatures, year-round rain, strong seasonality | Broad leaves maximise summer photosynthesis but lose water and freeze, so they are **shed in autumn** |
| **Temperate grassland** | Moderate but unreliable rain, drought, fire, grazing | Grasses dominate because their **meristems sit at the base**, protected from fire and grazing; rain is too low and fire too frequent for trees to establish |
| **Desert** | Under ~250 mm rain, large day–night swing, high evaporation | Scarcity selects succulence, CAM photosynthesis, deep roots, sunken stomata and a thick cuticle — minimal leaf area, maximal retention |
| **Tropical rainforest** | Hot and wet year-round, no frost, often poor soil | Constant warmth and water allow year-round growth: layered **canopy**, extreme **species richness**, evergreen leaves, rapid cycling in warm litter rather than soil |

Two cautions. **Precipitation alone does not decide** — a cold place with moderate rain is tundra, not forest, because temperature limits growth. **Aquatic systems** are classified by depth, salinity, light and current instead.

**Worked:** at 15 °C and 600 mm rain, forest or grassland? Temperature permits trees, so rainfall and its *variability* decide — 600 mm, moderate and unreliable, plus fire, tips the outcome to grassland. Climate sets the possibilities; disturbance holds the choice.

## The biosphere level

### The zone where life exists

The **biosphere** is the portion of Earth where life exists — the thin shell of lower atmosphere, hydrosphere and upper lithosphere (surface soils, subsurface rock), not the whole planet: the deep mantle and outer space are excluded.

| Requirement | Source |
| --- | --- |
| **Liquid water** | The hydrological cycle, held inside a narrow temperature band |
| **Usable energy** | Almost entirely **sunlight**, captured by oxygenic photosynthesis |

### The energy and water budget

Nearly all energy powering the biosphere enters through one reaction:

```
6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy ──▶ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
                                     │            ▲
                                     │            └── the oxygen comes from WATER
                                     ▼
                       chemical energy entering the biosphere
```

That equation links three biosphere-level facts: the **energy budget** (light in, heat out), **atmospheric composition** (O₂ maintained against respiration and combustion) and the **water budget** (water is a reactant, not a mere solvent). The energy/matter asymmetry becomes a planetary balance sheet — [09 — Photosynthesis](../03-cellular-processes/09-photosynthesis.md).

Two consequences: **global biogeochemical cycles** (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, water) are biosphere-level because their pools cross every biome; and habitable conditions are partly *maintained by life itself* — photosynthesis built the atmosphere supporting aerobic life, so the biosphere is studied as a system, not a container.

## Habitat and niche

### Address versus profession

| | **Habitat** | **Niche** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analogy | The **address** | The **profession** |
| Definition | The place an organism **lives** | Its **role**: requirements, resources, conditions, and how it uses them |
| Example | An oak's habitat is the hillside it grows on | Full sun as a seedling, deep well-drained soil, and its role as producer, acorn source, epiphyte substrate |

A species can change address and keep its profession; if it cannot perform the profession there, it cannot persist. **Habitat is a place; a niche is a set of conditions and functions.**

### Fundamental niche, realised niche, and competitive exclusion

The **fundamental niche** is the full range of conditions and resources in which a species could survive alone; the **realised niche** is what it actually occupies once competitors, predators and parasites are present — usually narrower. The rule connecting them is the **competitive exclusion principle**: **two species cannot occupy the same niche indefinitely** — one is excluded locally, or the two diverge until their niches no longer overlap.

```
two species with identical niche requirements
        │
        ▼
interference or exploitation competition
        ├──▶ one outcompetes the other ──▶ LOCAL EXCLUSION
        └──▶ selection at the margins of the overlap
                    │
                    ▼
   RESOURCE PARTITIONING / CHARACTER DISPLACEMENT ──▶ coexistence
```

Competition and partitioning: [05 — Ecological relationships](05-ecological-relationships.md). The evolutionary outcome — divergence of competing lineages, and speciation once geographic contact is broken — is [04 — Speciation](../06-evolution/04-speciation.md). Character displacement is competitive exclusion's constructive resolution: rather than one species losing, both change.

**Worked:** two warbler species in one forest, one feeding above and one below. Different habitats? No — **same habitat, different niches**: resource partitioning, evidence that competitive exclusion shaped the community without requiring either to be absent.

## Scale: space and ecological time

### Spatial scale

Ecology is explicitly multi-scale: the same organisms are studied at several rungs at once.

```
MICROBIAL MAT (mm) → ORGANISM → POPULATION → COMMUNITY → ECOSYSTEM → LANDSCAPE → BIOME → BIOSPHERE
     10⁻³ m           10⁻¹ m     10²–10⁴ m   10³–10⁵ m  10⁴–10⁶ m  10⁵–10⁷ m  10⁶–10⁷ m  10⁷ m
```

### Ecological time

| Time scale | Processes |
| --- | --- |
| Seconds–hours | Behaviour, physiological response, predator avoidance |
| Days–months | Reproduction, dispersal, outbreaks |
| Years–decades | Population cycles, succession, community change |
| Decades–centuries | Biome boundary shifts, persistent ecosystem change |
| Millennia and beyond | Biogeochemical cycles, speciation, extinction — [06 — Evolution](../06-evolution/01-variation-fitness-adaptation.md) |

### Patterns do not transfer automatically between levels

The most important consequence: **a pattern at one level may not exist at another.** A trait favoured in an individual may spread *or* vanish at population level, because that level adds frequencies, competition and environmental feedback the individual level lacks.

| Level shift | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Individual → population | A trait that wins contests but shortens life may raise one individual's success while lowering the population's growth rate |
| Individual → species | **Selection is not for the good of the species**: adaptation is defined on individual fitness ([01 — Variation, fitness and adaptation](../06-evolution/01-variation-fitness-adaptation.md)); species-level benefit is the group-selection error |
| Community → ecosystem | Removing a dominant species reorganises the community, yet ecosystem productivity may barely move — redundancy absorbs the shock |

The rule: **state the level before stating the claim.** "Good for the organism" and "good for the population" are different hypotheses needing different evidence.

## Relevance to medicine and the real world

**Conservation works at several levels at once, and fails when only one is addressed.** Species recovery is nominally a population-level programme — headcounts, minimum viable population size, corridors — but it is void if the ecosystem's abiotic conditions have gone: salmon cannot be restocked into a river whose flow and temperature have been altered, and a coral cannot recover above its bleaching threshold. The sequence is **ecosystem conditions first, population recovery second**, with community structure as the intermediate check — biodiversity targets without that abiotic floor are counting exercises ([06 — Biodiversity](06-biodiversity.md)).

**Zoonotic emergence is a community-level phenomenon, and the clearest medical case for level discipline.** A reservoir virus is not, by itself, an outbreak: species that do not normally meet must meet. Deforestation fragments habitat and farming brings wildlife into contact with livestock and people, so **spillover** occurs there. Nipah moving from bats to pigs to people, and Ebola's spread through great-ape and human contact networks, are community-composition stories: change the members and you change the risk. Interventions at the wrong rung fail — treating cases does nothing to the reservoir community, and culling one host can *raise* incidence. Transmission machinery: [05 — Microbial reproduction and transmission](../07-microbiology/05-microbial-reproduction-and-transmission.md).

**Climate change is the biosphere-level driver that propagates downwards through every rung**; each arrow names a different level:

```
greenhouse gases accumulate (BIOSPHERE: energy budget)
        ↓ radiative balance → temperature and precipitation shift
        ↓ biome boundaries move — treeline advances, grassland aridifies (BIOME)
        ↓ community composition changes; vector and host ranges shift (COMMUNITY)
        ↓ local populations rise or crash (POPULATION) → incidence changes (MEDICINE)
```

So malaria, dengue and tick-borne encephalitis are tracked against **altitude and latitude gradients**: the vector's climate envelope is a biome-scale object even when the patient is an individual — framing them as merely clinical misidentifies the rung generating the trend.

**Ecosystem services are ecosystem-level functions humans consume without paying for them.** Pollination of crops by wild bees, water filtration by wetlands, carbon storage in peat and soil, pest control by predators and coastal protection by mangroves are emergent properties — no single organism performs any of them, and none survives removal of the abiotic conditions sustaining the system. [08 — Plant biology](../08-plant-biology/) and [07 — Beneficial microorganisms](../07-microbiology/07-beneficial-microorganisms.md) treat the machinery — floral rewards, nitrogen fixation, mycorrhizal exchange — but the *service* belongs to the whole ecosystem and is lost to habitat degradation long before any species goes extinct: the economic argument for protecting systems rather than species lists.

**Framing a problem at the correct level is itself a clinical skill.** Antibiotic resistance is visible at five rungs at once: a mutation in a target gene (cellular), selection shifting allele frequency in a bacterial population (population), **horizontal gene transfer across species** in a biofilm (community), resistance genes enriched in farm effluent (ecosystem), and global spread of a resistance plasmid (biosphere). Treating it as a patient-level problem — better prescribing alone — addresses one rung of a five-rung object, which is why stewardship ignoring sewage, agriculture and animal husbandry plateaus. The level you name determines the intervention you can design.

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "A population is a group of interacting individuals" | A population is **one species in one area at one time**. Interaction *between* species defines a **community**; *within* a species it is density dependence. |
| "Community and ecosystem mean the same thing" | A community is **biotic only**; the ecosystem adds **abiotic** factors — soil, water, light, temperature — which is what makes energy flow and nutrient cycling possible. |
| "Energy cycles through an ecosystem" | Energy **flows one-way** and leaves as heat; only **matter** cycles, so solar input is obligatory. |
| "Nutrients travel one-way from soil to organism" | Nutrients **cycle**: decomposition returns atoms to abiotic pools — an ecosystem process. |
| "A biome is just a very large community" | A biome is defined by **climate** at continental scale and *contains* many communities — climate sets the biome, not the reverse. |
| "Habitat and niche are synonyms" | Habitat = **address** (where it lives); niche = **profession** (what it does and needs). |
| "Two species can share a niche if resources are plentiful" | **Competitive exclusion** operates regardless of abundance; overlapping niches resolve by exclusion, partitioning or character displacement. |
| "Age structure is a property of an individual" | No individual has an age structure: it is a **population-level** property forecasting growth independently of the current birth rate. |
| "Species richness alone shows a community is healthy" | Richness ignores **composition, evenness and interactions** — a rich community may be dominated by a single invader. |
| "Selection always benefits the species" | Selection is defined on **individual fitness**: a trait favoured in an individual can spread or vanish at population level. |
| "The biosphere is the whole Earth" | The biosphere is only the **inhabited shell**: lower atmosphere, hydrosphere, upper lithosphere. |
| "Restoring a species restores the ecosystem" | Species recovery is a **population** programme; without the **abiotic** conditions and community context it needs, recovery does not hold. |

## Key facts

- Levels nest: **organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere**; each rung carries an **emergent property** the one below lacks.
- **Population** = one species, one area, one time; properties are size, density, distribution, age structure — [04 — Population growth](04-population-growth.md).
- **Distribution**: clumped (patchy resources, sociality, dispersal limits — commonest), uniform (competition, territoriality, chemical inhibition), random (rare).
- **Age structure** (pre-/reproductive/post-reproductive proportions) forecasts growth and explains momentum.
- **Community** = all species in an area, **biotic only**: composition, richness, evenness, trophic structure, interactions ([05 — Ecological relationships](05-ecological-relationships.md)).
- **Ecosystem** = community + abiotic factors, defined by **energy flow (one-way)** and **nutrient cycling (circular)**.
- Energy is fixed by producers and ~90% lost as heat per transfer, so it must be resupplied while matter is reused indefinitely.
- **Biome** = continental community type selected by **temperature × precipitation**: tundra, taiga, temperate deciduous forest, grassland, desert, tropical rainforest — abiotic → biotic.
- **Biosphere** = the zone where life exists, running on liquid water and light energy — both bound up in [09 — Photosynthesis](../03-cellular-processes/09-photosynthesis.md), whose oxygen comes from splitting water.
- **Habitat = address; niche = profession** — fundamental niche (alone) versus realised niche (with competitors present).
- **Competitive exclusion**: two species cannot share a niche indefinitely — resolved by exclusion, partitioning or character displacement ([04 — Speciation](../06-evolution/04-speciation.md)).
- **Scale is spatial and temporal** — microbial mat to biosphere, seconds to millennia — and a pattern at one level may not exist at another.
- Misidentifying the level misidentifies the problem: epidemic = population, zoonosis = community, climate = biosphere.

## Practice questions

**1. Which statement correctly distinguishes a population from a community?**

A. A population includes all species in an area; a community includes only one
B. A population is one species in one area at one time; a community is all species in that area and how they interact
C. A population includes abiotic factors; a community does not
D. A community is by definition smaller than a population

**Answer: B**

Explanation: One species, one area and one time; adding species creates a community, defined by between-species interaction. Abiotic factors enter only at ecosystem level (C); a community holds many populations (D).

---

**2. Individual desert shrubs are spaced almost evenly across a plain. The mechanism most likely responsible is**

A. Patchy distribution of water around each plant
B. Random seed rain in a uniform environment
C. Competition between neighbours, possibly reinforced by chemical inhibition
D. Social grouping for protection

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Even spacing signals direct interaction — contest competition or allelopathic exudates. Clumping follows patchy resources (A), random spacing needs *no* strong interaction (B), and D produces clumping.

---

**3. A population's age-structure diagram has a very wide pre-reproductive base. The most defensible prediction is**

A. The population will decline next year regardless of birth rate
B. The population has exceeded its carrying capacity
C. The population is at equilibrium
D. Growth is already committed for at least one further generation

**Answer: D**

Explanation: A wide base means many individuals have not yet reproduced, so births rise as they mature — momentum persisting even if fertility falls at once. Decline (A) needs a narrow base; overshoot (B) and equilibrium (C) do not follow from age structure.

---

**4. Which of the following is a property of a community rather than an ecosystem?**

A. Species composition and trophic structure
B. Rate of decomposition
C. Soil nitrogen concentration
D. Dissolved oxygen of the water

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Composition and trophic structure describe which biotic populations are present and how they feed: community properties. Decomposition rate, soil nitrogen and dissolved oxygen (B–D) need an abiotic component, upgrading the object to an ecosystem.

---

**5. In an ecosystem, energy and matter differ in that**

A. Both cycle indefinitely within the system
B. Energy flows one-way and is lost as heat, whereas matter cycles
C. Matter flows one-way, whereas energy cycles
D. Both are conserved within the system

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Energy is fixed by producers and dissipates as heat at every transfer, so it must be resupplied; atoms are conserved and return to abiotic pools, so matter recirculates. A and D deny the one-way loss; C reverses it.

---

**6. Tropical rainforest supports far greater species richness than taiga chiefly because**

A. Constant high temperature and rainfall permit year-round growth and layered niches
B. Its organisms evolved more recently
C. Taiga contains no producers
D. Rainforest soils are richer in nutrients

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Warmth without a growing-season limit and water without a dry season allow continuous production, a tall layered canopy and many specialised niches. Taiga soils are often *more* nutrient-rich (D), producers abound in both (C), and recency does not track richness (B).

---

**7. Two warbler species forage at different heights in the same forest. The best interpretation is**

A. They occupy different niches in one habitat, having partitioned resources to avoid competitive exclusion
B. They occupy different habitats
C. They are the same species with different feeding habits
D. Their abiotic environments differ between the canopy and the floor

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Habitat is the address and niche is the profession: both birds live in the same forest but perform different roles — resource partitioning, the standard evidence that competitive exclusion shaped the community. Separate habitats (B), different abiotic conditions (D) and one species (C) are excluded.

---

**8. After forest clearing, a virus carried by fruit bats reaches humans via pig farms. This is best described as**

A. A population-level event only
B. A biosphere-level event
C. An ecosystem-level event caused by abiotic change
D. A community-level event, in which altered species contacts enable spillover

**Answer: D**

Explanation: The pathogen already existed in its reservoir; what changed was which species now mix — habitat disruption redrawing the community's contact network, making spillover community-level even though the outcome appears in individuals. A ignores the cross-species requirement; B and C name levels whose trigger does not create it.

---

**9. Which statement about levels of organisation is correct?**

A. An emergent property can be demonstrated on a single isolated part
B. A trait that increases an individual's fitness must improve the population's success
C. A pattern observed at one level may not exist at the level above it
D. Natural selection acts for the good of the species

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Each level adds quantities the level below lacks — frequencies, interactions, feedback — so a pattern need not survive the climb, and a trait favoured in an individual may spread or vanish at population level. Emergent properties belong to the arrangement, not the part (A); selection acts on individual fitness (B, D).

---

**10. Nearly all the energy entering the biosphere does so through**

A. Chemosynthesis at deep-sea hydrothermal vents
B. Oxygenic photosynthesis converting light energy into chemical energy
C. Weathering of rocks releasing stored nutrients
D. Combustion of fossil fuels
