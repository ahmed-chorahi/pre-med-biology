# Pre-Med Biology

A progressive biology resource built for pre-medical students. It is written to move you from *knowing a term* to *understanding a mechanism* to *seeing why it matters in medicine*.

---

## What is this?

A textbook and study guide in Markdown. Each chapter works through a concept in a deliberate order:

```
What is it?  →  Where is it?  →  How is it built?  →  What does it do?  →  How does it work?  →  Why does it matter?  →  What does medicine do with it?
```

The emphasis is on **mechanism and structure–function relationships**, not on lists of facts. A chapter that tells you ribosomes make proteins is not finished; a chapter that shows the ribosome reading codons, tRNAs delivering amino acids, and peptide bonds forming has done its job.

## Who is it for?

Students who:

- are preparing for medical, dental, veterinary, or nursing entry exams (MCAT-style reasoning included)
- have finished or are finishing high-school biology and want genuine understanding rather than re-reading notes
- will need a working foundation for anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, and pharmacology in medical school

It assumes no prior university biology. It does assume comfort with basic algebra and reading a simple chemical formula.

## How should I study it?

Work **in order**. The chapters build on each other deliberately — for example, `01-biochemistry/03-proteins.md` is written on the assumption that you understand covalent bonds and water from `00-foundations`.

For each chapter:

1. **Read for the mechanism.** Follow the arrow chains (`↓`) rather than skimming. Those are the load-bearing parts of the explanation.
2. **Say it back.** Close the file and explain the process out loud without looking. If you can only recite a label, you have not finished it.
3. **Do the Key Facts and Practice Questions** at the end of the chapter *before* moving on.
4. **Use `practice/`** for cumulative testing once you have finished the matching section.
5. **Return to Common Confusions** whenever you get something wrong — most wrong answers come from a specific, identifiable mix-up rather than from not knowing the material.

Suggested pace: one section per week for the foundation sections, slower for the anatomy and physiology systems, which are conceptually denser but faster to read.

## How does this prepare me for medicine?

Medicine is the study of what happens when biology stops working properly. This resource builds the biology first, then shows where it connects — always through mechanism, never as a list of diseases.

Examples of the connections you will find threaded through the text:

```
CELL MEMBRANE → ION TRANSPORT → MEMBRANE POTENTIAL → NERVE AND MUSCLE FUNCTION
DNA MUTATION → ALTERED PROTEIN → ALTERED CELL FUNCTION → POSSIBLE DISEASE
INSULIN → GLUCOSE REGULATION → HOMEOSTASIS
KIDNEY → FILTRATION → FLUID AND ELECTROLYTE REGULATION → HOMEOSTASIS
```

The same reasoning chain runs through a plasma membrane, a mutated ion channel, and an inhaled particle. That is the point: medicine is mostly pattern recognition applied to mechanism, and mechanism is what these chapters teach.

**Scope note.** This is an educational biology resource. It discusses disease only as broken biology. It does not provide diagnosis, treatment, or medication guidance.

## Further reading

These are the standard open resources that cover the same ground, and are useful for a second explanation of anything here that resists understanding:

- **OpenStax Biology 2e** — free, peer-reviewed, comprehensive
- **OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e** — free, aligned with the systems chapters
- **NCBI Bookshelf** — free full-text life-science texts and StatPearls

Nothing here is copied from them; they are listed because they are good places to check a concept you are stuck on.
