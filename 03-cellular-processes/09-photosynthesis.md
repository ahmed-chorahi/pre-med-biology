# Photosynthesis

## Why it matters

Photosynthesis is the reaction that fills the two reservoirs everything else depends on: **atmospheric O₂** and **chemical energy in food**. It runs in chloroplasts (02 — Cell Biology), reverses the logic of respiration (chapters 06–07), and — because every animal cell ultimately depends on it — is the metabolic starting point of every food chain.

The chapter has one structural idea: photosynthesis is **two linked reactions in two compartments**. The **light reactions** capture energy on the thylakoid membranes; the **Calvin cycle** spends it in the stroma. Nothing else makes sense until that split is fixed.

## The overall picture

```
                    LIGHT
                      │
                      ▼
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  LIGHT REACTIONS  (thylakoid membrane)   │
    │  H₂O ──▶ O₂  (photolysis)               │
    │  ADP + Pᵢ ──▶ ATP                       │
    │  NADP⁺ ──▶ NADPH                        │
    └───────────────┬──────────────────────────┘
                    │  ATP + NADPH (energy + reducing power)
                    ▼
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  CALVIN CYCLE  (stroma)                  │
    │  CO₂ ──▶ sugar (G3P)                     │
    │  uses ATP and NADPH; regenerates RuBP    │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────┘

SUMMARY:  6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy ──▶ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
```

**Note the bookkeeping:** the O₂ released comes from **water**, not CO₂ (chapter 04), and the carbon fixed comes from CO₂. Photosynthesis *reduces* carbon (adds electrons from water); respiration *oxidises* it. They are exact chemical opposites sharing the same molecules in reverse.

## Light and pigment

| Pigment | Absorption peaks | Colour reflected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chlorophyll a** | ~430 nm (blue), ~662 nm (red) | Green |
| **Chlorophyll b** | ~453 nm, ~642 nm | Green |
| **Carotenoids** | Blue-green | Yellow-orange (reflected) |

- **Chlorophyll a is the reaction-centre pigment** — the only one that can donate an excited electron to the primary acceptor. Accessory pigments (chlorophyll b, carotenoids) are an **antenna**: they absorb wavelengths chlorophyll a misses and pass the energy inward.
- **An action spectrum** plots rate of photosynthesis against wavelength — it follows absorption by the *combination* of pigments, proving accessory pigments contribute.
- **Carotenoids also photoprotect** — they quench singlet oxygen generated by excess light. Antenna and safety net in one molecule.

**Green is the wavelength *not* used** — the reflected light is why plants look green. Photosynthesis runs on the blue and red ends of the spectrum.

## The light reactions

**Location: thylakoid membranes.** Photosystems and carriers are embedded; the products (ATP, NADPH) are released into the stroma; water is split in the lumen.

### The two photosystems

| | **Photosystem II (PSII)** | **Photosystem I (PSI)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reaction centre** | P680 | P700 |
| **Primary electron acceptor** | Pheophytin → plastoquinone (PQ) | Ferredoxin |
| **Special job** | **Photolysis of water** — splits H₂O, releases O₂ | Produces strong reductant for NADP⁺ |
| **Order in linear flow** | **First** | Second (despite the name) |

**The naming embarrassment:** PSII was discovered after PSI and *also* because experiments isolated the two in the wrong order of function. **Electrons flow PSII → PSI**, always. The names are historical, not sequential — a fact that trips up nearly every student once.

### Linear (non-cyclic) electron flow

```
H₂O ──▶ PSII (P680, absorbs light) ──▶ excited e⁻ ──▶ PQ
   │         splitting releases 2H⁺ into lumen + O₂
   ▼
PQ ──▶ CYT b6f ──▶ PUMPS H⁺ INTO LUMEN ──▶ plastocyanin (PC)
                                              │
                                              ▼
                                             PSI (P700, absorbs light)
                                              │ excited e⁻
                                              ▼
                                          ferredoxin ──▶ NADP⁺ REDUCTASE
                                              │
                                              ▼
                                           NADPH

H⁺ gradient across thylakoid membrane ──▶ ATP SYNTHASE ──▶ ATP
```

**Three products, one pathway:**

1. **O₂** — waste product of replacing PSII's lost electrons by splitting water.
2. **NADPH** — strong reducing power, made at the end of the chain.
3. **ATP** — made by **photophosphorylation**: H⁺ pumped by cytochrome *b₆f* (plus those released by water splitting) accumulate in the lumen and flow back through ATP synthase.

**Chemiosmosis again — but with a twist worth knowing:** the proton gradient in chloroplasts is built partly by the **electron transport chain** and partly by **water splitting itself** (H⁺ released inside the lumen), and it is **dissipated by using H⁺ to reduce NADP⁺ on the stromal side** as well as by ATP synthase. Same Mitchell principle, slightly different plumbing — which is why chloroplast ATP synthase runs *backwards* in orientation relative to mitochondria: **H⁺ flows lumen → stroma.**

### Cyclic electron flow

PSI can also cycle its excited electrons **back to cytochrome *b₆f*** via ferredoxin instead of to NADP⁺:

```
PSI → ferredoxin → PQ → cyt b6f → H⁺ pumped → back to PSI
                          (no NADPH, no O₂ — just ATP)
```

**Why it exists:** the Calvin cycle needs **3 ATP per 2 NADPH**; linear flow produces them closer to 1:1. Cyclic flow tops up the ATP without overproducing NADPH or releasing more O₂. It is a **balance dial**, not a separate pathway.

## The Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)

**Location: stroma.** Named for Melvin Calvin (Nobel Prize, 1961, using radioactive ¹⁴CO₂ and paper chromatography). Three phases:

### 1. Carbon fixation

```
CO₂ + RIBULOSE-1,5-BISPHOSPHATE (5C) ──RuBisCO──▶ 2 × 3-PHOSPHOGLYCERATE (3C)
```

- **RuBisCO** (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase) is the most abundant protein on Earth — and famously **slow** (~3 reactions/sec vs millions for a typical enzyme) and imperfect.
- Each fixed CO₂ adds one carbon: 5C + 1C → two 3C molecules. **Three turns of the cycle** fix three CO₂ and yield **one net G3P** (3×1C = 3C), which is the exportable product.

### 2. Reduction

```
3-PGA ──ATP──▶ 1,3-BPG ──NADPH──▶ GLYCERALDEHYDE-3-PHOSPHATE (G3P)
```

**The ATP and NADPH from the light reactions are spent here.** This is the moment the two halves of photosynthesis couple: light energy, now in chemical form, reduces carbon.

### 3. Regeneration of RuBP

```
5 × G3P ──ATP──▶ 3 × RIBULOSE-1,5-BISPHOSPHATE
```

Most of the G3P is recycled to regenerate the CO₂ acceptor. **Without regeneration the cycle stops after three turns** — which is why the pathway is a *cycle* and not a linear synthesis.

### The balance

```
3 CO₂ + 9 ATP + 6 NADPH ──▶ 1 G3P (net) + 9 ADP + 8 Pᵢ + 6 NADP⁺
```

**Six G3P (6 turns of the cycle) = one glucose.** Everything the cell does with sugar — glycolysis, storage as starch, transport as sucrose — begins from G3P leaving the cycle.

## Photorespiration: the flaw

**RuBisCO's oxygenase activity.** When O₂ is high and CO₂ low (hot, dry days — stomata closed), RuBisCO fixes **O₂ instead of CO₂**, producing 2-phosphoglycolate — a **toxic two-carbon fragment** the cycle cannot use.

```
photorespiration: RuBisCO + O₂ → phosphoglycolate
                      → salvage pathway across THREE organelles
                        (chloroplast → peroxisome → mitochondrion)
                      → releases CO₂, CONSUMES ATP and reducing power
                      → fixes nothing — pure loss
```

**It is called photorespiration because it resembles respiration** (consumes O₂, releases CO₂) but occurs in the light and wastes energy. On a hot day, a C3 plant can lose **25–30% of fixed carbon** to it.

### Evolution's fixes: C4 and CAM

| Strategy | Solution | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C3** (wheat, rice, most trees) | Nothing — fix CO₂ directly, suffer photorespiration | Temperate, wetter conditions |
| **C4** (maize, sugarcane) | **Spatial separation:** PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ → 4C acid in **mesophyll**, shipped to **bundle sheath** cells where CO₂ is released around RuBisCO — a **CO₂-concentrating pump** | Hot, high light |
| **CAM** (cactus, pineapple) | **Temporal separation:** stomata open **at night** to fix CO₂ into malate; stored in vacuole; released **by day** with stomata closed | Arid — water conservation first |

**The anatomy betrays the biochemistry:** C4 plants have **Kranz anatomy** — a wreath of bundle sheath cells ringed by mesophyll — visible under a microscope. Structure → function again: two cell types for two fixation jobs.

**C4/CAM cost extra ATP** to concentrate CO₂, and lose in cool, wet conditions where photorespiration was never costly — which is why **C3 plants still dominate most of the biosphere**. Evolutionary trade-off, not progress: both strategies persist.

## Regulation: the cycle only runs in the light

The Calvin cycle's enzymes are **switched on by light**, through three linked mechanisms:

| Signal | Mechanism | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **pH rise** | H⁺ pumped into lumen leaves stroma alkaline | Activates RuBisCO activase and other enzymes |
| **Mg²⁺ influx** | Follows charge balance into stroma | RuBisCO requires Mg²⁺ |
| **Thioredoxin reduction** | Ferredoxin (reduced, light-only) reduces thioredoxin → breaks disulfide bonds in target enzymes | **Deactivates** the dark-enzyme **RuBisCO oxygenase side** and *activates* Calvin enzymes |

**The elegant point:** the cycle cannot run in the dark because its enzymes are *off*, not because ATP is missing — and they are switched off by the same light-driven electron flow that makes the ATP. **The two halves share a control signal**, so the cell never spends ATP fixing carbon it has no power to continue fixing.

**Also light-regulated:** starch synthesis in the chloroplast, and the ** stomatal opening** that lets CO₂ in (guard cell photophosphorylation — chapter 05 of this section's structure material).

## Linking back to respiration

```
PHOTOSYNTHESIS                    RESPIRATION
6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light ──▶ sugar + O₂     sugar + O₂ ──▶ 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ATP
      (store energy)                            (release energy)
            ▲                                        │
            └────────── same molecules, reversed ─────┘
```

**A plant does both, continuously.** Chloroplasts make the fuel; mitochondria burn it — including in the dark, and in every non-green tissue (roots, seeds). Plants release CO₂ at night; leaves consume O₂ as well as producing it. The two processes are not plant-vs-animal — they are **storage-vs-spending**, and every eukaryotic cell does the second while plants add the first.

## Medical and practical relevance

**Agriculture is applied photosynthesis.** Yield per acre tracks RuBisCO's inefficiency and photorespiratory loss — which is why **engineering RuBisCO**, introducing C4 pathways into C3 crops, and improving stomatal behaviour are leading goals of crop science. Feeding a rising population is, chemically, a RuBisCO problem.

**Oxygen atmosphere = a photosynthetic legacy.** The O₂ in air is entirely biogenic, produced by photolysis over billions of years. The **Great Oxidation Event** reshaped life and is discussed in 06 — Evolution; here it is worth noticing that oxygen began as a **waste product**, and that the oxidative metabolism of chapter 07 only became possible because that waste accumulated.

**Photoprotection failure = photodamage.** Excess light generates reactive oxygen species in the antenna; carotenoids and cyclic flow dissipate it. In humans, **macular pigment** (lutein, zeaxanthin — dietary carotenoids) performs the same quenching in the retina, which is why dietary carotenoids are associated with eye health.

**Chloroplast-derived biotechnology:** the organelle's own genome and high expression capacity make it a production system for vaccines and therapeutic proteins (mentioned in 02 — Cell Biology) — photosynthesis machinery recruited as a factory.

## Key facts

- Overall: **6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂**; **O₂ comes from water photolysis**; carbon comes from CO₂.
- Two stages in two compartments: **light reactions (thylakoid membrane)** make **ATP, NADPH, O₂**; **Calvin cycle (stroma)** uses them to fix CO₂ into **G3P**.
- **Electron flow: PSII (P680) → PQ → cyt b₆f → PC → PSI (P700) → ferredoxin → NADPH.** PSII comes first despite its name.
- **Photophosphorylation:** H⁺ accumulated in the lumen (by the chain and by water splitting) flows back through ATP synthase, lumen → stroma.
- **Cyclic electron flow** tops up ATP without making NADPH or O₂ (Calvin needs 3 ATP : 2 NADPH).
- Calvin: **fixation (RuBisCO) → reduction (ATP, NADPH) → regeneration (ATP)**; 3 turns = 1 net G3P; 6 turns = 1 glucose.
- **RuBisCO is slow and oxygenates** → **photorespiration** wastes up to ~30% of fixed carbon in hot, dry conditions.
- **C4 = spatial CO₂ pump (Kranz anatomy); CAM = temporal (night-time fixation)** — both concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO at extra ATP cost.
- Calvin enzymes are **light-activated** (stromal pH, Mg²⁺, thioredoxin) — the cycle is off in the dark by design.
- Plants run **both** photosynthesis and respiration; only plants (and phototrophs) run the former.

## Practice questions

**1. The oxygen released during photosynthesis is derived from**

A. Carbon dioxide
B. Glucose
C. Water
D. Chlorophyll

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Photosystem II splits water (photolysis) to replace the electrons it loses to light, releasing O₂ as a by-product — the reaction centre's electron deficit is the whole reason water is cracked. CO₂'s oxygen ends up in sugar and in regenerated water during respiration; glucose and chlorophyll are not oxygen sources.

---

**2. Which sequence correctly orders linear electron flow?**

A. PSI → PSII → cytochrome b₆f
B. PSII → cytochrome b₆f → PSI → NADP⁺ reduction
C. PSII → PSI → cytochrome b₆f
D. Cytochrome b₆f → PSII → PSI

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Water supplies electrons to PSII (P680); they pass through plastoquinone and the cytochrome b₆f complex (which pumps H⁺), then plastocyanin to PSI (P700), and finally to ferredoxin and NADP⁺ reductase. The numbering of the photosystems is historical — PSII functions first.

---

**3. Where is the proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis in chloroplasts established?**

A. Across the outer chloroplast envelope
B. Across the thylakoid membrane, with H⁺ accumulated in the lumen
C. Across the plasma membrane
D. Across the nuclear envelope

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Cytochrome b₆f pumps H⁺ into the thylakoid lumen and water splitting releases H⁺ there too; ATP synthase lets them flow back into the stroma. The gradient is therefore thylakoid lumen (high) vs stroma (low) — the chloroplast's version of chemiosmosis with reversed plumbing relative to mitochondria.

---

**4. The enzyme RuBisCO catalyses**

A. The splitting of water
B. The fixation of CO₂ onto ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate
C. The reduction of NADP⁺
D. The oxidation of glucose

**Answer: B**

Explanation: RuBisCO carboxylates RuBP, producing two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate — the entry point of carbon into the Calvin cycle. Water splitting is photosystem II's job; NADP⁺ reduction is ferredoxin–NADP⁺ reductase; glucose oxidation is respiration.

---

**5. Photorespiration occurs because**

A. Plants respire only at night
B. RuBisCO can bind O₂ instead of CO₂, producing a toxic two-carbon compound that is salvaged at a cost in ATP and released CO₂
C. The Calvin cycle stops in light
D. Stomata release oxygen at night

**Answer: B**

Explanation: RuBisCO's oxygenase side is favoured when O₂ is high and CO₂ low — typically when stomata close on hot days. The phosphoglycolate produced cannot enter the Calvin cycle and must be salvaged across three organelles, consuming energy and losing fixed carbon. It is not nocturnal respiration (A) nor a light-independent process (C).

---

**6. A C4 plant differs from a C3 plant in that it**

A. Performs no Calvin cycle
B. Uses PEP carboxylase in mesophyll cells to concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells, minimising photorespiration
C. Fixes carbon only at night
D. Lacks RuBisCO entirely

**Answer: B**

Explanation: C4 plants separate initial fixation (PEP carboxylase, no oxygenase activity) from the Calvin cycle in space — Kranz anatomy delivers CO₂-rich acid to bundle sheath cells, so RuBisCO sees high CO₂ and rarely binds O₂. Option C describes CAM plants (temporal separation); both C4 and CAM retain RuBisCO.

---

**7. What is the main role of cyclic electron flow around PSI?**

A. To produce NADPH and O₂
B. To generate ATP without additional NADPH, balancing the 3:2 ATP:NADPH requirement of the Calvin cycle
C. To split water
D. To fix carbon dioxide

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Linear flow gives roughly equal ATP and NADPH, but the Calvin cycle needs 3 ATP per 2 NADPH. Cyclic flow returns electrons from ferredoxin to the cytochrome b₆f complex, pumping protons and making ATP while producing no NADPH and consuming no water — a tuning mechanism for the energy ratio.

---

**8. Six turns of the Calvin cycle produce how much net usable carbohydrate?**

A. One glucose directly
B. One net molecule of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (three carbons)
C. Six glucose molecules
D. Six RuBP

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Each turn fixes one CO₂; three turns' worth of G3P equals three carbons — one net G3P after regeneration consumes the rest. Six turns fix six carbons, which corresponds to one glucose, but the direct export product of the cycle is G3P, which the cell then uses to build glucose or other molecules.

---

**9. Why does a plant cell require both chloroplasts and mitochondria?**

A. Chloroplasts make all the cell's ATP; mitochondria are vestigial
B. Chloroplasts store energy in sugar; mitochondria oxidise that sugar to produce ATP for cellular work
C. Mitochondria fix carbon; chloroplasts respire
D. Only one functions at a time, alternating day and night

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Photosynthesis's ATP and NADPH are spent inside the chloroplast on carbon fixation; they are not exported to power the rest of the cell. The sugar produced is the currency that mitochondria convert to ATP — which is why plants respire continuously, including at night, and why non-green tissues depend entirely on imported sugar.

---

**10. Which observation would best support that accessory pigments contribute to photosynthesis?**

A. Chlorophyll a absorbs red light
B. The action spectrum extends into wavelengths absorbed by carotenoids and chlorophyll b, where chlorophyll a absorbs poorly
C. Plants appear green
D. Oxygen is evolved at photosystem II

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The action spectrum measures actual photosynthetic rate at each wavelength; where it exceeds chlorophyll a's absorption spectrum — in the blue-green region absorbed by carotenoids — energy captured by accessory pigments must be reaching the reaction centre. Absorption alone (A) doesn't prove use; colour (C) is reflected light; oxygen evolution (D) shows the water-splitting step, not the antenna's contribution.
