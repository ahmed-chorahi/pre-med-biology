# 11 — Reproduction & Development

**Two specialised cells become one, and one cell becomes a body.** This section follows the events that start a human: how meiosis is completed differently in the two sexes, how a single sperm is admitted and all others are blocked, how the first week is spent dividing without growing, how three germ layers set the map for every tissue in section 09, and how a placenta feeds, gasses, and immunologically tolerates a fetus that carries half someone else's antigens. It is the place where meiosis, gene regulation, tissues, and the immune system all reappear in sequence.

**Prerequisites:** [03 — Cellular Processes](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md), which must be understood before gametogenesis means anything — crossing over and independent assortment are what these chapters use; [04 — Genetics](../04-genetics/), for the inheritance the zygote receives; [09 — Human Biology](../09-human-biology/), for the tissue types that differentiation produces; and [10 — Anatomy & Physiology](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/), whose [endocrine](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/04-endocrine-system.md) and [reproductive](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/10-reproductive-system.md) chapters supply the organs and hormone loops these events run on.

**What this section gives you that a developmental timeline does not:** why maternal age raises aneuploidy risk (oocytes sit in prophase I for decades, so the spindle must hold); why the fast and slow blocks to polyspermy exist at all (a triploid cell cannot divide); the germ-layer fate map that tells you instantly which tissue comes from where; why the placenta expresses a non-classical MHC instead of none at all; and why the weeks of organogenesis are the window in which teratogens do structural damage — the timing logic behind every reproductive-medicine decision.
