# Placenta and Maternal–Fetal Exchange

## Why it matters

The **placenta** is the only organ a human builds and then discards, and it is built almost entirely from **foetal** tissue. It exists because four engineering problems must be solved simultaneously: **exchange without mixing** — two blood supplies, bearing antigens from two different genomes, must trade over a huge surface while staying physically separate; **waste removal without working kidneys** — foetal renal function is negligible yet the foetus generates urea, creatinine, CO₂ and bilirubin continuously; **endocrine support** — progesterone must be made from implantation onward, the myometrium kept quiescent, and maternal metabolism redirected toward the foetus; and **immunological tolerance of a semi-allogeneic graft** — the foetus carries **50% paternal antigens** and should be rejected like a stranger's kidney.

Medicine watches this organ closely because its failures are common and specific. **Pre-eclampsia** is failed remodelling of the maternal spiral arteries; **placenta praevia** is implantation over the internal os; **TORCH infections** are classified by whether the agent crosses it; **erythroblastosis fetalis** is caused by the one antibody class that does cross; and every drug used in pregnancy is judged by a single rule — *if it is lipid-soluble, it crosses*. Structure, transport, immunology and endocrinology cannot be separated here: the pathology is the physiology with one step removed.

## Structure: villi, intervillous spaces, and two circuits that never mix

### What the placenta is made of

The placenta descends from the **trophoblast**, the first cell fate decision at the blastocyst stage ([03 — Early development and cleavage](03-early-development-and-cleavage.md)). Two partners complete it: the **chorion** (trophoblast plus a mesodermal core, into which foetal vessels grow) and the maternal **decidua basalis**.

| Region | Fate | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Villi overlying decidua basalis | Anchor and proliferate → **chorion frondosum** ("leafy") | Exchange happens where maternal blood is |
| Villi elsewhere | Regress by mid-gestation → **chorion laeve** ("smooth") | No maternal blood supply to trade with |
| Chorion frondosum + decidua basalis | Fuse into the **discoid placenta** (~22 cm, ~500 g at term) | One compact exchange organ, delivered after the baby |

```
PRIMARY villus      trophoblast shell + mesodermal core
      ↓ cytotrophoblast grows inward
SECONDARY villus    mesenchymal core expands
      ↓ fetal capillaries grow in (~week 4-5)
TERTIARY villus     CORE NOW CONTAINS FETAL BLOOD VESSELS
      ↓ branching, thinning, surface folding
TERMINAL villi      functional UNITS of exchange (~60 million at term)
```

### The membrane the blood actually crosses

Maternal blood never touches a foetal capillary — it touches the **syncytiotrophoblast**, and a substance must cross every layer beneath it:

| Layer | Origin | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Syncytiotrophoblast** | Fused trophoblast — a continuous multinucleate **syncytium** with microvilli | Barrier and transporter; secretes hCG and hPL; carries **FcRn** for IgG |
| **Cytotrophoblast** | Proliferating progenitor beneath it | Present early, **nearly absent at term** — its loss thins the barrier |
| Mesenchymal core | Extra-embryonic mesoderm with **Hofbauer macrophages** | Structural support |
| **Foetal capillary endothelium** | Extra-embryonic mesoderm | Final layer before foetal blood |

Diffusion distance falls from **~25 µm at 8–10 weeks to ~3.5 µm at term**, because flux varies inversely with thickness ([02 — Passive transport](../03-cellular-processes/02-passive-transport.md)). Maternal blood flows *between* the villi, so the human placenta is **haemochorial** — maternal blood in direct contact with foetal tissue.

### Spiral artery remodelling: why the inflow is a trickle, not a jet

Spiral arteries in a non-pregnant uterus are coiled, muscular and high-resistance; unconverted, they would deliver arterial-pressure blood into a fragile villous tree.

```
ENDOVASCULAR TROPHOBLAST invades the artery wall (weeks 8-16)
        ↓ replaces endothelium and smooth muscle
ARTERIES BECOME FLACID, DILATED TUBES — no muscle, no elasticity
        ↓ resistance falls; flow reaches ~500-600 mL/min at term
SLOW JETS POOL IN THE INTERVILLOUS SPACE, drained by decidual veins
LOW-PRESSURE, HIGH-FLOW PERFUSION → exchange without vessel damage
```

This is the most clinically consequential step in placentation; its failure causes pre-eclampsia.

### The two circulations: parallel, never joined

| | **Maternal circuit** | **Foetal circuit** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supply | Uterine arteries → **remodelled spiral arteries** | **Two umbilical arteries** (from the internal iliacs) |
| Exchange site | **Intervillous space** — a pool bathing the villi | **Villous capillaries** inside each terminal villus |
| Return | Decidual **uterine veins** → maternal systemic blood | **One umbilical vein** → ductus venosus / portal → foetal heart |
| Mixing | **Never** | **Never** — foetal blood stays in a closed circuit |

The maternal side is a pool continuously washed out; the foetal side is a closed loop threaded through it. No erythrocyte crosses in either direction — only gases, solutes and specifically transported molecules.

### The umbilical cord: two arteries, one vein

The cord is ~55 cm of **Wharton's jelly** (mucoid tissue resisting compression) carrying three vessels in a helix:

| Vessel | Number | Direction | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Umbilical vein** | **1** | Placenta → foetus | **Oxygenated**, nutrient- and IgG-rich blood |
| **Umbilical arteries** | **2** | Foetus → placenta | **Deoxygenated**, CO₂- and waste-laden blood |

**Why this looks backwards:** vessels are named by **direction relative to the heart, not by oxygen content**. The exchange organ in the foetus is the placenta, so the vessel returning *from* it *toward* the heart is a vein and carries oxygenated blood, while the vessels leaving the heart *for* it are arteries and carry deoxygenated blood — exactly the inversion of the pulmonary circulation, and the clearest proof that **arteries mean "away from the heart", not "oxygenated"**. Two arteries rather than one double the return flow, and their alternating pulse wave compresses the single vein, augmenting placental perfusion.

### Diffusion across the placental membrane: Fick's law again

```
        surface area × diffusion coefficient × partial-pressure difference
flux ∝ ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                     membrane thickness
```

| Term | Placental adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| **Area** | ~60 million terminal villi with microvilli — **~10–12 m²** of exchange surface |
| **Thickness** | Loss of cytotrophoblast, capillaries pushed to the villous edge → **~3.5 µm** |
| **Gradient** | Intervillous pO₂ ~40–50 mmHg vs umbilical vein ~30–35 mmHg |
| **Flow** | Both circuits continuously refreshed, so the gradient never flattens |

Maternal blood *pools* rather than running true counter-current, which is why fetal blood never fully equilibrates (umbilical vein pO₂ stays ~30–35 mmHg). The same law as alveolar gas exchange, with surface area and thinness substituted for speed.

## What crosses, what does not

### The master table

| Substance | Route | Energy / gradient | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **O₂, CO₂** | Simple diffusion through the lipid bilayer | Down the partial-pressure gradient | Fetal pO₂ never reaches maternal arterial levels |
| **Glucose** | **Facilitated diffusion** via GLUT1/GLUT3 | Down maternal → fetal gradient, **no ATP** | Foetal glucose is always ~10–15% **lower** — the foetus is a glucose sink |
| **Amino acids** | **Active transport** (Na⁺-coupled system A and L) | **Against** the gradient, costs ATP | Foetal concentrations exceed maternal by 20–100% |
| **Fatty acids** | Free diffusion plus carrier uptake | Down gradient; maternal lipids rise late | Late-gestation fuel; early gestation runs on glucose |
| **IgG** | **FcRn receptor-mediated transcytosis** | Active, saturable, mainly **after week 28** | Passive immunity for ~3–6 months after birth |
| **IgM, IgA, IgD, IgE** | **Do not cross** | — | IgM in cord blood means the **foetus** made it → intrauterine infection |
| **Water, Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻** | Bulk flow and diffusion through aquaporins | Osmotic and hydrostatic | Continuous exchange with amniotic fluid |
| **Urea, creatinine, unconjugated bilirubin** | Passive diffusion | Down foetal → maternal gradient | The placenta is the fetal kidney; mother conjugates the bilirubin |
| **Lipid-soluble drugs** | Free diffusion (unbound, un-ionised, small) | Down gradient | **Thalidomide, alcohol, tetracycline, antiretrovirals** reach foetal blood at maternal levels |
| **Proteins, protein-bound drugs, maternal cells** | Do not cross (or only the free fraction) | — | The two bloods stay separate |

### Oxygen: foetal haemoglobin and the double Bohr effect

**Foetal haemoglobin (HbF, α₂γ₂)** binds **2,3-BPG** far less avidly than adult HbA, so the deoxygenated state is less stabilised and the dissociation curve shifts **left** — higher affinity, which is what lets it load O₂ across a gradient much smaller than an adult lung's.

```
AT THE PLACENTA   maternal Hb shifted RIGHT by CO₂/H⁺ → RELEASES O₂
                  fetal Hb shifted LEFT by losing CO₂  → BINDS O₂ avidly
AT THE TISSUES    fetal Hb shifted RIGHT by CO₂/H⁺ (Bohr effect) → RELEASES O₂
```

This reciprocal is the **double Bohr effect**: fetal blood giving up CO₂ becomes alkaline and loads O₂ while maternal blood receiving that CO₂ becomes acidic and unloads it. The **Haldane effect** completes the coupling — oxygenated Hb carries less CO₂, so as HbF becomes oxygenated at the placenta it sheds CO₂ for transfer. Oxygen delivery and CO₂ removal are one coupled system, not two.

### Glucose and amino acids: the two gradients

Glucose is polar, so the syncytiotrophoblast carries it by **facilitated diffusion** with saturation kinetics and no ATP. Because the foetus consumes glucose greedily, **fetal glucose is always below maternal glucose**, and that gradient alone drives transfer — there is no pump that could work against it, so a fasting or hyperemotic mother transports less and the foetus is affected accordingly. Amino acids are the exception: Na⁺-coupled **active transport** accumulates them against the gradient at the placenta's own metabolic expense, so foetal concentrations are *higher* than maternal ones. Growth requires importing material against equilibrium, which is why it must be paid for ([03 — Active transport](../03-cellular-processes/03-active-transport.md)).

### IgG: the one antibody that crosses

**IgG is actively transported, not filtered.** The syncytiotrophoblast expresses **FcRn**, which binds IgG Fc in the acidic endosome and releases it on the foetal side at neutral pH.

| Antibody | Crosses? | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **IgG** (IgG1 > IgG2 > IgG3 > IgG4) | **Yes** — FcRn transcytosis, accelerating after week 28 | Passive immunity, half-life ~3 weeks, mostly gone by **6 months** |
| **IgM** (pentamer) | No — too large, not a substrate | IgM in cord blood = foetal synthesis = intrauterine infection |
| **IgA** | No | Mucosal protection arrives later in breast milk instead |
| **IgE, IgD** | No | No transport pathway |

This is passive immunity with an expiry date, which is why vaccination in pregnancy (pertussis, influenza) is timed for the last weeks — that is when transfer is greatest.

### Drugs and waste

```
small + lipid soluble + un-ionised + not protein bound
        ↓  FREE DIFFUSION ACROSS THE PLACENTA
fetal plasma concentration ≈ maternal concentration
```

Protein binding offers partial protection (only the free fraction crosses) and the placenta has some CYP3A4 and glutathione-S-transferase activity, but this barrier is far weaker than the liver's. Prescribing in pregnancy therefore assumes guilt until proven innocent. Waste runs the other way: urea, creatinine and **unconjugated bilirubin** leave passively, and because the fetal liver conjugates bilirubin poorly, the mother conjugates and excretes it for the foetus — protection that vanishes when the cord is cut, which is why physiological jaundice of the newborn follows directly from birth.

## Immunological tolerance of a semi-allogeneic graft

The foetus expresses **paternal HLA molecules** the mother's T cells have never encountered, sits in contact with maternal blood and decidual leukocytes for nine months, and is not rejected. Rejection is what the immune system *should* do, so tolerance here is actively manufactured.

| Mechanism | Molecule / cell | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No classical class I** | **HLA-A and HLA-B absent** from villous trophoblast | Nothing for CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells to recognise |
| **No class II** | **HLA-DR/DP/DQ absent** | Trophoblast cannot present antigen to CD4⁺ helper T cells |
| **Non-classical HLA-G** | **HLA-G**, minimally polymorphic → essentially invariant | Engages inhibitory receptors **KIR2DL4, ILT2/ILT4** on decidual NK cells and macrophages |
| **HLA-E** | Non-classical HLA-E | Engages **NKG2A** on NK cells — a "do not attack" signal |
| **Decidual NK cells** | CD56^bright CD16⁻ uterine NK | Poorly cytotoxic; secrete VEGF, PlGF, MMP-9 → angiogenesis and controlled invasion |
| **Regulatory T cells** | **CD4⁺CD25⁺FoxP3⁺ Tregs**, expanded by paternal antigen | Suppress effector T cells locally and systemically; low numbers associate with recurrent loss |
| **IDO** | **Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase** in syncytiotrophoblast | Depletes local **tryptophan** → T cells arrest and die; kynurenines add suppression |
| **Checkpoint** | **PD-L1** on trophoblast → **PD-1** on T cells | Anergy, exhaustion, apoptosis |
| **Complement control** | **CD46, CD55, CD59** | Blocks maternal complement at the interface |
| **Implantation window** | Pro-inflammatory cytokines, macrophages, IL-10, TGF-β, Th2 bias | Implantation is *inflammatory by design* in a narrow window, then resolves into tolerance |

### The parallel with pathogen immune evasion — and the difference

[06 — Host–pathogen interaction](../07-microbiology/06-host-pathogen-interaction.md) catalogues microbes that survive by hiding from recognition: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* blocks phagosome–lysosome fusion, *Listeria* escapes the vacuole, *Plasmodium* hides inside erythrocytes where there is no MHC at all, herpesviruses go latent. The trophoblast uses the same family of moves — **remove the molecule the immune system would recognise, occupy the receptors that would trigger attack, switch off the cells that arrive** — but three differences separate physiology from evasion: it is **local** (the mother remains immunocompetent elsewhere), **regulated and reversible** (hormone-driven, ending at delivery), and **non-destructive** (it suppresses effectors rather than killing them). When the regulation fails or overshoots, the result is disease — pre-eclampsia, recurrent loss — which is why tolerance is a controlled state, not a passive absence.

## Endocrine function of the placenta

| Hormone | Source | Primary function | Clinical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **hCG** | **Syncytiotrophoblast** from ~day 6–8 | Rescues the corpus luteum → progesterone continues; supports foetal gonads | **Pregnancy test** (β-subunit immunoassay); higher in twins; marker of **trophoblastic disease**; induces ovulation |
| **hPL** | Syncytiotrophoblast, rises through gestation | **Maternal insulin resistance** + lipolysis → frees glucose for the foetus; mammary development | No routine use; reflects placental mass |
| **Progesterone** | Corpus luteum → **placenta** after the luteo-placental shift | **Myometrial quiescence**: blocks oxytocin receptors, gap junctions and Ca²⁺ entry; maintains endometrium | Serum levels in early pregnancy; progestogens used (mixed evidence) against preterm labour |
| **Oestrogens** (oestradiol, **oestriol**) | Placenta (aromatase) + **foetal** precursors — the **feto-placental unit** | Myometrial growth, uterine blood flow, late oxytocin-receptor upregulation | Falling **oestriol** can signal foetal compromise |
| **Relaxin** | Corpus luteum and placenta | Softens cervix, remodels pubic symphysis collagen, inhibits early contractions | Studied for preterm-labour prevention |
| **Placental CRH** | Syncytiotrophoblast | Exponential late rise → fetal ACTH/cortisol → lung maturation; the "**placental clock**" | Marker of timing of labour |

### hCG and the luteo-placental shift

```
IMPLANTATION (day 6-8) → syncytiotrophoblast secretes hCG
        ↓ binds the LH receptor on the CORPUS LUTEUM
LUTEUM RESCUED → progesterone continues → no menstruation
        ↓ weeks 8-12: placental steroidogenesis ramps up
LUTEO-PLACENTAL SHIFT → placenta makes progesterone itself
        ↓ hCG falls to a plateau; progesterone rises steadily to term
```

Before week 8 the pregnancy **depends on the corpus luteum**, so luteal failure means miscarriage — hence early progesterone support. Because hCG peaks at weeks 8–10 then falls, a value low for dates suggests failing trophoblast, ectopic pregnancy or a molar pregnancy, while an abnormally high value suggests trophoblastic disease or twins; specificity comes from assaying the **β subunit**. Progesterone holds the myometrium quiet by **downregulating oxytocin receptors, preventing gap-junction formation and lowering intracellular calcium**, so muscle cannot coordinate into contractions — the reason loss of that signal is one trigger of term labour.

## Fetal circulation shunts

The foetus does not breathe, eat or excrete — the placenta does all three. Lungs are fluid-filled and high-resistance, used only for growth (~10% of combined output), and the liver receives oxygenated blood it does not need to process. Three **right-to-left shunts** route blood past them.

| Shunt | Location | Bypasses | Closure at birth | Adult remnant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ductus venosus** | Umbilical vein → IVC | **Liver** | Cord clamp removes inflow; vasoconstricts | **Ligamentum venosum** |
| **Foramen ovale** | Right → left atrium | **Lungs** | Cord clamp + lung aeration → LV pressure exceeds RV → flap shut | **Fossa ovalis** |
| **Ductus arteriosus** | Pulmonary artery → aorta | **Lungs** | Rising pO₂ and falling prostaglandins constrict it | **Ligamentum arteriosum** |

```
PLACENTA (gas exchange) ──umbilical vein──▶ heart ──▶ body
        ▲                                    │
        └── two umbilical arteries ◀── aorta ┘
   all three shunts run RIGHT → LEFT: blood reaches the body
   without passing the non-functional lungs

BIRTH: cord clamped + lungs inflate
   systemic resistance ↑, pulmonary resistance ↓, pO₂ ↑, PGE₂ falls
   → foramen ovale closes, ductus arteriosus constricts, ductus venosus collapses
```

Because the ductus stays open partly through prostaglandins, **NSAIDs** are used deliberately to close a patent ductus arteriosus in preterm infants — and the same property is why NSAIDs given to the mother after mid-gestation risk constricting it prematurely. Structural detail belongs to [05 — Cardiovascular system](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/05-cardiovascular-system.md); the principle is that **all three shunts are right-to-left because the placenta, not the lung, is the exchange organ.**

## Maternal adaptations to pregnancy

| Adaptation | Mechanism | Purpose | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blood volume ↑30–50%** | Plasma ↑40–50%, red-cell mass ↑20–30%; oestrogen-driven erythropoietin, RAAS | High-flow state for the uteroplacental circuit; reserve against delivery haemorrhage | **Dilutional anaemia** — haemoglobin concentration falls though total red-cell mass rises |
| **Cardiac output ↑30–50%** | Stroke volume rises first, heart rate later | Feeds the enlarged circulation | Palpitations, flow murmurs |
| **Systemic vascular resistance ↓** | Progesterone, NO, prostacyclin, plus the placental bed itself | Blood pressure stays low despite extra output | BP nadir ~24 weeks; orthostatic dizziness |
| **Insulin resistance** | hPL, cortisol, placental GH, progesterone | **Spares glucose for the foetus**; mother runs on fat | Fasting hypoglycaemia, postprandial hyperglycaemia; gestational diabetes if compensation fails |
| **Hypercoagulability** | Fibrinogen ~2×, factors VII/VIII/X and vWF ↑, protein S ↓, **PAI-1/2** suppress fibrinolysis | Immediate haostasis when the placenta separates | **Pregnancy is a thrombotic state** — DVT and PE risk rise sharply |
| **Respiratory alkalosis** | Progesterone-driven hyperventilation → PaCO₂ ~30 mmHg | **Widens the maternal–foetal CO₂ gradient** | Subjective breathlessness |
| **Leukocytosis, GFR ↑30–50%** | Physiological; dilated renal pelvis | Clears foetal waste; preparedness for delivery | Must not be misread as infection; pyelonephritis risk rises |

The hypercoagulability is the clearest example of a useful adaptation turning dangerous: fibrinogen nearly doubles and fibrinolysis is suppressed so that placental separation is met with instant haemostasis — and immobility, caesarean section and pre-eclampsia then compound a several-fold rise in venous thromboembolism. All of it is ordinary feedback control ([03 — Homeostasis control loop](../09-human-biology/03-homeostasis-control-loop.md)) run through the hormonal axes of [04 — Endocrine system](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/04-endocrine-system.md).

## Medical relevance

**TORCH infections are defined by placental crossing.** Whether an agent causes congenital disease depends on whether it can cross the membrane, and on gestational age at exposure — the placenta is the tissue that decides, in the sense of [06 — Host–pathogen interaction](../07-microbiology/06-host-pathogen-interaction.md).

| Agent | Crosses? | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***Toxoplasma gondii*** | **Yes** | Chorioretinitis, intracranial calcifications, hydrocephalus, hepatosplenomegaly; often silent at birth with later chorioretinitis — [04 — Fungi and protozoa](../07-microbiology/04-fungi-and-protozoa.md) |
| **Rubella** | **Yes**, worst in first 12 weeks | Congenital rubella: cataracts, sensorineural deafness, patent ductus arteriosus, intellectual disability |
| **CMV** | **Yes** | **Commonest** congenital infection: sensorineural hearing loss (may be the only sign), microcephaly, periventricular calcifications |
| ***Treponema pallidum*** | **Yes**, any trimester | Congenital syphilis: stillbirth, hydrops, snuffles, Hutchinson teeth, saddle nose, sabre shins — hence antenatal screening |
| **HSV** | **Rarely** | Risk is **perinatal**: neonatal HSV from vaginal delivery → caesarean if lesions active |
| **HIV** | **Yes**, 15–45% untreated | Under 1% with antiretrovirals, caesarean delivery and managed feeding |
| **Most bacteria** (except *Listeria*, syphilis, Lyme) | No | Confined to decidua and amniotic fluid → preterm labour rather than congenital disease |

**Rh incompatibility and erythroblastosis fetalis are IgG pathology.** An Rh-negative mother carrying an Rh-positive foetus (the D antigen is a dominant Mendelian trait — [05 — Human inheritance patterns](../04-genetics/05-human-inheritance-patterns.md)) usually tolerates the first pregnancy: fetal red cells — an antigen-bearing **connective tissue cell type** ([01 — Four tissue types](../09-human-biology/01-four-tissue-types.md)) — leak into maternal blood mainly at delivery, and the mother responds with IgM first and then **anti-D IgG**. IgM cannot cross, so the first baby escapes; the memory now exists. In a second Rh-positive pregnancy that **IgG crosses via FcRn**, coats foetal erythrocytes and causes immune haemolysis → anaemia, jaundice, **hydrops fetalis** from high-output failure, or **kernicterus** once the placenta's bilirubin clearance is removed at birth. Prevention is elegant: **anti-D immunoglobulin** at ~28 weeks and within 72 hours of delivery (and after any sensitising event) binds and clears fetal Rh-positive cells *before* the mother's own IgG response can start — it prevents sensitisation and cannot reverse it. ABO incompatibility causes only mild jaundice because naturally occurring anti-A/anti-B are mainly IgM.

**Pre-eclampsia is failed spiral artery remodelling expressed as maternal disease.** When endovascular trophoblast does not convert those arteries they stay narrow and high-resistance → intermittent perfusion → villous ischaemia and reperfusion → shedding of syncytial debris and release of **sFlt-1** (a soluble VEGF/PlGF trap) and soluble endoglin → maternal endothelial dysfunction → **new-onset hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria**. Severe disease gives HELLP syndrome, eclampsia and stroke, and the foetus may be growth-restricted because the same failed remodelling starves it. Risk factors map onto the mechanism: first pregnancy, multiple gestation, obesity, chronic hypertension, previous affected pregnancy. **Delivery is the only definitive treatment**; low-dose aspirin from the first trimester improves placentation in high-risk women, and magnesium sulphate prevents eclampsia.

**Placenta praevia is a placement problem.** Implantation over or near the **internal os** puts the placenta in the path of delivery. The sign is **painless, bright-red bleeding** in the second or third trimester — painless because nothing ruptures, only the placenta shears as the lower segment stretches. Risks mirror implantation history: previous caesarean section, multiparity, advanced maternal age. Diagnosis is by **ultrasound, never digital examination**; if it persists to term, delivery is caesarean.

**Every drug in pregnancy is judged by lipid solubility,** because free diffusion carries unbound, lipid-soluble molecules across and exposure equals maternal dosing. **Thalidomide** caused phocomelia only during the limb-bud window (~days 20–36) and is harmless outside it — proof that *timing* and *crossing* are separate questions. **Alcohol** crosses freely at every stage with no safe amount: fetal alcohol spectrum disorder causes growth restriction, microcephaly, facial dysmorphism and lifelong intellectual disability. **Tetracyclines** chelate calcium in developing bone and teeth after mid-gestation; **NSAIDs** after 20 weeks constrict the fetal ductus and cause oligohydramnios; **ACE inhibitors/ARBs** cause fetal renal failure; **warfarin** in the first trimester causes nasal hypoplasia; **retinoids** are frankly teratogenic. Antiretrovirals show the balance: theoretical fetal risk against preventing maternal progression and transmission, so they are given. The timing logic is the teratogen schedule of [03 — Early development and cleavage](03-early-development-and-cleavage.md).

**The placenta ages, and term pregnancy is limited by that ageing.** Exchange surface peaks near 36 weeks then declines — syncytial knots increase, fibrin is deposited on villi, infarcts accumulate, the membrane thickens as villi scar — while fetal demand keeps rising. That is the physiological reason post-term pregnancy carries rising risks of oligohydramnios, meconium-stained liquor, stillbirth and macrosomia, and the reason **term is defined at all**. The placenta cannot be repaired or regenerated and fails the same way every time, so antenatal surveillance is essentially monitoring a consumable: at term, all four engineering problems are being solved a little less well than a fortnight earlier.

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "The umbilical vein carries deoxygenated blood like other veins" | Vessels are named by **direction relative to the heart**. The umbilical vein runs placenta → foetus (toward the heart) and carries **oxygenated** blood; the two umbilical arteries run away from it and carry deoxygenated blood, as the pulmonary arteries do. |
| "Maternal and foetal blood mix in the placenta" | They **never mix** — the circuits run in parallel, separated by the placental membrane; only dissolved or specifically transported substances cross. |
| "The placenta is a maternal organ" | It is **foetal**: trophoblast plus extra-embryonic mesoderm, with only the decidua basalis contributed by the mother. |
| "All maternal antibodies cross, giving the baby immunity" | **Only IgG crosses**, via FcRn; IgM and IgA do not. The transferred IgG decays and is mostly gone by six months. |
| "Foetal and adult haemoglobin are interchangeable" | **HbF binds 2,3-BPG poorly** → left-shifted curve → higher O₂ affinity, needed to load at the placenta; the Bohr effect allows unloading at the tissues. |
| "Arteries always carry oxygenated blood" | Arteries carry blood **away from the heart** — pulmonary and umbilical arteries are deoxygenated. |
| "Glucose crosses by active transport, so the foetus gets glucose even when the mother fasts" | Glucose crosses by **facilitated diffusion down its gradient**; fetal glucose is always lower, so a starving mother simply transports less. |
| "Amino acids just diffuse down their gradient too" | They are transported **against** it by Na⁺-coupled active transport, which is why fetal concentrations exceed maternal ones. |
| "Pre-eclampsia is a kidney disease because of the proteinuria" | It is a **placental disease with maternal endothelial consequences**; the kidney is a victim, not the source. |
| "The placenta makes progesterone from the start" | The **corpus luteum** does until the **luteo-placental shift** at weeks 8–12; before that, losing the luteum loses the pregnancy. |
| "Rh incompatibility threatens the first Rh-positive baby" | The first baby is safe — sensitisation happens at delivery, so the **second and later** pregnancies are at risk, which is why anti-D is given after the first. |
| "The foetus has no immune system, so rejection is not an issue" | The foetus is a **semi-allogeneic graft** with paternal MHC; tolerance is actively manufactured — no HLA-A/B, HLA-G, Tregs, IDO, PD-L1 — not passively absent. |
| "A pregnant woman is simply anaemic" | Total red-cell mass **rises**; plasma rises more. The lower haemoglobin is **dilutional**, an adaptation for flow, not a deficiency. |

## Key facts

- The placenta is a **foetal organ** (trophoblast + extra-embryonic mesoderm); the exchange portion is the **chorion frondosum** against the maternal decidua basalis.
- Fetal capillaries inside **villi** are bathed by maternal blood in the **intervillous space** — a **villous haemochorial** arrangement in which the two blood supplies **never mix**.
- **Spiral artery remodelling** by endovascular trophoblast converts high-resistance vessels into flaccid tubes (~500–600 mL/min of low-pressure flow); **failure causes pre-eclampsia and growth restriction**.
- The barrier thins from **~25 µm to ~3.5 µm** by term while surface area reaches **~10–12 m²** — Fick's law applied to anatomy.
- The cord has **two arteries and one vein**, named by direction relative to the heart — which is why the oxygenated vessel is a vein.
- **O₂ and CO₂** cross by free diffusion; **HbF's left-shifted curve** (weak 2,3-BPG binding) permits loading, and the **double Bohr effect** couples O₂ uptake to CO₂ removal.
- **Glucose** crosses by **facilitated diffusion** (foetal level lower); **amino acids** by **active transport** (foetal level higher); fatty acids by diffusion; waste (urea, creatinine, bilirubin) passively.
- **IgG alone crosses**, by **FcRn transcytosis** after ~week 28, giving passive immunity that decays over months; **IgM and IgA do not**, so cord-blood IgM means intrauterine infection.
- **Lipid-soluble, unbound, small molecules cross freely** — thalidomide, alcohol, tetracycline, antiretrovirals; most proteins, protein-bound drugs and maternal cells do not.
- Tolerance of the **50% paternal graft**: **no HLA-A/B or MHC II on trophoblast**, **HLA-G** and HLA-E engaging inhibitory receptors, angiogenic **decidual NK cells**, **FoxP3⁺ Tregs**, **IDO** tryptophan depletion, **PD-L1/PD-1**, plus controlled inflammation at implantation.
- **hCG** rescues the corpus luteum then falls after the **luteo-placental shift** (weeks 8–12); **hPL** drives maternal insulin resistance to spare glucose; **progesterone** keeps the myometrium quiescent; **oestriol** needs the **feto-placental unit**.
- **Ductus venosus, foramen ovale and ductus arteriosus are all right-to-left shunts** bypassing liver and lungs; all close when the cord is clamped and the lungs inflate.
- Maternal changes: **blood volume ↑30–50%, cardiac output ↑, systemic vascular resistance ↓, dilutional anaemia, insulin resistance, hypercoagulability** — pregnancy is deliberately a high-flow, glucose-sharing, thrombotic state.
- The placenta **ages and cannot be repaired**: after ~36 weeks surface area, perfusion and transfer all decline, which is why term is defined and post-term pregnancy is monitored.

## Practice questions

**1. A woman at 30 weeks is told the umbilical cord has two arteries and one vein. The vein carries oxygenated blood because**

A. it is an artery by embryological origin
B. vessels are named by direction relative to the fetal heart, and this vessel returns blood from the placenta toward the heart
C. the fetal liver oxygenates the blood before it reaches the heart
D. veins carry oxygenated blood throughout pregnancy

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Nomenclature follows direction, not oxygen content — the umbilical vein runs placenta → foetus (toward the heart), while the two arteries run heart → placenta. The pulmonary circulation shows the same inversion. The liver receives but does not oxygenate this blood, and veins carry deoxygenated blood everywhere else.

**2. Which statement about placental exchange is correct?**

A. Maternal and fetal blood mix in the intervillous space to improve diffusion
B. Substances cross by Fick's law, so a thinner membrane and larger surface area increase flux
C. Glucose crosses by active transport against its gradient
D. Fetal blood oxygen tension fully equilibrates with maternal arterial blood

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Flux varies with area × gradient ÷ thickness, which is why the membrane thins to ~3.5 µm and the villous surface expands to ~10–12 m². The bloods never mix (A); glucose uses facilitated diffusion (C); and because maternal blood pools rather than running true counter-current, umbilical vein pO₂ reaches only ~30–35 mmHg (D).

**3. Fetal haemoglobin loads oxygen at the placenta more effectively than adult haemoglobin mainly because it**

A. binds 2,3-BPG more avidly, right-shifting its curve
B. binds 2,3-BPG less avidly, left-shifting its curve and raising oxygen affinity
C. contains iron in the Fe³⁺ state
D. circulates outside red cells

**Answer: B**

Explanation: The γ chains of HbF bind 2,3-BPG weakly, so the deoxygenated state is less stabilised and the curve shifts left — higher affinity permits loading across the small placental gradient. Delivery still occurs because CO₂ and H⁺ at the foetal tissues produce the Bohr right-shift. A describes HbA.

**4. Cord blood tests positive for IgM against an infectious agent. The best interpretation is**

A. maternal IgM crossed in late gestation
B. the placenta actively transports IgM through FcRn
C. the fetus mounted its own response — intrauterine infection
D. IgM was absorbed from swallowed amniotic fluid

**Answer: C**

Explanation: IgM is a pentamer and is not transported across the placenta, so any IgM present must have been synthesised by the foetus — which is exactly why it diagnoses intrauterine infection. Maternal transfer is exclusively IgG.

**5. Transplacental transfer of immunoglobulin is best described as**

A. filtration of all classes in proportion to their plasma levels
B. FcRn-mediated transcytosis of IgG, accelerating after week 28, giving passive immunity that decays over months
C. active transport of IgA to provide mucosal immunity
D. irrelevant, because the fetus makes all its own antibodies from week 12

**Answer: B**

Explanation: FcRn binds IgG Fc in the endosome and releases it on the fetal side — active, saturable and selective for IgG, greatest in the third trimester. IgM, IgA, IgD and IgE do not cross, and transferred IgG has a half-life of about three weeks, so protection is temporary and non-specific.

**6. The trophoblast avoids maternal cytotoxic T-cell attack principally because it**

A. expresses high levels of classical HLA-A and HLA-B
B. destroys all maternal T cells in the decidua
C. lacks classical HLA-A/B and expresses the non-polymorphic HLA-G, which engages inhibitory receptors
D. hides inside macrophages so no antigen is presented

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Villous trophoblast has no classical HLA-A or HLA-B, so CD8⁺ T cells have no target, while invasive trophoblast expresses **HLA-G**, which binds inhibitory receptors such as ILT2/ILT4 and KIR2DL4 to switch off decidual NK cells and macrophages — alongside Tregs, IDO and PD-L1. It is active, local tolerance rather than systemic immunosuppression (B), and D describes a pathogen strategy, not physiology.

**7. The luteo-placental shift is best described as**

A. the corpus luteum taking over hCG production at week 12
B. hCG moving from the placenta to the corpus luteum
C. progesterone production transferring from the corpus luteum to the placenta around weeks 8–12, after hCG has maintained the luteum
D. the placenta taking over oestrogen production from the syncytiotrophoblast

**Answer: C**

Explanation: hCG from the syncytiotrophoblast rescues the corpus luteum, which supplies progesterone until placental steroidogenesis matures — then progesterone production transfers to the placenta and hCG falls to a plateau. hCG is never made by the corpus luteum, and its source does not change.

**8. An Rh-negative mother delivers her first baby, an Rh-positive male. Anti-D immunoglobulin is given within 72 hours in order to**

A. treat haemolysis already occurring in the newborn
B. replace the IgG the baby failed to receive
C. clear fetal Rh-positive red cells before the mother mounts her own IgG response, preventing sensitisation in later pregnancies
D. convert the mother's blood group to Rh-positive

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Fetal red cells enter mainly at delivery and prime the mother; her eventual anti-D IgG would cross the placenta and haemolyse foetal erythrocytes in a *subsequent* Rh-positive pregnancy. Exogenous anti-D clears those cells before sensitisation is established — it prevents, and cannot reverse, an existing response.

**9. Pre-eclampsia is best described as**

A. primary renal disease causing secondary hypertension
B. failed spiral artery remodelling leading to placental ischaemia, release of anti-angiogenic factors, and maternal endothelial dysfunction
C. decidual infection spreading to the kidneys
D. excessive maternal plasma volume overwhelming the heart

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Incomplete trophoblast invasion leaves narrow, high-resistance arteries → intermittent perfusion → ischaemia and reperfusion → sFlt-1 and soluble endoglin release → systemic endothelial injury → hypertension and proteinuria. The kidney is a victim of endothelial dysfunction rather than the origin, and the condition is neither infectious nor a volume overload.

**10. An NSAID prescribed to a woman at 24 weeks is a concern mainly because it may**

A. cross into breast milk but not the placenta
B. constrict the fetal ductus arteriosus and reduce renal perfusion, causing oligohydramnios
C. block IgG transfer across the placenta
D. prevent spiral artery remodelling and cause pre-eclampsia

**Answer: B**

Explanation: NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandin synthesis, and prostaglandins keep the fetal ductus arteriosus patent — so exposure after mid-gestation can constrict it prematurely while reducing fetal renal blood flow and amniotic fluid volume. The same property is used therapeutically to close a patent ductus in preterm infants.
