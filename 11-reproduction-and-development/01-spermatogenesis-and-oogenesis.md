# Spermatogenesis and Oogenesis

## Why it matters

Meiosis, as described in [12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md), is one replication, two divisions, four haploid cells. What that chapter did not say is *where in the body it happens*. The answer is narrow — **meiosis runs to completion in exactly two cell types, the developing sperm and the developing egg, and nowhere else**; no lymphocyte and no bone-marrow stem cell ever passes through prophase I. Gametogenesis is therefore the only place the machinery is switched on, and its two versions differ enough to explain real clinical facts:

- **Infertility workups are asymmetric.** A semen analysis takes twenty minutes and can be repeated weekly; an oocyte cannot be sampled at all, so female fertility is assessed indirectly — ovulation tracking, hormones, follicle counts. Spermatogenesis is **continuous and external**, oogenesis **intermittent and inaccessible**.
- **Maternal age raises aneuploidy risk; paternal age largely does not.** The egg ovulated at 40 began meiosis before birth and sat in prophase I for four decades; sperm were made within ~70 days. Trisomy 21 epidemiology is a readout of **how long a cell has been in meiotic arrest**.
- **Fertilisation must finish a job the egg started.** The oocyte arrests twice — prophase I, then metaphase II — and only the sperm releases the second arrest.

```
GAMETE UNIQUENESS
   somatic cell ──mitosis──▶ identical daughter ──▶ stop
   germ cell    ──MEIOSIS──▶ n gamete  ← ONLY HERE does reduction occur
                    │
     ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
SPERMATOGENESIS              OOGENESIS
continuous, amplifying       finite, one at a time
4 products, equal split      1 product, unequal split
meiosis runs straight through meiosis pauses twice
heat-sensitive, external     decades of arrest, internal
```

Everything below unpacks those four contrasts. Chromosomes, synaptonemal complex, crossing over, and independent assortment are unchanged between the sexes — **the difference lies in the cell biology wrapped around them**: when meiosis starts, whether it pauses, how cytoplasm is divided, and what the product must carry.

## Meiosis reframed: animals make gametes directly

One comparison first, because it is the commonest confusion when plant and animal reproduction are studied together.

| | **Animals** | **Plants** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product of meiosis | **Gametes directly** (sperm, egg) | **Spores** (never gametes) |
| Haploid phase | Single cells only | A multicellular **gametophyte** |
| How gametes are made | By meiosis itself | By **mitosis** in the gametophyte |
| Reduction happens at | Gamete formation | **Spore** formation |

```
ANIMAL   2n germ cell ──MEIOSIS──▶ n GAMETE ──fertilisation──▶ 2n zygote
PLANT    2n sporophyte ──MEIOSIS──▶ n SPORE ──mitosis──▶ n gametophyte
                                                             │ mitosis
                                                             ▼
                                                        n GAMETES
```

The swap is in [06 — Plant reproduction and alternation of generations](../08-plant-biology/06-plant-reproduction-and-alternation-of-generations.md). On the animal side: **there is no intervening haploid individual in us** — a haploid human cell is a gamete by definition, since there is no haploid growth or mitosis. The output must be right first time, which is why both programmes invest heavily in checkpoints.

The sources of variation from [12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md) carry over unchanged: **crossing over** at pachytene creates recombinant chromatids, **independent assortment** at metaphase I gives 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million alignments, and **random fertilisation** multiplies the pools. Only the *frequency and reliability* differ between the sexes.

## Spermatogenesis

### The site: the seminiferous tubules

Spermatogenesis takes place in the **seminiferous tubules** — roughly 250 m of coiled tube per testis — kept at **2–3 °C below core body temperature**, the organising constraint of the whole system (see Medical relevance below). Two anatomical features matter:

- **The interstitium** between tubules holds the **Leydig cells** (testosterone source) and the blood supply, so Leydig hormone must diffuse *inwards*.
- **The tubule wall** is built from **Sertoli cells** spanning basement membrane to lumen, joined by tight junctions that create a **basal compartment** (spermatogonia, exposed to blood) and an **adluminal compartment** (meiotic cells).

```
SEMINIFEROUS TUBULE — cross-section
  lumen  ●  ●  ●        free spermatids released (spermiation)
  ──────────────────     SERTOLI TIGHT JUNCTIONS = BLOOD–TESTIS BARRIER
  adluminal              meiotic cells — hidden from lymphocytes because
                         they express "new" antigens after crossing over
  ──────────────────     basement membrane
  basal                  SPERMATOGONIA (stem cells, in contact with blood)
  ──────────────────
  INTERSTITIUM:  Leydig cells, macrophages, capillaries
```

The **blood–testis barrier** is therefore a *defence* structure: meiotic cells display novel surface proteins after crossing over, so Sertoli tight junctions hide them from lymphocytes. Break the barrier and the result is **anti-sperm antibody infertility**.

### The sequence: mitotic amplification, then meiosis

```
SPERMATOGONIUM (2n)  — stem cell, basal compartment
      │  MITOTIC DIVISIONS  ← amplification before any of them commit
      ▼
PRIMARY SPERMATOCYTE (2n)  — replicates DNA, enters prophase I
      │  MEIOSIS I  (reductional — homologues separate)
      ▼
SECONDARY SPERMATOCYTE (n, 2 chromatids)  — short-lived
      │  MEIOSIS II  (equational — sisters separate)
      ▼
SPERMATID (n)  — round, non-motile, genomically complete
      │  SPERMIOGENESIS  ← DIFFERENTIATION, NOT DIVISION
      ▼
SPERMATOZOON (n)  — acrosome, flagellum, condensed nucleus
```

Three features of this chain are deliberate:

1. **Mitotic amplification comes first.** One stem cell becomes hundreds of cells entering meiosis in parallel rather than one meiosis yielding four sperm. Type A dark spermatogonia self-renew while pale and type B types differentiate, so the **stem cell pool is never exhausted** — production continues from puberty to death.
2. **Cytokinesis is equal at every step.** No cytoplasm is hoarded, because the job is to swim and deliver a nucleus, not to feed an embryo — four equal products are four useful products.
3. **Meiosis itself is textbook meiosis.** Primary spermatocytes are the largest cells in the tubule and spend days in prophase I, their coarse chromatin obvious on biopsy. Everything else runs as in [12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md).

### Spermiogenesis: differentiation without division

The round **spermatid** is already haploid and genomically complete but cannot fertilise anything. **Spermiogenesis** converts it into a spermatozoon — **no further division occurs**, so the genome is never touched again. It is a packaging problem:

| Problem | Solution | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head < 5 µm and transcriptionally silent | Histones replaced by **protamines**; DNA near-crystalline | Condensed nucleus |
| Egg coverings must be pierced | Golgi-derived vesicle capping the nucleus, packed with **hyaluronidase** and **acrosin** | **Acrosome** |
| It must swim metres against flow | 9+2 axoneme built on the centriole | **Flagellum** |
| Beating needs ATP at the tail | Mitochondria spiral around the proximal tail | **Midpiece** |
| Excess cytoplasm unbalances it | Shed and recycled | **Residual bodies** |

```
nucleus ──histones → protamines──▶ dense head      Golgi ──▶ ACROSOME
centriole ──elongates──▶ FLAGELLUM    mito ──spiral──▶ MIDPIECE
bulk cytoplasm ──pinched off──▶ RESIDUAL BODY → phagocytosed by Sertoli
```

The mature spermatid is released into the lumen (**spermiation**); full motility is acquired later, in the epididymis.

### Support cells: Sertoli and Leydig

Spermatogenesis is a two-cell system in hormonal terms, and the distinction below is high-yield.

| | **Sertoli cells** | **Leydig cells** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Location** | Inside the tubule, wall to lumen | Interstitium, outside the tubule |
| **Stimulated by** | **FSH** | **LH** |
| **Secrete** | Androgen-binding protein (ABP), **inhibin B**, fluid | **Testosterone** |
| **Roles** | Nourish germ cells; form the **blood–testis barrier**; **phagocytose residual bodies**; carry sperm to the rete testis | Maintain **intratesticular testosterone** at ~50–100× serum level, needed for meiosis; virilise |
| **Feedback** | **Inhibin B → selectively suppresses FSH** | **Testosterone → suppresses GnRH and LH** |
| **If damaged** | Oligospermia, normal testosterone | Low libido, low testosterone, impaired meiosis |

```
HYPOTHALAMUS ──GnRH (pulsatile)──▶ PITUITARY ANTERIOR
                                       │           │
                                      LH          FSH
                                       ▼           ▼
                                  LEYDIG      SERTOLI
                                       │           │
                                  TESTOSTERONE  inhibin B
                        NEGATIVE FEEDBACK: testosterone → GnRH + LH ↓
                                           inhibin B    → FSH ↓
```

**Testosterone and inhibin are separate signals on separate axes**, so sperm production (FSH/Sertoli) can be tuned independently of virilisation (LH/testosterone). The trap: **exogenous testosterone** switches off GnRH and therefore LH *and* FSH, collapses intratesticular testosterone, and causes **azoospermia** — a man can be fully virilised and infertile at once. See [04 — The endocrine system](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/04-endocrine-system.md) and [10 — The reproductive system](../10-anatomy-and-physiology/10-reproductive-system.md).

### Timing and output

| Parameter | Value | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One full cycle** (spermatogonium → released sperm) | **~64–74 days** (~76 days with epididymal transit) | Today's semen analysis reflects conditions ~3 months ago |
| **Start** | Puberty, under FSH/LH | Testicular growth, rising semen volume |
| **Continuity** | **Continuous from puberty to old age** | No analogue of menopause — stem cells self-renew |
| **Output** | Hundreds of millions per day; **~200–300 million per ejaculate** | Fertility depends on numbers as well as quality |
| **Sensitivity** | Fever, toxins, heat cut output **~2–3 months later** | The lag *is* the cycle length |

Hence the clinical pearl: ask about a febrile illness or chemotherapy *three months ago*, not this week, when a semen analysis is poor. It also explains why pre-pubertal chemotherapy is less damaging — surviving stem cells restart the programme.

## Oogenesis

### All the oocytes you will ever have

The female programme begins with the opposite decision: **no mitotic amplification**.

```
EMBRYONIC ovary (weeks 2–6): germ cells migrate → OOGONIA (2n)
        │  MITOSIS — only before birth
        ▼
   ~6–7 million oogonia (≈20 weeks)  → all enter MEIOSIS I, then STOP
        ▼
   ~1–2 million PRIMARY OOCYTES at birth  ← frozen here
        │  atresia continues
        ▼
   ~300,000–400,000 at puberty → ~400–500 ovulations in a lifetime
        ▼
   MENOPAUSE — pool exhausted
```

Every oogonium you will ever possess is made during foetal life; germline mitosis **ceases before birth**. That is the structural reason for **menopause**, and why "ovarian reserve" has no male equivalent — male production is renewable.

### Arrest at dictyotene: prophase I for decades

The primary oocytes stop **inside prophase I**, at **diplotene**: the synaptonemal complex is dismantled but homologues stay loosely attached as extended **lampbrush chromosomes**. This prolonged arrest is **dictyotene** — a holding pattern letting the cell grow enormously while waiting up to forty years for a hormonal signal. During it the oocyte accumulates ribosomes, mitochondria, and **stored maternal mRNA** (see [03 — Early development and cleavage](03-early-development-and-cleavage.md)), stockpiles the **spindle** needed at ovulation, and maintains the **cohesin** holding homologues and sisters together.

That last job ages badly: cohesin is loaded once, prenatally, and is not efficiently replaced, so the glue degrades over the decades — the bridge to the maternal-age effect.

### One cohort per cycle: recruitment, dominance, ovulation

From puberty the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis runs a monthly cycle: FSH recruits a small cohort of follicles, **one becomes dominant**, the rest undergo **atresia**.

| Event | What happens to the oocyte | Hormonal driver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Follicular phase** | Primary oocyte still in dictyotene; granulosa cells secrete **oestrogen** | **FSH** recruits the cohort |
| **Dominance** | One follicle gains FSH receptors fastest; the others undergo **atresia** | Falling FSH + local factors |
| **LH surge** | Triggers **resumption of meiosis I** | High oestrogen flips feedback to positive |
| **Meiosis I completes** | **Primary oocyte → secondary oocyte + first polar body**, ~34–36 h after the surge | Ovulation itself |
| **Ovulation** | The **metaphase II secondary oocyte** is expelled with its zona pellucida and taken up by the fimbriae | LH surge |
| **Fertilisation or ~24 h later** | Meiosis II completes — or the oocyte degenerates | Sperm entry |

Learn the assignment exactly: **meiosis I completes at ovulation, not at fertilisation; meiosis II completes only at fertilisation.**

### Asymmetric cytokinesis: one cell keeps everything

Cytokinesis is unequal in both divisions — engineering, not accident.

```
PRIMARY OOCYTE (2n, huge)
      │  MEIOSIS I — UNEQUAL CYTOKINESIS
      ▼
SECONDARY OOCYTE (~99% of cytoplasm) + FIRST POLAR BODY (a sliver)
      │  MEIOSIS II — again unequal, and only if fertilised
      ▼
OOTID (ovum) + SECOND POLAR BODY   (first polar body may divide too)

   final tally:  1 functional egg  +  3 polar bodies that degenerate
```

Why the asymmetry is required:

1. **The egg must bankroll the first cleavages.** Until the embryonic genome activates (~8-cell stage), every protein, ribosome, and mitochondrion comes from the oocyte's stockpile — see [03 — Early development and cleavage](03-early-development-and-cleavage.md).
2. **Polar bodies are a disposal route, not a loss.** They carry surplus chromosomes out while giving up almost no cytoplasm — meiosis's chromosomal result on one cell's cytoplasmic budget.
3. **The alternative is four inviable half-eggs.** Spermatogenesis can afford four equal products because sperm carry no cytoplasmic burden.

Polar bodies also act as a crude **quality-control dump**, letting a mis-segregating oocyte expel the error — an imperfect filter, as persistent trisomy shows.

### Why meiosis II waits for the sperm

The secondary oocyte sits at **metaphase II**, spindle assembled and chromosomes aligned, held by a **cytostatic factor** that keeps the anaphase-promoting complex inactive — a checkpoint in the sense of [10 — The cell cycle and its checkpoints](../03-cellular-processes/10-cell-cycle-and-checkpoints.md) that **requires an external trigger**, not a timer:

```
SECONDARY OOCYTE at metaphase II ──ARRESTED──▶ waits ~24 h
        │  SPERM FUSES → Ca²⁺ OSCILLATIONS in the ooplasm
        ▼  cytostatic factor destroyed → APC/C active
        ▼  MEIOSIS II completes → ootid + second polar body
        ▼  cortical reaction, block to polyspermy, egg activation

   no sperm within ~24 h → meiosis never finishes → degeneration
```

Two consequences follow. **Fertilisation is not merely nuclear fusion — it is the signal that finishes the female meiotic programme.** The sperm gives only a nucleus and a centriole while the oocyte supplies all else, shaping the early embryo. The next step is in [02 — Fertilisation and the zygote](02-fertilization-and-the-zygote.md).

### The maternal age effect: forty years of wear on one division

The risk of a trisomic conception rises steeply and nonlinearly with maternal age:

| Maternal age | 20 | 30 | 35 | 40 | 45 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Approximate risk of trisomy 21 | ~1 in 1,500 | ~1 in 900 | ~1 in 350 | ~1 in 100 | ~1 in 30 |

This is not general metabolic "wear and tear" — it follows from the arrest:

```
COHESIN LOADED ONCE, IN FETAL LIFE
        ▼
   ~40 YEARS OF DICTYOTENE ARREST
        ├─ cohesin along chromosome arms degrades
        ├─ recombination set decades earlier, some too distal
        ├─ spindle assembly checkpoint weakens
        └─ mitochondria and translation machinery age
        ▼
   HOMOLOGUES / SISTERS NO LONGER HELD RELIABLY
        ▼
   NONDISJUNCTION at meiosis I (most common) or meiosis II
        ▼
   n+1 egg → trisomy 21, 18, 13  (or n−1 → monosomy, usually inviable)
```

The contrast is decisive: **sperm are made continuously and finish meiosis within ~74 days**, so paternal age contributes far less — a man's meiosis is never more than a few months old. That asymmetry explains why counselling centres on maternal age, as noted in [12 — Meiosis](../03-cellular-processes/12-meiosis.md).

## Master comparison

| Feature | **Spermatogenesis** | **Oogenesis** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Where** | Seminiferous tubules (external, 2–3 °C below core) | Ovary (internal) |
| **Initiation** | At **puberty**, under FSH/LH | **Before birth** — all oogonia enter meiosis as fetuses |
| **Mitotic amplification** | Yes — spermatogonia self-renew continuously | **None after birth** — fixed, finite pool |
| **Continuity** | **Continuous** to old age | **Cyclical**, ~28-day cohort, then menopause |
| **Arrest point(s)** | None; meiosis runs straight through | **Two**: prophase I (dictyotene) foetal life → ovulation; **metaphase II** ovulation → fertilisation |
| **Meiosis I completes** | Within the cycle, ~24 days after entry | **At ovulation** |
| **Meiosis II completes** | Immediately after meiosis I | **Only if fertilised** |
| **Cytokinesis** | **Equal** | **Unequal** — nearly all cytoplasm to the egg |
| **Products per meiosis** | **4 functional spermatozoa** | **1 ovum + 3 degenerating polar bodies** |
| **Final product** | Small, motile, transcriptionally silent | Large, non-motile, nutrient- and mRNA-rich |
| **Differentiation** | **Spermiogenesis** (acrosome, flagellum, midpiece, condensation) | None — growth is cytoplasmic accumulation |
| **Supporting cells** | **Sertoli** (barrier, nourishment, phagocytosis, inhibin) and **Leydig** (testosterone) | **Granulosa/cumulus cells** and **corpus luteum** |
| **Hormonal control** | FSH → Sertoli; LH → Leydig → testosterone; inhibin B → FSH | FSH/LH cycle; **LH surge triggers meiosis I** |
| **Lifetime numbers** | Effectively unlimited | ~400–500 ovulations from ~400,000 at puberty |
| **Clinical correlation** | Cryptorchidism, varicocele, semen analysis, exogenous testosterone | Maternal-age aneuploidy, menopause, PCOS, OHSS |

## Gamete structure: form follows function

Both are meiotic products, engineered for opposite tasks.

| | **Spermatozoon** | **Oocyte** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Size** | ~60 µm long, head ~5 µm | ~120 µm across — the largest cell in the body |
| **Motility** | **Flagellum** (9+2 axoneme), progressive beating | None — moved by fimbriae and tubal peristalsis |
| **Energy** | **Midpiece mitochondria** spiralled around the axoneme | Glycogen and lipid stores for the embryo |
| **Penetration** | **Acrosome**: hyaluronidase (cumulus), acrosin (zona pellucida) | Zona pellucida and cumulus as barriers; **cortical granules** block polyspermy |
| **Genome** | Histones largely replaced by **protamines** — silent | Held at metaphase II; **maternal mRNA stored** for the first cycles |
| **Cytoplasm** | Almost none — shed as residual bodies | Enormous — ribosomes, organelles, proteins to drive cleavage |
| **Strategy** | Quantity: ~200–300 million per ejaculate, a few hundred reaching the tube | Quality: **one per cycle**, won by dominance |
| **Job** | Deliver a nucleus across a hostile tract | Bankroll the embryo until its genome activates |

The trade is exact: the sperm **sacrifices cytoplasm for motility**, the oocyte **sacrifices motility for cytoplasm**.

## Medical relevance

**Semen analysis** is the front line of male infertility assessment (WHO lower reference limits):

| Parameter | Lower limit | Parameter | Lower limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Volume | ≥ 1.5 mL | Total motility | ≥ **40%** (progressive ≥ 32%) |
| Concentration | ≥ **15 million/mL** | Vitality | ≥ 58% live |
| Total sperm number | ≥ **39 million** | Normal morphology | ≥ **4%** (strict criteria) |

Because a cycle takes **~64–74 days**, one abnormal result is never interpreted alone: two samples weeks apart, with a febrile illness sought **~3 months back**. Leucocytes or agglutination suggest infection; volume <1 mL with low pH suggests duct obstruction.

**Cryptorchidism — why the testes hang outside.** Spermatogenesis cannot run at 37 °C, so the pampiniform plexus and cremasteric–dartos reflex hold the testes 2–3 °C below core. An intra-abdominal testis is too warm: **meiosis fails, germ cells die by apoptosis, the epithelium degenerates**. Bilateral cases are usually infertile, and retained germ cells raise the risk of **seminoma** — hence orchiopexy in the first year. **Heat damages meiosis**.

**Varicocele** — dilation of the pampiniform plexus, usually left because the left testicular vein joins the renal vein at a right angle. Reflux impairs heat dissipation and adds oxidative stress → lower count and motility. It is found in ~40% of infertile men; varicocelectomy helps a substantial minority, targeting the *meiotic* environment rather than hormones.

**Ovarian reserve and maternal-age aneuploidy.** With the pool fixed before birth, reserve is measured indirectly: **anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH)**, antral follicle count, day-3 FSH. It falls with age even when cycles stay regular — hence fertility declines before menopause. The aneuploidy curve is a *separate* mechanism (cohesin ageing in dictyotene); together they explain the fall after 35, and with it counselling, folic acid, and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT-A).

**Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS)** is iatrogenic over-recruitment: gonadotrophins abolish FSH-mediated dominance, so **many follicles develop instead of one**. Very high oestrogen, then hCG from an implanting embryo, upregulates **VEGF** and vascular permeability → third-spacing: ascites, pleural effusion, haemoconcentration, oliguria, and in severe cases thrombosis and renal failure. Management is supportive — and OHSS shows that **one follicle per cycle is a safeguard**.

**Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)** is anovulation, not a meiotic defect. Excess androgens plus insulin resistance mean many antral follicles start but **none achieves dominance**, so the oestrogen rise and positive-feedback LH surge never occur and **ovulation — and resumption of meiosis I — never happens**. Oocytes stay in dictyotene while follicles accumulate as the "polycystic" appearance. Rotterdam criteria: oligo/anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic morphology, other causes excluded. The machinery is normal — **the failure is the signal** — so letrozole or clomiphene restores ovulation.

**Arrest points and fertilisation.** Because the oocyte parks at metaphase II, fertilisation is not simple fusion: sperm entry triggers the Ca²⁺ oscillations that destroy the cytostatic factor and **complete meiosis II**. Immature oocytes or failed activation yield no viable embryo, and one that never got the LH surge never reached metaphase II. This is why IVF laboratories grade oocyte *maturity*, and why [02 — Fertilisation and the zygote](02-fertilization-and-the-zygote.md) begins exactly where oogenesis stops.

## Common confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| "Meiosis makes gametes in plants too" | In plants meiosis makes **spores**; gametes are made mitotically. Only in **animals** does meiosis produce gametes. |
| "The egg completes meiosis at fertilisation" | Only **meiosis II** completes then; **meiosis I completes at ovulation**, giving the secondary oocyte and first polar body. |
| "Oogenesis produces four eggs" | It produces **one ovum and three degenerating polar bodies**; spermatogenesis produces four *functional* products. |
| "Polar bodies are useless cells" | They are a **chromosome-disposal and quality-control route**: surplus chromosomes out, almost no cytoplasm lost. |
| "Oogonia keep dividing after birth" | All oogonia form **before birth**; germline mitosis ceases — hence the finite pool and menopause. |
| "Male and female infertility are assessed the same" | Male assessment is direct and repeatable (semen analysis); female assessment is **indirect** (ovulation, hormones, AMH, follicle count). |
| "Maternal age effects are general ageing" | They are **~40 years of prophase I arrest** degrading cohesin and spindle function; sperm finish meiosis in ~74 days, so paternal age matters far less. |
| "Testosterone therapy boosts fertility" | Exogenous testosterone **suppresses GnRH → LH and FSH**, collapsing intratesticular testosterone. It is virilising and *contraceptive*. |
| "Sertoli and Leydig cells do the same job" | **Leydig = testosterone (LH)**; **Sertoli = nourishment, barrier, phagocytosis, inhibin B (FSH)**. |
| "The blood–testis barrier is only about toxins" | Its main purpose is **immunological**: meiotic cells display novel antigens and must be hidden from lymphocytes. |
| "A round spermatid is a spermatozoon" | A spermatid is haploid but useless until **spermiogenesis** — a step with **no division** — remodels it. |

## Key facts

- Meiosis completes **only in gametes**; in animals it produces gametes **directly**, in plants spores.
- **Spermatogenesis**: spermatogonium (2n, mitotic amplification) → primary spermatocyte (2n) → meiosis I → secondary spermatocyte (n) → meiosis II → spermatid (n) → **spermiogenesis** → spermatozoon.
- **Equal cytokinesis → 4 functional sperm**; **unequal cytokinesis → 1 ovum + 3 degenerating polar bodies**, which are a chromosome-dumping and quality-control device.
- **Spermiogenesis is differentiation without division**: acrosome (hydrolytic enzymes), flagellum (9+2), mitochondrial midpiece, histone→protamine condensation; surplus cytoplasm becomes **residual bodies**, phagocytosed by Sertoli.
- **Sertoli** (FSH): nourishment, **blood–testis barrier**, residual-body phagocytosis, inhibin B. **Leydig** (LH): testosterone. Axis: **GnRH → LH/FSH → testosterone + inhibin → negative feedback**.
- One spermatogenic cycle is **~64–74 days**, **continuous from puberty to old age**; **~200–300 million sperm per ejaculate**, so illness or drug effects appear **~3 months later**.
- All oogonia form **before birth** and arrest in **prophase I (dictyotene)**; from puberty one follicle per cycle resumes.
- **Meiosis I completes at ovulation**; the oocyte arrests at **metaphase II**; **meiosis II completes only if fertilised** → ootid + second polar body. The egg keeps all cytoplasm because it funds cleavage until genome activation.
- The finite pool explains **menopause**; decades of arrest explain **rising aneuploidy with maternal age** (cohesin degradation → nondisjunction → trisomy 21).
- Sperm = **acrosome + midpiece mitochondria + flagellum**; oocyte = **nutrient cytoplasm, stored mRNA, organelles**.
- Clinical anchors: semen analysis, **cryptorchidism and varicocele (heat damages meiosis)**, ovarian reserve, **OHSS**, **PCOS as anovulation**, exogenous testosterone.

## Practice questions

**1. The fundamental difference between spermatogenesis and oogenesis is that they differ in**

A. The mechanics of meiosis I and meiosis II
B. When meiosis starts, whether it arrests, and how cytoplasm is divided
C. Chromosome sets in the starting cell
D. The requirement for crossing over

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Both start from a 2n germ cell and run identical meiosis. What differs is initiation (puberty vs foetal life), arrest (none vs two) and cytokinesis (equal vs unequal).

**2. A round spermatid acquires an acrosome, a flagellum, a mitochondrial midpiece and a condensed nucleus. This process is best described as**

A. Meiosis III
B. Mitotic amplification of spermatogonia
C. Differentiation without further cell division
D. Fertilisation-dependent activation

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Spermiogenesis remodels an already haploid spermatid — no chromosome segregation occurs, so the genome is untouched. It follows meiosis rather than constituting a division; amplification happens earlier, in spermatogonia.

**3. Which pair of statements about the supporting cells of the testis is correct?**

A. Sertoli form the barrier, phagocytose residual bodies and respond to FSH; Leydig secrete testosterone in response to LH
B. Sertoli secrete testosterone in response to LH; Leydig form the blood–testis barrier
C. Both cell types respond to FSH
D. Leydig cells phagocytose residual bodies

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Sertoli are the FSH target — tight junctions form the barrier, they nourish germ cells, engulf residual bodies and release inhibin B. Leydig sit in the interstitium, respond to LH, and make the testosterone meiosis needs.

**4. In human oogenesis, meiosis I is completed and meiosis II is completed respectively at**

A. Birth and puberty
B. Fertilisation and the first cleavage division
C. Puberty and ovulation
D. Ovulation and fertilisation

**Answer: D**

Explanation: The LH surge resumes meiosis I, which finishes just before ovulation, giving a secondary oocyte and first polar body. That cell arrests at metaphase II until sperm-induced Ca²⁺ oscillations release it.

**5. Unequal cytokinesis in oogenesis is advantageous despite three non-viable polar bodies because it**

A. Lets polar bodies later develop into ova
B. Concentrates cytoplasm, organelles and stored mRNA in the cell supporting cleavage before genome activation
C. Allows four times as many eggs per cycle
D. Prevents crossing over from occurring

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Preimplantation divisions run on maternal supplies, so a half-sized egg would fail. Polar bodies expel surplus chromosomes while losing almost no cytoplasm; they degenerate rather than become ova.

**6. The steep rise in trisomy 21 risk with maternal age is best explained by**

A. Older oocytes starting with more chromosomes
B. Reduced sperm quality in older women
C. Decades of prophase I arrest degrading cohesin, making homologue separation error-prone
D. Failure of embryonic genome activation

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Oocytes hold in dictyotene for ~40 years and cohesin loaded prenatally is not replaced efficiently, so segregation becomes unreliable. Chromosome number starts normal; genome-activation failure causes arrest, not trisomy.

**7. A man started on exogenous testosterone is azoospermic three months later. The mechanism is**

A. Direct toxicity of testosterone to spermatids
B. Destruction of the blood–testis barrier by antibodies
C. Accelerated spermiogenesis depleting stem cells
D. Negative feedback suppressing GnRH, LH and FSH, removing support meiosis requires

**Answer: D**

Explanation: Exogenous androgen switches off GnRH and hence LH and FSH, collapsing intratesticular testosterone and Sertoli support so meiosis stops while virilisation continues. The three-month delay is the ~64–74 day cycle.

**8. Which finding demonstrates that heat impairs spermatogenesis?**

A. Varicocele and undescended testis both reduce sperm output
B. Cryptorchidism increases sperm count
C. Febrile illness immediately improves semen parameters
D. A laptop on the lap raises scrotal temperature without affecting meiosis

**Answer: A**

Explanation: Meiosis needs a scrotum 2–3 °C below core; an intra-abdominal testis loses germ cells and a varicocele impairs heat dissipation. Fever shows its effect ~2–3 months later — the cycle length.

**9. A woman with PCOS has many small antral follicles but no ovulation. The defect is**

A. A mutation of the cohesin complex in her oocytes
B. Absence of an LH surge, so meiosis I is never resumed from dictyotene
C. Failure of meiosis II after fertilisation
D. Exhaustion of the oocyte reserve

**Answer: B**

Explanation: In PCOS no follicle achieves dominance, so the oestrogen rise and LH surge never occur and the primary oocyte stays in prophase I. The oocytes are normal — the failure is ovulatory, not meiotic.

**10. Compared with sperm, the human oocyte is unusually large because it**

A. Must perform mitosis after fertilisation unaided
B. Contains four chromosome sets
C. Stores nutrients, organelles and maternal mRNA to drive cleavage until genome activation
D. Retains a functional acrosome to penetrate the zona pellucida

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Preimplantation development runs on maternal contributions, so the oocyte stockpiles ribosomes, mitochondria and transcripts. It is haploid, performs no mitosis, and has no acrosome — that belongs to sperm.
